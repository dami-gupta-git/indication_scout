You are adjudicating disease-scoped drug-safety abstracts for {drug} and the candidate indication
"{disease}". Classify every PMID independently.

Before classifying, answer two questions about the abstract.

First, name WHO OR WHAT was studied — "patients" for humans given {drug}, "animals" for any in-vivo
animal work (mice, rats, offspring of treated dams, other non-human species), "cells_or_tissue" for
in-vitro, cell-line, organoid, xenograft or ex-vivo work, and "unclear" when the abstract does not
say. A xenograft or a study of human cell lines implanted in animals is "cells_or_tissue", not
"patients", however human the cells are. Report this as study_subjects for every PMID.

Second, name what {drug} was being GIVEN FOR in the abstract — the condition it was meant to treat
or prevent — and whether "{disease}" is explicitly reported to increase, trigger, or worsen the
harm caused by {drug}.

Use "confirmed_harm" only when the abstract reports a concrete adverse outcome, toxicity,
contraindication, or safety-related trial stop attributable to {drug} in human patients AND either:
1. {drug} was being given to treat or prevent {disease}; or
2. {disease} was explicitly identified as a cause of, susceptibility factor for, or amplifier of
   that harm from {drug}.
A paper that says safety or tolerability was assessed, that adverse events were collected, or that
harm was possible is not a confirmed harm unless it reports the harmful result. Comparator use,
background treatment, combination treatment without attribution to {drug}, a different disease,
and non-human work are not confirmed indication-specific harm.

The evidence_quote must SAY THE HARM HAPPENED. A sentence saying the harm did not occur, or that the
drug was tolerated, is not a confirmed harm — mark it "safety_assessed_only". Naming a harm is not
the same as reporting one.
  e.g. "he increased the dose to 2250 mg/d without seizures" — this says no seizure occurred.

The abstract must also ATTRIBUTE the event to {drug}. Attribution is author language tying the event
to the drug — drug-related, treatment-related, caused by, led to discontinuation, dose reduction or
trial stop, or an event rate reported for {drug} against a comparator. RECORDING that an event
occurred in patients who received {drug} is not attribution, and neither is counting it as a
pre-specified safety endpoint. When the abstract's own conclusion is that {drug} was safe, tolerated
or well tolerated, an event it reports is a counted event -> "safety_assessed_only", unless the
authors separately attribute that event to {drug}.
  e.g. "The primary outcome measure occurred in one patient (sudden death)" in a study concluding
  the drug appeared safe — the event is counted, not attributed => "safety_assessed_only".
This raises the bar for a harm flag, so apply it only to attribution. A harm the authors DO attribute
to {drug} stays "confirmed_harm" even when the abstract also reports the drug was generally well
tolerated — a tolerability statement does not cancel an attributed event.

"{disease}" merely naming WHICH PATIENTS were studied is not enough. When {drug} was given for a
different condition, mark the paper "irrelevant" unless the abstract explicitly says {disease}
increased, triggered, or worsened the attributable harm from {drug}.
  e.g. an accidental overdose in a child who has autism — {drug} was not being given for autism.
  e.g. {drug} prescribed for depression in a patient who also has low back pain — it was given for
  depression, not for the back pain.
  e.g. cocaine abuse sensitization caused psychosis to emerge at a lower dose of {drug} — this is a
  confirmed harm for cocaine use disorder because the disease explicitly increased susceptibility.

For each PMID choose exactly one status:
- "confirmed_harm": the abstract reports a concrete attributable harm in human patients and {drug}
  was being given for {disease}, OR {disease} explicitly increased, triggered, or worsened that
  harm. Never use this status when study_subjects is not "patients".
- "safety_assessed_only": safety was evaluated but no concrete attributable harm was reported.
- "irrelevant": {drug} was not being given for {disease} and {disease} was not an explicit risk
  modifier — including when {disease} only names the patients — or the drug or attribution does not
  match.
- "unclear": the abstract does not contain enough information to decide.

For "confirmed_harm", provide a short adverse_outcome and a short exact evidence_quote of no more
than 80 words copied contiguously from the title or abstract — copy the whole sentence rather than
shortening it, and never join text across a gap. For every other status, return null for both
fields. Return one verdict for every input PMID and no other PMIDs.
