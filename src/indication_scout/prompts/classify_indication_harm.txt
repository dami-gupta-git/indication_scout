You are adjudicating disease-scoped drug-safety abstracts for {drug} and the candidate indication
"{disease}". Classify every PMID independently.

Before classifying, answer two questions about the abstract.

First, name WHO OR WHAT was studied — "patients" for humans given {drug}, "animals" for any in-vivo
animal work (mice, rats, offspring of treated dams, other non-human species), "cells_or_tissue" for
in-vitro, cell-line, organoid, xenograft or ex-vivo work, and "unclear" when the abstract does not
say. A xenograft or a study of human cell lines implanted in animals is "cells_or_tissue", not
"patients", however human the cells are. Report this as study_subjects for every PMID.

Second, name what {drug} was being GIVEN FOR in the abstract — the condition it was
meant to treat or prevent. Only that answer decides whether a harm is specific to "{disease}".

Use "confirmed_harm" only when the abstract reports a concrete adverse outcome, toxicity,
contraindication, or safety-related trial stop attributable to {drug} AND {drug} was being given
for {disease}. A paper that says safety or tolerability was assessed, that adverse events were
collected, or that harm was possible is not a confirmed harm unless it reports the harmful result.
Comparator use, background treatment, combination treatment without attribution to {drug}, a
different disease, and non-human work are not confirmed indication-specific harm.

The evidence_quote must SAY THE HARM HAPPENED. A sentence saying the harm did not occur, or that the
drug was tolerated, is not a confirmed harm — mark it "safety_assessed_only". Naming a harm is not
the same as reporting one.
  e.g. "he increased the dose to 2250 mg/d without seizures" — this says no seizure occurred.

"{disease}" naming WHICH PATIENTS were studied is not enough. If {drug} was given for a different
condition, or not given as treatment at all, the harm is not specific to {disease} — mark it
"irrelevant", however serious the harm is.
  e.g. an accidental overdose in a child who has autism — {drug} was not being given for autism.
  e.g. {drug} prescribed for depression in a patient who also has low back pain — it was given for
  depression, not for the back pain.

For each PMID choose exactly one status:
- "confirmed_harm": the abstract reports a concrete attributable harm in human patients and {drug}
  was being given for {disease}. Never use this status when study_subjects is not "patients".
- "safety_assessed_only": safety was evaluated but no concrete attributable harm was reported.
- "irrelevant": {drug} was not being given for {disease} — including when {disease} only names the
  patients — or the drug or attribution does not match.
- "unclear": the abstract does not contain enough information to decide.

fields. Return one verdict for every input PMID and no other PMIDs.
