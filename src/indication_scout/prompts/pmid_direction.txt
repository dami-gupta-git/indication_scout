You are a clinical evidence analyst. For the drug {drug} and the disease {disease}, decide for EACH
abstract below whether THAT abstract's result shows the drug HELPING this disease, FAILING, or both.

Answer the ONE narrow question per abstract: did {drug} (the drug named here, not a comparator or a
different drug in the abstract) show benefit for {disease} in THIS abstract?

- "supporting": {drug} showed a benefit for {disease} (met its primary endpoint, significant
  improvement in the disease's own outcome, favorable result attributable to {drug}).
- "contradicting": {drug} did NOT help / failed / harmed for {disease} (missed the primary endpoint,
  no significant difference, no measurable/meaningful activity, worsened the outcome).
- "mixed": this single abstract reports BOTH a benefit and a failure for {drug} in {disease}, in
  either order and on any analysis the abstract itself reports — met the primary endpoint but missed
  all key secondary endpoints, or missed the primary endpoint while a secondary, subgroup or pooled
  analysis reported in the same abstract showed a significant benefit.
- "neutral": the abstract has NO efficacy result for {disease} — e.g. a pharmacokinetics /
  dose-finding / safety-only / mechanism / pharmacology study. It neither supports nor contradicts.
  Use this whenever there is no outcome showing the drug helped or failed for the disease. Do NOT
  force such an abstract into supporting or contradicting.

Rules:
- Judge by {drug}'s effect on {disease}'s OWN outcome. A benefit on a side metabolic marker (weight,
  glucose, lipids) is NOT benefit for {disease} unless the disease's defined endpoint improved.
- If the benefit in the abstract belongs to a DIFFERENT drug (a comparator that outperformed {drug}),
  that is "contradicting" for {drug}.
- Judge each abstract by its OWN reported results, never by the overall program or by a
  separate publication. An analysis the abstract itself reports (including a pooled or
  post-hoc analysis combining its patients with earlier trials) IS its own result.

Also report, for EACH abstract, the design of the study THAT abstract reports:

- "is_human": true if the reported study was carried out in people (trial, cohort, case series, case
  report or other human data); false if it was carried out only in animals, cells or tissue. A mouse
  or rat MODEL of a human disease is not human — judge the subjects studied, not the disease named.
- "is_controlled": true if the reported study itself compared {drug} against a control or comparator
  group (randomized, placebo-controlled, active-controlled or crossover); false for single-arm,
  open-label, uncontrolled, observational, case-report, modelling and preclinical studies.

Judge both fields from the study the abstract reports, never from a study it cites. An abstract often
opens with background sentences describing earlier work — a placebo-controlled trial mentioned there
says nothing about this abstract's own design. If the abstract does not state its own design, answer
false.
