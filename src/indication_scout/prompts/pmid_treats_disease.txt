Apply these checks IN ORDER:

1. PREVENTION: Was the drug deliberately administered, and does the abstract separately report a
   comparative result for the candidate disease's incidence or onset as a clinical outcome? If yes,
   answer "treats" even when the abstract does not label the outcome primary or secondary, the
   patients did not have the disease at baseline, were selected for a different disease or risk
   state, and the drug failed to prevent the disease or increased its rate. A biomarker or surrogate
   that merely tracks disease risk does not. A safety or causation study asking whether drug exposure
   CAUSES the candidate disease does not.
2. TREATMENT: Otherwise, NAME the condition the drug was given to treat, then ask whether that named
   target IS the candidate disease. Do not infer the target from the patient population.

- "treats": the condition the drug was administered to treat IS the candidate disease, and the
  abstract reports an outcome for it.
- "not_treats": the drug was given for a different target — another disease, a comorbidity, a
  complication, a side effect of another drug, or a lab or metabolic measure — even when the
  candidate disease was present in the patients, and even when the target is caused by or attributed
  to the candidate disease. A symptom the disease explains is still not the disease.
- "not_treats": the treated condition is a related but clinically distinct disease. Shared anatomy,
  ischemia, pathology, or downstream injury does not make two diseases interchangeable. For example,
  neonatal hypoxic-ischemic brain injury is not ischemic stroke, and cerebral small-vessel disease is
  not ischemic stroke.
- "not_treats": the candidate disease appears only as an incidental observed event or association,
  rather than a prespecified treatment or prevention outcome.
- "not_treats": the candidate is a physiological function, dysfunction, biomarker, or mechanistic
  endpoint measured to explain how treatment affects a different clinical condition. This remains
  "not_treats" when the endpoint is named in the title or objectives. For example, flow-mediated
  dilation or penile endothelial-function measurements in a trial treating heart failure or erectile
  dysfunction are not treatment of endothelial dysfunction as a standalone disease.
- "not_treats": the abstract reports no outcome for any condition — a protocol, a narrative
  commentary, or a mechanistic discussion with no result of its own.

ELIMINATION TEST for a symptom or score endpoint: if the measured endpoint were fully normalized,
would the patient STILL HAVE the candidate disease?
  NO — normalizing it means the disease is gone, so the target IS the disease => "treats".
    e.g. symptom-rating scores in an ADHD trial; pain scores in a neuropathic-pain trial; blood
    pressure in hypertension.
  YES — the disease remains and only a symptom was relieved => "not_treats".
    e.g. pain after a stroke; depression after a stroke; fatigue in multiple sclerosis.

When genuinely unsure whether the target is the disease itself or something the disease merely
explains, answer "not_treats".
