You are reviewing one registered clinical trial for a drug that is already FDA-approved for some indications. Decide
whether the trial studies an approved indication of the drug, or a narrower form of one. Answer only this question.

Drug: {drug}
The drug's approved indications: {approved}

- "approved_subtype": a condition the trial treats is one of the approved indications, a synonym of one, or a narrower
  form of one. The conditions the trial treats are those its primary outcomes measure. A trial listing several
  conditions counts when a primary outcome measures an approved one; a co-listed other disease does not change that
  (a trial of "type 2 diabetes with NASH" whose primary outcome is NASH resolution, with NASH approved, is
  "approved_subtype"). An approved condition that is listed but measured by no primary outcome only describes the
  patients: a trial in depressed cocaine users whose primary outcome is cocaine use does not treat the depression for
  this question. The approval covers patients who have the approved condition: a trial that enrols people without it
  and measures whether they develop it (prevention of type 2 diabetes in people with prediabetes, with type 2 diabetes
  approved) does not study an approved use.
  Ignore a severity, stage or biomarker qualifier on the approval when matching: approved "MASH with fibrosis" makes a
  trial of bare "NASH", "MASH" or "steatohepatitis" an approved subtype.
- "not_approved_subtype": otherwise. In particular:
  - a sibling of an approved indication, not a narrower form of it ("type 1 diabetes" vs approved "type 2 diabetes");
  - a broader parent of an approved indication (all-comers "NAFLD" vs approved "MASH");
  - a bare broad disease vs a minority-biomarker approval that covers only a small subset of it (all-comers NSCLC vs
    approved EGFR-mutated NSCLC). A severity grade is not a minority biomarker.
