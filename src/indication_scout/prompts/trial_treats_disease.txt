NAME the condition this trial set out to treat, then ask whether that named condition IS the
candidate disease. Read the objective off the title, summary and primary outcomes. Do not infer it
from the enrolled population — patients bring their other diagnoses with them.

- "treats": the condition the trial set out to treat IS the candidate disease, and its primary
  outcome measures that disease.
- "treats": PREVENTION — the trial administers the drug and measures the candidate disease's
  incidence or onset as a prespecified clinical outcome, even in people who do not have it at
  baseline.
- "not_treats": the trial targets a COMPLICATION, comorbidity or manifestation occurring in patients
  who have the candidate disease, rather than the candidate disease itself. Being caused by the
  candidate disease does not make it the candidate disease. For example, a trial treating
  cardiomyopathy in muscular dystrophy patients treats the cardiomyopathy, and a trial treating
  pulmonary hypertension in lung disease patients treats the pulmonary hypertension.
- "not_treats": the trial's objective is pharmacokinetics, bioavailability, drug-drug interaction,
  dose-finding, tolerability or safety characterization rather than treating a condition — even when
  it is run in patients who have the candidate disease, and even when it measures a clinical
  variable along the way.
- "not_treats": the trial treats a related but clinically distinct disease. Shared anatomy,
  pathology or downstream injury does not make two diseases interchangeable.
- "not_treats": the candidate disease appears only as an enrolment criterion, a background
  condition, an observed event, or a safety endpoint.

When genuinely unsure whether the trial's target is the candidate disease itself or something the
candidate disease merely explains, answer "not_treats".
