# Why IndicationScout

IndicationScout surfaces, organizes, and prioritizes drug-repurposing
opportunities already represented in public evidence. A drug name goes in; the
candidate indications come from Open Targets associations and competitor-drug
activity, never from the model. It synthesizes known
information and does not claim that the underlying relationships are new to
science.

| User | What they would use IndicationScout for |
|---|---|
| Drug-repurposing researchers | Screen and triage drug-indication hypotheses within existing public evidence and select candidates for deeper investigation. |
| Translational scientists | Connect mechanism, literature, clinical-trial, and safety evidence for a drug-disease relationship they are evaluating. |
| Pharma and biotech portfolio teams | Identify additional opportunities for existing assets and prioritize where to spend scientific diligence resources. |
| Clinical-development strategists | Determine whether a candidate indication has active development, completed readouts, terminated programs, or limited human evidence. |
| Business-development and licensing teams | Evaluate the public evidence and competitive activity behind a potential licensing or partnership opportunity. |
| Competitive-intelligence analysts | See which sponsors, competitor drugs, trials, and development stages are active in each candidate indication for a given drug. |
| Academic researchers | Find relevant hypotheses, publications, trials, and evidence gaps for research planning. |
| Research consultants and investment analysts | Conduct an initial technical landscape assessment before commissioning specialist diligence. |
| Research operations and knowledge-management teams | Produce a repeatable, cited first-pass evidence review per drug instead of searching each database manually. |
| System evaluation and validation researchers | Use historical cutoffs and saved reports to evaluate whether known opportunities could have been identified earlier. |
