## Findings by Disease

### Heart Failure

#### Literature — Heart Failure

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 7

The evidence is mixed: several small randomized controlled trials report that sildenafil improves cardiac output, exercise capacity, diastolic function, pulmonary pressures, and quality of life in heart failure patients with reduced ejection fraction (PMIDs 16275194, 18036451, 18381181, 21036891), but a larger multinational RCT (SilHF) found no improvement in symptoms, quality of life, or exercise capacity in HFrEF with pulmonary hypertension (PMID 35596935), and another RCT found no significant effect on blood pressure or 6-minute walk distance (PMID 23241003). In HFpEF, a RELAX trial sub-study found opposing vascular and ventricular effects with no net clinical benefit (PMID 25782985), consistent with the neutral primary RELAX trial result.

**Key findings:**
- In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover study of 20 HFrEF patients, sildenafil 50 mg significantly increased cardiac index by 0.37 L/min/m² and reduced total systemic resistance by 479 dynes·s·cm⁻⁵ (PMID 16275194).
- A 6-month RCT in 46 CHF patients found sildenafil 50 mg twice daily significantly improved peak VO₂ (14.8 to 18.7 mL/min/kg), reduced VE/VCO₂ slope, lowered pulmonary artery systolic pressure, and improved endothelial function compared to placebo (PMID 18036451).
- A 4-week double-blind RCT in 19 CHF patients showed sildenafil improved maximal oxygen uptake, ventilatory efficiency, oxygen uptake kinetics, and reduced pulmonary artery systolic pressure compared to placebo (PMID 18381181).
- A 1-year prospective RCT in 45 systolic HF patients found sildenafil 50 mg three times daily significantly improved LV ejection fraction, diastolic function (E/E' ratio), reversed cardiac remodeling (LV mass index, left atrial volume index), peak VO₂, and quality of life (PMID 21036891).
- The multinational SilHF RCT (n=69 HFrEF patients with PASP ≥40 mmHg) found sildenafil did not improve symptoms, quality of life, PASP, or 6-minute walk distance compared to placebo, and those on sildenafil had numerically more serious adverse events (33% vs. 21%) (PMID 35596935).
- A 12-week single-center RCT in 106 systolic HF patients found sildenafil did not significantly reduce mean blood pressure (placebo-corrected effect 1.16 mmHg, P>0.05) nor improve 6-minute walk distance (non-significant +14 m, P=0.67) (PMID 23241003).
- In a RELAX trial sub-study of 48 HFpEF patients, sildenafil reduced arterial elastance (beneficial vascular effect) but also reduced LV contractility by 11–16% compared to placebo, with no effect on pulmonary artery systolic pressure, suggesting offsetting ventricular and vascular effects (PMID 25782985).

### Raynaud Disease

#### Literature — Raynaud Disease

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 4

The overall body of evidence supports sildenafil as an efficacious treatment for Raynaud's phenomenon, with the majority of studies demonstrating improvements in attack frequency, duration, blood flow, and symptom severity compared to placebo or baseline. Two controlled trials showed statistically significant reductions in attack frequency and improved microvascular blood flow with sildenafil versus placebo (PMID: 21360507; PMID: 28281457), and an uncontrolled pilot study reported significant improvement in RP severity (PMID: 22833239). The two mixed-verdict studies acknowledge statistical superiority over placebo but note limitations such as modest aggregate effect sizes and heterogeneity in patient response (PMID: 30383134; PMID: 21360507), which do not negate the net positive direction of the evidence.

**Key findings:**
- A double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT found that modified-release sildenafil produced a significantly greater mean percentage reduction in RP attack frequency compared to placebo (-44.0% vs. -18.1%, P=0.034) in patients with lcSSc-related RP (PMID: 21360507).
- A double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT demonstrated that sildenafil 100 mg/day for 8 weeks significantly improved finger blood flow before and after cold stimulus (p=0.026 and p=0.028, respectively) and reduced RP attack duration and VAS severity scores versus placebo in SSc patients (PMID: 28281457).
- An n-of-1 randomized, double-blind trial series found a greater than 90% probability that sildenafil at 40 mg or 80 mg was superior to placebo for most outcomes, though aggregate effect sizes were not clinically relevant and substantial heterogeneity in response was observed (PMID: 30383134).
- An open-label uncontrolled pilot study reported statistically significant improvement in severity of Raynaud's phenomenon after 3 months of sildenafil 25 mg three times daily, with no new digital infarcts and healing of existing ulcers in SSc patients (PMID: 22833239).

### Infertility

#### Literature — Infertility

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 5

The evidence is mixed: a systematic review and meta-analysis of nine studies found sildenafil significantly improved endometrial thickness, clinical pregnancy rate, and biochemical pregnancy rate in women with thin endometria (PMID: 33176524), and a double-blind RCT found oral sildenafil added to clomiphene citrate produced higher Applebaum scores and clinical pregnancy rates than estradiol valerate (PMID: 39070520). However, a randomized placebo-controlled trial found no significant improvement in endometrial thickness or pregnancy outcomes with vaginal sildenafil in repeated IVF failure patients (PMID: 31710189), a controlled study found neither sildenafil nor vaginal estradiol improved endometrial thickness in women with thin endometria (PMID: 15266759), and a comparative observational study found sildenafil yielded lower clinical pregnancy rates than estrogen as adjuvant therapy (PMID: 37241011).

**Key findings:**
- A meta-analysis of nine studies (n=1452) found sildenafil significantly increased endometrial thickness (WMD: 1.22; 95% CI: 1.07–1.38), reduced radial artery resistance index, and improved clinical pregnancy rate (RR: 1.31; 95% CI: 1.11–1.53) and biochemical pregnancy rate (RR: 1.45; 95% CI: 1.11–1.89) compared to placebo or no treatment (PMID: 33176524).
- A double-blind RCT found that adding oral sildenafil to clomiphene citrate produced significantly higher Applebaum scores (17.05 vs. 15.14, P=0.000) and clinical pregnancy rates (28.92% vs. 20.83%, P=0.04) compared to adding estradiol valerate (PMID: 39070520).
- A randomized placebo-controlled trial in women with repeated IVF/ICSI failures found no statistically significant difference in endometrial thickness or implantation, chemical, or clinical pregnancy rates between vaginal sildenafil and placebo groups, though a clinically meaningful but non-significant two-fold increase in chemical pregnancy was observed (PMID: 31710189).
- A controlled study found that neither vaginal sildenafil nor vaginal estradiol significantly improved endometrial thickness or blood flow in women with a history of thin endometria undergoing frozen embryo transfer cycles (PMID: 15266759).
- A comparative observational study found clinical pregnancy rates of 46% in the sildenafil group versus 58% in the estrogen group and 27% in the clomiphene-only control group, with statistically significant differences across groups (p=0.005), suggesting sildenafil was less effective than estrogen as adjuvant therapy for unexplained infertility (PMID: 37241011).

### Covid-19

#### Literature — Covid-19

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 2

The available evidence from two small randomized controlled trials points in a generally positive direction for certain clinical outcomes but falls short of consistent statistical significance across primary endpoints, yielding a mixed overall verdict. One trial found non-significant trends toward reduced mortality and intubation but a significant reduction in ICU stay, with significant effects only after adjustment for PAH severity (PMID: 37243243). The other pilot trial found no significant improvement in its primary oxygenation endpoints, yet showed significant reductions in invasive mechanical ventilation requirement and hospital length of stay (PMID: 34980198).

**Key findings:**
- In a placebo-controlled RCT of ICU patients with severe COVID-19 and PAH, sildenafil reduced one-week mortality (4% vs. 13.3%, p=0.078) and intubation rate (8% vs. 18.7%, p=0.09) without reaching statistical significance on unadjusted primary endpoints, but significantly reduced ICU stay (15 vs. 19 days, p<0.001) (PMID: 37243243).
- After adjustment for PAH, sildenafil significantly reduced mortality risk (OR=0.21, 95% CI: 0.05–0.89) and intubation risk (OR=0.26, 95% CI: 0.08–0.86) in severe COVID-19 patients (PMID: 37243243).
- In a pilot RCT of COVID-19 inpatients with perfusion mismatch on sCTA, sildenafil showed no significant difference in primary oxygenation outcomes (PaO2/FiO2 ratio and A-a gradient, p=0.67 and p=0.69 respectively) (PMID: 34980198).
- Sildenafil-treated patients required invasive mechanical ventilation significantly less often than placebo (0 vs. 4 patients, log-rank p=0.04) and had a shorter median hospital stay (9 vs. 12 days, p=0.04) (PMID: 34980198).
- Both trials were small (n=150 and n=40 respectively), limiting the ability to draw definitive conclusions, and both called for larger confirmatory studies (PMID: 37243243; PMID: 34980198).

### Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia

#### Literature — Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 8

The preponderance of evidence from randomized controlled trials and other controlled studies supports sildenafil as effective for improving lower urinary tract symptoms associated with benign prostatic hyperplasia. Multiple placebo-controlled trials demonstrated significant improvements in IPSS scores with sildenafil versus placebo (PMIDs 17296414, 28590828, 18597830), and a self-controlled trial showed sildenafil significantly improved IPSS, PVR, and QoL (PMID 37245088). The single contradicting study (PMID 21602833) found sildenafil monotherapy did not significantly improve IPSS, but it was an open-label, uncontrolled comparison against combination therapy rather than placebo, limiting its weight against the consistent positive controlled evidence.

**Key findings:**
- In a 12-week double-blind RCT (n=369), sildenafil produced significantly greater improvement in IPSS versus placebo (-6.32 vs -1.93, p<0.0001), along with improvements in BPH Impact Index and quality of life, though no difference in urinary flow rates was observed (PMID 17296414).
- A post-hoc analysis of the same RCT confirmed that sildenafil significantly improved LUTS scores versus placebo across all BMI categories and in men with severe LUTS (-8.6 vs -2.4, p<0.0001), with no significant difference in maximal urinary flow rate (PMID 18597830).
- A multicenter double-blind RCT found sildenafil 25 mg qd and 25 mg bid significantly improved IPSS versus placebo (p=0.020 and p=0.025, respectively), and sildenafil 50 mg qd specifically improved nocturia significantly versus placebo (p=0.027), though Qmax and PVR did not change significantly in any group (PMID 28590828).
- A randomized controlled uroflowmetric study showed a single 50 mg dose of sildenafil significantly increased Qmax (15.6 to 19.3 cc/s, p<0.0001) and average flow rate in men with ED and BPH-LUTS compared to untreated controls (PMID 18649004).
- A single-arm self-controlled trial (n=33) demonstrated that sildenafil significantly improved PVR, IPSS, and IPSS-QoL index (all p<0.001), and was more effective than tadalafil in reducing PVR and improving QoL index (PMID 37245088).
- A prospective open randomized study (n=30) found statistically significant improvements in IPSS (12.8 to 8.6, 32.8% change), post-voiding residuum, and maximal urine flow with continuous low-dose sildenafil, though the authors acknowledged the small sample size as a limitation (PMID 29693865).
- A pilot RCT comparing alfuzosin, sildenafil, and combination therapy found IPSS improvement with sildenafil monotherapy (-11.8%) was significant but smaller than with alfuzosin (-15.6%) or combination (-24.1%), and frequency, nocturia, PVR, and Qmax were significantly improved with alfuzosin and combination but not sildenafil alone (PMID 17258855).
- An open-label multicenter study found that sildenafil monotherapy significantly improved IIEF-5 scores but did not produce statistically significant improvement in IPSS or QoL scores, whereas the combination of sildenafil plus doxazosin GITS improved all three outcomes (PMID 21602833).

### Hypertensive Disorder

#### Literature — Hypertensive Disorder

**Evidence strength:** moderate, supports
**Relevant studies:** 2

Two controlled human trials provide evidence supporting sildenafil for hypertensive disorder. In a randomized, double-blind crossover study, sildenafil 50 mg three times daily for 16 days significantly reduced ambulatory and clinic blood pressure compared to placebo in untreated hypertensive subjects (PMID: 16940217). In a proof-of-concept study in treatment-resistant hypertension, sildenafil alone acutely reduced brachial and central aortic blood pressure, with additional reduction when combined with isosorbide mononitrate (PMID: 20479335). **Key findings:**

- Sildenafil also reduced arterial wave reflection acutely and after chronic treatment, though the chronic change was not statistically different from placebo (PMID: 16940217).
- In treatment-resistant hypertension patients on usual antihypertensive medication, sildenafil 50 mg alone acutely reduced brachial and central aortic blood pressure compared to placebo (PMID: 20479335).
- The combination of sildenafil 50 mg and isosorbide mononitrate 10 mg produced a maximum brachial BP reduction of 26/18 mmHg compared to placebo in treatment-resistant hypertension, without significant adverse effects (PMID: 20479335).

### Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 4

### Coronary Artery Disorder

#### Literature — Coronary Artery Disorder

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 3

The available evidence on sildenafil for coronary artery disease is mixed, with both studies showing some beneficial vascular and hemodynamic effects alongside notable limitations or null findings. ### Acute Kidney Injury

#### Literature — Acute Kidney Injury

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 6

The available evidence is mixed: multiple preclinical rat studies consistently demonstrate that sildenafil attenuates AKI through antioxidant, antiapoptotic, and anti-inflammatory mechanisms (PMIDs 26850186, 19474186, 26377490, 38748227), but two well-designed double-blind, placebo-controlled RCTs in humans found no renoprotective benefit of sildenafil—one in robotic partial nephrectomy (PMID 27613357) and one in cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass (PMID 32245569). **Key findings:**
- A double-blind RCT of i.v. sildenafil in at-risk cardiac surgery patients found no significant difference in serum creatinine between sildenafil and placebo groups (MD: 0.88 µmol/L), and sildenafil was associated with a statistically significant increase in multiple organ dysfunction scores (PMID 32245569).

- In a glycerol-induced ARF rat model, sildenafil alone and in combination with furosemide improved renal function markers, reduced KIM-1 and NGAL tubular expression, and decreased caspase-3 and IL-1β immunohistochemical markers of cellular damage (PMID 38748227).

### Chronic Kidney Disease

#### Literature — Chronic Kidney Disease

**Evidence strength:** weak, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 2

The available evidence on sildenafil for chronic kidney disease is mixed. A preclinical study in STZ-induced diabetic nephropathic rats showed significant anti-inflammatory and anti-apoptotic renal benefits with sildenafil (PMID: 41482414), while a clinical study in cats with acute-on-chronic kidney disease found that sildenafil induced only low-level mitochondrial biogenesis and was ineffective in mitigating renal damage and fibrosis via the TGF-β/SMAD pathway (PMID: 39478527). These two comparably relevant but conflicting findings—one positive preclinical and one mixed clinical—do not allow a clear net determination of efficacy.

**Key findings:**
- In STZ-induced diabetic nephropathic rats, sildenafil significantly decreased renal expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines TNF-α and IL-6 and apoptotic marker Bax, while increasing anti-apoptotic Bcl-2 expression, suggesting potential to slow diabetic nephropathy progression (PMID: 41482414).
- In cats with acute-on-chronic kidney disease, sildenafil citrate was found to have a beneficial effect on glomerular filtration but was ineffective in mitigating renal damage and fibrosis via the TGF-β/SMAD pathway (PMID: 39478527).
- Serum cGMP concentrations increased in non-azotemic cats treated with sildenafil but decreased in the ACKD group by week 4, suggesting impaired cGMP/NO pathway responsiveness in diseased kidneys (PMID: 39478527).
- Urinary biomarkers of kidney damage (NGAL, KIM-1, IL-18, TGF-β1) remained elevated throughout the study in the ACKD group despite sildenafil treatment, indicating limited therapeutic impact on renal injury markers (PMID: 39478527).

### Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease

#### Literature — Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease

**Evidence strength:** strong, contradicts
**Relevant studies:** 5

The preponderance of controlled trial evidence does not support sildenafil as an effective treatment for COPD. Three randomized controlled trials found no improvement in exercise capacity, and one additionally reported worsened gas exchange and quality of life (PMIDs: 23429918, 22360383, 39389522), while the single positive RCT (PMID: 21545068) is offset by the weight of consistent negative evidence from comparably or better-designed studies. A hemodynamic study confirmed pulmonary artery pressure reduction but no improvement in exercise capacity (PMID: 18342559), further undermining the mechanistic rationale for clinical benefit.

**Key findings:**
- A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 63 patients with severe COPD and elevated pulmonary arterial pressure found no significant difference in cycle endurance time, 6-minute walk distance, or quality of life between sildenafil and placebo groups (PMID: 23429918).
- A randomized crossover trial in COPD patients with emphysema found sildenafil had no effect on 6-minute walk distance or peak oxygen consumption, and significantly worsened gas exchange, symptoms, and quality of life compared to placebo (PMID: 22360383).
- A randomized crossover trial in mild-to-moderate COPD found that acute oral sildenafil (50 mg or 100 mg) failed to improve ventilatory efficiency, gas exchange, dyspnea, or exercise tolerance compared to placebo (PMID: 39389522).
- A right heart catheterization study showed sildenafil attenuated exercise-induced increases in mean pulmonary artery pressure in COPD patients, but this was not accompanied by improved stroke volume, cardiac output, or maximal exercise capacity (PMID: 18342559).
- A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 33 patients with severe COPD reported a significant increase in 6-minute walk distance (median change +190 m) and reduction in pulmonary artery pressure after 12 weeks of sildenafil compared to placebo (PMID: 21545068).

### Endothelial Dysfunction

#### Literature — Endothelial Dysfunction

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 5

The evidence is mixed, with comparably credible controlled trials pointing in both directions. Two randomized placebo-controlled crossover studies found that sildenafil improved endothelial function in specific contexts—preventing ischemia-reperfusion-induced FMD impairment (PMID: 15699265) and abrogating acute smoking-induced FMD reduction (PMID: 15533731)—while two other randomized placebo-controlled crossover studies found no improvement in endothelial function in patients with coronary heart disease (PMID: 15863522) or in chronic smokers (PMID: 14748820). A preclinical/in vitro study in CTEPH-derived endothelial cells showed partial restoration of ERK/eNOS signaling with sildenafil (PMID: 40763482), but this does not resolve the conflicting clinical trial evidence.

**Key findings:**
- In a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled crossover study of 16 patients with coronary heart disease, sildenafil increased endothelium-independent vasodilatation but did not improve acetylcholine-mediated forearm blood flow or fibrinolytic function, leading to the conclusion that PDE5 inhibition does not reverse endothelial dysfunction in coronary heart disease (PMID: 15863522).
- In a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled crossover study of 10 healthy male volunteers, oral sildenafil 50 mg preserved flow-mediated dilatation of the radial artery after ischemia-reperfusion (FMD after IR: 6.2±1.1% with sildenafil vs. 1.2±0.7% with placebo, P<0.01), demonstrating protection against IR-induced endothelial dysfunction via K(ATP) channel opening (PMID: 15699265).
- In a randomized placebo-controlled crossover study of 14 male smokers, sildenafil 50 mg abolished the acute smoking-induced decrease in brachial artery FMD (sildenafil/smoking: 3.83% to 4.33% vs. placebo/smoking: 4.56% to 2.80%, P<0.05), suggesting PDE5 inhibition can abrogate acute smoking-induced endothelial dysfunction (PMID: 15533731).
- In a randomized double-blind crossover study of 9 chronic smokers, sildenafil 50 mg produced no significant change in brachial artery flow-mediated dilation (0.18%, 95% CI -1.7–2%) or postischemic reactive hyperemia compared to placebo, indicating no improvement in endothelial function in chronic smokers (PMID: 14748820).
- In an in vitro study using endothelial cells isolated from CTEPH patients, treatment with sildenafil partially restored ERK/eNOS signaling (which was dysregulated compared to healthy controls) but did not restore PKG signaling, suggesting only partial recovery of endothelial function at the molecular level (PMID: 40763482).

**Supporting PMIDs:** [15699265](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15699265/), [15533731](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15533731/), [40763482](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40763482/)

**Contradicting PMIDs:** [15863522](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15863522/), [14748820](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14748820/)
