## Drug Safety

_Drug-wide safety signal — applies to the drug generally, not to any single candidate indication below._

**Regulatory label:** FDA label boxed-warning text (7 product labels, LLM-summarized — see appendix for verbatim text):
Across all seven labels reviewed, the sole boxed-warning hazard is the risk of thyroid C-cell tumors, specifically medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC): in rodents, semaglutide causes dose-dependent and treatment-duration-dependent thyroid C-cell tumors at clinically relevant exposures, and it is unknown whether semaglutide causes such tumors in humans because human relevance has not been determined. All labels contraindicate use in patients with a personal or family history of MTC or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2), require counseling patients on MTC risk and symptoms (e.g., neck mass, dysphagia, dyspnea, persistent hoarseness), and state that routine serum calcitonin monitoring or thyroid ultrasound is of uncertain value for early MTC detection. One substantive difference in scope exists: the OZEMPIC label (first product listed) names both RYBELSUS and OZEMPIC in the boxed warning text itself — extending the contraindication and counseling requirements explicitly to the oral tablet formulation (RYBELSUS) — whereas all other labels restrict their boxed-warning language solely to the single named product (Ozempic or Wegovy). No label introduces any additional hazard beyond thyroid C-cell tumor risk, and no label differs in the direction of effect, an age cutoff, or a distinct risk population.

## Findings by Disease

### Stroke Disorder

#### Literature — Stroke Disorder

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 6

The preponderance of evidence from randomized controlled trials and meta-analyses supports semaglutide in reducing stroke risk. The SUSTAIN 6 trial demonstrated a significant reduction in nonfatal stroke (HR 0.61, 95% CI 0.38–0.99, p=0.04) [PMID: 27633186], and a post hoc pooled analysis confirmed semaglutide reduced any first stroke versus placebo (HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.46–1.00) [PMID: 35582947]. A systematic review and meta-analysis across 17 studies found a 32% reduction in nonfatal stroke (HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.56–0.83) [PMID: 41188987], while the one contradicting meta-analysis finding no significant stroke effect was based on only four RCTs and is outweighed by the broader and more comprehensive evidence base [PMID: 41272444].

**Key findings:**
- In SUSTAIN 6, nonfatal stroke occurred in 1.6% of semaglutide patients versus 2.7% of placebo patients (HR 0.61, 95% CI 0.38–0.99, p=0.04) [PMID: 27633186].
- A post hoc pooled analysis of SUSTAIN 6 and PIONEER 6 found semaglutide reduced incidence of any first stroke versus placebo (0.8 vs 1.1 events/100 PYO; HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.46–1.00; p=0.048), driven significantly by small-vessel occlusion prevention (HR 0.51, 95% CI 0.29–0.89; p=0.017) [PMID: 35582947].
- A systematic review and meta-analysis of 17 studies (n=40,632) found semaglutide reduced nonfatal stroke by 32% (HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.56–0.83) [PMID: 41188987].
- A phase 2 randomized trial in large vessel occlusion patients found no significant difference in favorable neurological recovery overall (adjusted RR 1.05, 95% CI 0.95–1.15, p=0.37), but exploratory analysis suggested benefit in patients not receiving intravenous thrombolysis (adjusted RR 1.18, 95% CI 1.02–1.36), yielding a mixed verdict for this acute stroke intervention setting [PMID: 41392086].
- A meta-analysis of four RCTs found semaglutide showed no significant effect on nonfatal stroke incidence, though it did significantly reduce overall MACE (RR 0.81, 95% CI 0.74–0.88) [PMID: 41272444].
- Semaglutide reduced stroke risk irrespective of prior stroke at baseline in the pooled SUSTAIN 6 and PIONEER 6 analysis [PMID: 35582947].

### Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

#### Literature — Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 14

Multiple controlled trials and observational studies consistently show that semaglutide improves glycemic control, reduces body weight, and lowers insulin requirements in adults with type 1 diabetes, particularly those with overweight or obesity. A 26-week double-blind RCT (PMID: 40550013) demonstrated that significantly more semaglutide-treated patients achieved the composite endpoint of time-in-range >70%, time-below-range <4%, and ≥5% weight loss compared to placebo (36% vs. 0%), and a separate double-blind crossover RCT (PMID: 39794615) confirmed increased time in target glucose range without increased hypoglycemia. A systematic review and meta-analysis (PMID: 41398803) and multiple real-world studies (PMIDs: 38444317, 39745353, 40318459, 40712645) corroborate these findings, though semaglutide is not yet FDA-approved for type 1 diabetes and gastrointestinal adverse events and careful insulin dose management are important safety considerations (PMID: 41804758).

**Key findings:**
- In a 26-week double-blind RCT (ADJUST-T1D), 36% of semaglutide-treated adults with T1D and obesity achieved the composite endpoint of TIR >70%, TBR <4%, and ≥5% weight loss versus 0% in placebo; semaglutide reduced body weight by 8.8 kg and improved HbA1c by 0.3 percentage points versus placebo (PMID: 40550013).
- A double-blind randomized crossover trial showed semaglutide increased time in target glucose range (3.9–10.0 mmol/L) by a mean 4.8 percentage points versus placebo without increasing time below range, with no severe hypoglycemia or DKA (PMID: 39794615).
- Post-hoc analysis of ADJUST-T1D found semaglutide produced a 22.6% reduction in total daily insulin dose over 26 weeks, driven more by bolus (-30.5%) than basal (-15.6%) reductions, with early effects largely independent of weight loss (PMID: 41429002).
- A post-hoc analysis of a crossover trial showed insulin needs decreased rapidly upon semaglutide initiation in T1D on pump therapy, predominantly due to reduced bolus insulin from less carbohydrate consumption, with adjustments to pump dosing required (PMID: 41144928).
- Following semaglutide discontinuation in the ADJUST-T1D extension, participants experienced greater decline in time in range (median -3.6% vs. +1.5% for placebo) and increased glycemic variability, suggesting benefits are not fully sustained after withdrawal (PMID: 42675550).
- A systematic review and meta-analysis of 8 studies (N=274 adults with T1D and overweight/obesity) found semaglutide reduced body weight by 6.53% and HbA1c by 0.55% at 6 months, and improved time in range by 4.62% at 3 months (PMID: 41398803).
- A retrospective chart review of 50 T1D patients on semaglutide showed significantly greater declines in BMI (7.9%), body weight (15.9 lbs), HbA1c, CGM glucose SD and CV, and increased TIR compared to matched controls (PMID: 38444317).
- A retrospective study of 100 adults with T1D found semaglutide users lost 9.1% body weight and reduced HbA1c by 0.54% over 12 months, with no change in controls (PMID: 39745353).
- A 2-year real-world study in 67 adults with T1D showed TIR improved from 46% to 71%, HbA1c from 8.2% to 7.1%, and total daily insulin dose halved from 1.4 to 0.7 IU/kg/day, with no DKA hospitalizations (PMID: 40318459).
- A real-world 12-month study in 42 overweight/obese T1D adults showed mean relative weight loss of 13.3%, HbA1c reduction of 0.4%, total daily insulin reduction of 13.6%, and MASLD prevalence reduction from 82.6% to 30.4% with semaglutide (PMID: 40712645).
- Two adolescent case reports (ages 12 and 17) with T1D and obesity showed notable improvements in glycemic control, weight, and insulin requirements after 12 months of semaglutide without adverse events (PMID: 42097623).
- A patient-reported outcomes analysis from a crossover RCT found 42% of T1D participants expressed interest in continued semaglutide use outside the study, citing lower insulin requirements, weight loss, and improved glycemic management, though the Diabetes Bowel Symptom Questionnaire showed increased GI symptom frequency and severity with semaglutide (PMID: 41175929).
- A real-world survey of 230 adults with T1D using semaglutide or tirzepatide found symptomatic hypoglycemia was less frequent with semaglutide than tirzepatide (13.4% vs. 29%), while gastrointestinal adverse events were comparable between groups (PMID: 41804758).

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 11

Multiple large randomized controlled trials and pooled analyses consistently demonstrate that semaglutide improves heart failure outcomes across HF subtypes. In patients with obesity-related HFpEF, semaglutide significantly improved KCCQ-CSS scores, body weight, 6-minute walk distance, and reduced NT-proBNP compared to placebo (PMID: 37622681, 38587233, 38599221, 38819334). In broader cardiovascular and cardiorenal populations, semaglutide reduced composite HF events and cardiovascular death in patients with prevalent HF, including those with T2D and CKD (PMID: 39181597, 39217553, 39222642, 41627802), and a pooled analysis across four trials confirmed reduction in worsening HF events (HR 0.59) and the composite of CV death or HF events (HR 0.69) in HFpEF patients (PMID: 39222642). The evidence supports repurposing semaglutide for heart failure, particularly HFpEF in the context of obesity.

**Key findings:**
- In the STEP-HFpEF trial, semaglutide 2.4 mg improved KCCQ-CSS by 16.6 points vs 8.7 points with placebo (estimated difference 7.8 points, P<0.001) and reduced body weight by 13.3% vs 2.6% in patients with HFpEF and obesity (PMID: 37622681)
- In the STEP-HFpEF DM trial, semaglutide improved KCCQ-CSS (estimated difference 7.3 points, P<0.001) and reduced body weight by 9.8% vs 3.4% with placebo in patients with HFpEF, obesity, and type 2 diabetes (PMID: 38587233)
- Pooled analysis of STEP-HFpEF and STEP-HFpEF DM (n=1145) showed semaglutide improved KCCQ-CSS (between-group difference 7.5 points, P<0.0001), reduced body weight by 8.4% more than placebo, improved 6-min walk distance by 17.1 m, and reduced CRP (treatment ratio 0.64), with effects consistent across subgroups (PMID: 38599221)
- In the SELECT trial prespecified analysis, semaglutide reduced MACE (HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.60-0.87) and composite HF endpoint (HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.64-0.98) in patients with prevalent heart failure, with benefits seen in both HFpEF and HFrEF subtypes (PMID: 39181597)
- In the FLOW trial prespecified analysis (T2D and CKD), semaglutide reduced composite HF events or CV death (HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.62-0.87, P=0.0005), HF events alone (HR 0.73, P=0.0068), and CV death alone (HR 0.71, P=0.0036), with consistent effects regardless of baseline HF history (PMID: 39217553)
- Pooled analysis across SELECT, FLOW, STEP-HFpEF, and STEP-HFpEF DM (n=3743 with HFpEF) showed semaglutide reduced CV death or worsening HF events (HR 0.69, 95% CI 0.53-0.89, P=0.0045) and worsening HF events alone (HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.41-0.82, P=0.0019) (PMID: 39222642)
- In the SOUL trial secondary analysis, oral semaglutide reduced composite HF outcomes (HR 0.78, 95% CI 0.63-0.96) in participants with T2D and HF at baseline, with particular benefit in those with preserved ejection fraction (HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.39-0.86) (PMID: 41627802)
- Semaglutide reduced NT-proBNP at 52 weeks in obesity-related HFpEF (estimated treatment ratio 0.82, 95% CI 0.74-0.91, P=0.0002), with larger improvements in KCCQ-CSS in those with higher baseline NT-proBNP (P interaction=0.02) (PMID: 38819334)
- In the STEP-HFpEF programme pooled subanalysis, semaglutide efficacy on KCCQ-CSS and body weight was consistent across age groups (<55, 55-64, 65-74, ≥75 years) with no treatment effect heterogeneity (P-interaction=0.80 and 0.41 respectively) (PMID: 41290376)
- Meta-analysis of semaglutide in HFpEF patients showed significant improvements in 6-min walk distance (MD 16.20 m, P<0.01), reductions in systolic blood pressure (MD -2.22 mmHg, P<0.01), CRP, and NT-proBNP compared to placebo (PMID: 39907981)
- In the SELECT trial analysis by HbA1c, semaglutide reduced the heart failure composite (HF hospitalization, urgent medical visit, or CV mortality) consistently across baseline HbA1c subgroups in patients with overweight/obesity and ASCVD without diabetes (PMID: 38907684)

### Polycystic Ovary Syndrome

#### Literature — Polycystic Ovary Syndrome

**Evidence strength:** moderate, supports
**Relevant studies:** 8

The evidence supports repurposing semaglutide for polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). A meta-analysis of 8 RCTs (526 patients) demonstrated that semaglutide significantly reduced BMI (MD: -2.20 kg/m²) and improved lipid profiles in PCOS patients (PMID: 40960939), while uncontrolled human studies reported clinically meaningful weight loss (11.3–12.6 kg over 6–12 months), improvements in menstrual regularity, and metabolic parameters including HOMA-IR and HbA1c (PMIDs: 42452625, 41283946). Preclinical studies in PCOS mouse models further support direct ovarian benefits via anti-inflammatory (PMID: 39247793), antioxidant/autophagy (PMID: 40433568), and anti-ferroptotic/epigenetic mechanisms (PMID: 41803302), though large-scale RCTs with standardized reproductive endpoints are still needed (PMID: 42652226).

**Key findings:**
- Meta-analysis of 8 RCTs (526 patients) showed semaglutide significantly reduced BMI (MD: -2.20 kg/m², 95% CI: -2.42 to -1.97, P<0.001), total cholesterol, triglycerides, and LDL-C in PCOS patients, with greater efficacy at doses ≥1.0 mg/week and in patients with baseline BMI >28 kg/m² (PMID: 40960939)
- In 96 women with PCOS (PMOS) and BMI >25 kg/m², semaglutide over 6 months reduced mean body weight by 11.3±5% and restored ovulatory cycles in 52.5% of previously anovulatory women, with near-complete menstrual normalization (95%) in overweight patients (PMID: 42452625)
- Retrospective study of 58 obese PCOS women showed mean weight loss of 12.6±9.6 kg (11.4% total body weight), with 81% losing >5% body weight; 50% of those with oligomenorrhoea reported improved menstrual cycles, and significant reductions in BMI, systolic blood pressure, HbA1c, and total cholesterol were observed (PMID: 41283946)
- Scoping review of 25 studies reported clinically relevant weight reduction of 7.6–11.5 kg over 3–6 months and ≥5% weight loss in approximately 80% of patients in responsive cohorts, with improvements in fasting insulin and HOMA-IR, and improvements in menstrual regularity across several studies (PMID: 42652226)
- In PCOS mice, semaglutide restored estrous rhythm, reduced cystic follicles, corrected serum hormone imbalance, and alleviated ovarian inflammation by downregulating TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β, and NF-κB via the AMPK/SIRT1/NF-κB signaling pathway (PMID: 39247793)
- Semaglutide alleviated ovarian oxidative stress and autophagy in PCOS mice via the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway, reducing MDA and CYP17A1 while increasing CYP19A1, GSH, and SOD, and restoring estrous rhythm and glucose tolerance (PMID: 40433568)
- Semaglutide reversed ovarian ferroptosis markers, restored GPX4 expression, and reduced GPX4 promoter hypermethylation and DNMT levels in a letrozole plus high-fat diet PCOS mouse model, with in vitro validation in DHT-stimulated granulosa cells (PMID: 41803302)
- A broad review of semaglutide's therapeutic applications noted it showed promise in managing PCOS-related obesity and insulin resistance, consistent with its GLP-1 receptor agonist mechanism (PMID: 40904035)

### Coronary Artery Disorder

#### Literature — Coronary Artery Disorder

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 7

Multiple lines of evidence support semaglutide for coronary artery disorder. A prespecified analysis of the SELECT RCT showed semaglutide reduced MACE including coronary revascularization and expanded MACE endpoints in people with established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, irrespective of baseline HbA1c (PMID: 38907684), and a meta-analysis of 45 RCTs found semaglutide associated with significantly lower risks of the broad CAD composite (RR=0.80), ACS (RR=0.77), MI (RR=0.70), unstable angina (RR=0.82), and angina pectoris (RR=0.73) in adults with T2DM and/or obesity (PMID: 42469143). Large animal models of coronary artery disease further demonstrate that oral semaglutide improves myocardial perfusion, left ventricular ejection fraction, arteriolar density, and endothelium-dependent vasodilation through AMPK/eNOS-mediated mechanisms (PMID: 39211263, 39665144, 41553720), and a real-world observational study found semaglutide associated with significantly lower MACE risk versus tirzepatide in patients with established ASCVD without diabetes (PMID: 41491349).

**Key findings:**
- In the SELECT RCT, semaglutide reduced the primary MACE endpoint by 20% versus placebo in people with overweight/obesity and established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease without diabetes, with consistent cardiovascular risk reduction across all baseline HbA1c subgroups (PMID: 38907684)
- Semaglutide reduced expanded MACE (including coronary revascularization and hospitalization for unstable angina) consistently across HbA1c subgroups in the SELECT trial (PMID: 38907684)
- Meta-analysis of 45 RCTs found semaglutide associated with significantly lower risk of the broad CAD-related composite (RR=0.80, 95% CI 0.73-0.87, p<0.001) in adults with T2DM and/or obesity (PMID: 42469143)
- Semaglutide was associated with significantly lower risks of ACS (RR=0.77), MI (RR=0.70), unstable angina (RR=0.82), and angina pectoris (RR=0.73) in pooled RCT analyses (PMID: 42469143)
- Oral semaglutide improved left ventricular ejection fraction at rest and during pacing, increased perfusion to the most ischemic myocardial region, and reduced perivascular and interstitial fibrosis in a swine ameroid constrictor model of CAD (PMID: 39211263)
- Semaglutide improved myocardial perfusion and LV function in a swine CAD model through increased AMPK pathway activation and downstream increases in endothelial nitric oxide synthase (PMID: 39665144)
- In a swine model of CAD with metabolic syndrome, semaglutide enhanced LV filling, end diastolic volume, stroke volume, cardiac index, arteriolar density, and microvascular endothelium-dependent vasodilation (PMID: 41553720)
- In a real-world US claims analysis, semaglutide was associated with statistically significant 29% reduction in rMACE-3 (HR 0.71, p=0.046) and 22% reduction in rMACE-5 (HR 0.78, p=0.040) versus tirzepatide in patients with overweight/obesity and established ASCVD without diabetes (PMID: 41491349)
- A state-of-the-art review noted semaglutide demonstrated significant reductions in MACE supported by meta-analyses of trials in T2DM patients and the SELECT trial for patients without T2DM, with cardioprotective effects in ASCVD (PMID: 40163257)

### Nicotine Dependence

#### Literature — Nicotine Dependence

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 2

Evidence on semaglutide for nicotine dependence is mixed. The only randomized controlled trial (PMID: 42189538) found that semaglutide did not significantly improve its co-primary laboratory outcomes of smoking resistance or weekly cigarettes per day, though it did reduce nicotine craving and body weight. A large real-world target trial emulation (PMID: 39074369) found semaglutide associated with significantly lower rates of TUD-related healthcare encounters compared to other antidiabetes medications, suggesting a potential benefit, but this observational design cannot establish causation and is subject to confounding.

**Key findings:**
- In a phase 2a RCT, semaglutide did not significantly improve co-primary outcomes of laboratory smoking resistance (β = 0.16, P = .16) or weekly cigarettes per day (β = -0.08, P = .30) compared to placebo (PMID: 42189538).

- The real-world study also found semaglutide associated with reduced smoking cessation medication prescriptions and counseling, which may reflect reduced need for cessation support or documentation bias (PMID: 39074369).

### Binge Eating Disorder

#### Literature — Binge Eating Disorder

**Evidence strength:** moderate, supports
**Relevant studies:** 3

The available evidence consistently supports semaglutide as a beneficial treatment for binge eating disorder. A systematic review found semaglutide effective and well-tolerated in BED populations, acting on both weight and cognitive symptoms (PMID: 40577093), a retrospective cohort study showed semaglutide produced greater reductions in Binge Eating Scale scores compared to lisdexamfetamine or topiramate (PMID: 37990682), and a randomized controlled trial found semaglutide increased remission rates in participants with baseline binge eating or full/subthreshold BED compared to placebo (PMID: 43035836). **Key findings:**
- A systematic review of 37 studies concluded semaglutide is effective and well-tolerated in BED, acting not only on weight decrease but also on cognitive symptoms linked to the disease (PMID: 40577093).

- In a randomized controlled trial (n=120), participants with baseline binge episodes or full/subthreshold BED were more likely to achieve abstinence with semaglutide compared to placebo over 60 weeks (PMID: 43035836).
- The RCT also found significantly greater mean reductions in loss of control over eating, global eating disorder pathology, weight and shape concern, drive for thinness, and body dissatisfaction with semaglutide versus placebo at several timepoints (PMID: 43035836).
- The RCT noted that the two groups did not differ significantly in overall binge frequency reductions, and rare cases of disordered eating behaviors occurred, suggesting a need for periodic monitoring in at-risk individuals (PMID: 43035836).
- The systematic review also noted preliminary positive findings for semaglutide in alcohol and substance use disorder, and flagged potential neuropsychiatric risks such as depressive symptoms and suicidal ideation warranting further study (PMID: 40577093).

### Prader-willi Syndrome

#### Literature — Prader-willi Syndrome

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 5

The overall evidence for semaglutide in Prader-Willi syndrome is mixed: several case reports and small series report meaningful weight loss and glycemic improvement (PMIDs 38553173, 39996062, 35538810), while the largest observational cohort (n=12) found no significant overall weight loss—only weight stabilization—though it did show a significant reduction in hyperphagia (PMID 42755517), and a three-patient case series showed variable outcomes ranging from weight stabilization to modest weight loss (PMID 39906041). Across all studies, tolerability was generally good, but the absence of controlled trial data and inconsistent weight-loss outcomes across studies prevent a definitive positive conclusion.

**Key findings:**
- In a retrospective observational cohort of 12 adults with PWS, semaglutide produced no significant overall weight loss but did stabilize weight (post -3.1% vs. pre +5.7%, P=0.037) and significantly reduced hyperphagia scores (-7.3 points on HQ-CT, P=0.003) (PMID 42755517).
- A three-patient case series showed variable efficacy: one patient had only prevention of further weight gain, while two others achieved weight loss of 14.4% and 11% relative to baseline, with good tolerability even post-metabolic surgery (PMID 39906041).
- Four patients with PWS and type 2 diabetes treated with semaglutide showed beneficial effects on weight loss and glycemic control (PMID 38553173).
- A single case report of a 27-year-old male with PWS, obesity, and T2D showed body weight reduction from 79 kg to 71 kg over 24 months and maintained excellent glycemic control (HbA1c 38 mmol/mol) on semaglutide 0.5 mg weekly, with no adverse events (PMID 39996062).
- A case report of a 33-year-old man with PWS and poorly controlled diabetes showed HbA1c reduction from 11.1% to 7.2% and weight loss from 99.5 kg to 94.3 kg after 12 months of semaglutide 1 mg/week, even after prior liraglutide failure (PMID 35538810).
- Mild gastrointestinal side effects were reported in 25% of patients in the observational cohort but did not lead to discontinuation, supporting an acceptable tolerability profile (PMID 42755517).

### Myocardial Infarction

#### Literature — Myocardial Infarction

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 7

The preponderance of evidence from large randomized controlled trials and multiple systematic reviews and meta-analyses supports semaglutide in reducing nonfatal myocardial infarction and broader MACE outcomes. A meta-analysis of RCTs found semaglutide reduced nonfatal MI by 18% (HR 0.82, 95% CI 0.68–0.92) and another found a significant reduction (HR 0.75, 95% CI 0.68–0.84) (PMIDs 41188987, 42186086). While the PIONEER 6 trial of oral semaglutide showed a numerically higher nonfatal MI rate with semaglutide versus placebo (HR 1.18, non-significant), this single underpowered trial is outweighed by consistent positive signals across larger trials and pooled analyses (PMID 31185157, 27633186, 38907684, 41272444, 42186086).

**Key findings:**
- In SUSTAIN-6, nonfatal MI occurred in 2.9% of semaglutide patients vs. 3.9% of placebo patients (HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.51–1.08), with the composite MACE endpoint significantly reduced (HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.58–0.95) (PMID 27633186).
- In PIONEER 6 (oral semaglutide), nonfatal MI occurred in 2.3% of the semaglutide group vs. 1.9% of placebo (HR 1.18, 95% CI 0.73–1.90), a non-significant numerically unfavorable result for MI specifically (PMID 31185157).
- In the SELECT trial subgroup analysis, semaglutide 2.4 mg reduced MACE by 20% in people with overweight/obesity and atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease without diabetes, consistently across HbA1c subgroups (PMID 38907684).
- A systematic review and meta-analysis of 17 studies (n=40,632) found semaglutide reduced nonfatal MI by 18% (HR 0.82, 95% CI 0.68–0.92) and MACE by 18% (HR 0.82, 95% CI 0.75–0.90) (PMID 41188987).
- A meta-analysis of 4 RCTs (n=27,617) found semaglutide significantly reduced MACE (RR 0.81, 95% CI 0.74–0.88) with benefits observed for nonfatal myocardial infarction specifically (PMID 41272444).
- A meta-analysis of 8 RCTs (n=39,204) found semaglutide significantly reduced nonfatal MI (HR 0.75, 95% CI 0.68–0.84, p<0.0001) and MACE (HR 0.82, 95% CI 0.77–0.87) compared to placebo (PMID 42186086).

### Parkinson Disease

#### Literature — Parkinson Disease

**Evidence strength:** weak, supports
**Relevant studies:** 6

All six relevant abstracts are preclinical (animal or in vitro) studies demonstrating neuroprotective effects of semaglutide in models of Parkinson's disease. Semaglutide improved motor function, preserved dopaminergic neurons, reduced α-synuclein accumulation, and attenuated neuroinflammation and oxidative stress in MPTP and 6-OHDA rodent models (PMID: 30741689, PMID: 30017231, PMID: 35873704, PMID: 40088335), and also enhanced neural stem cell transplantation outcomes by inhibiting reactive astrocytes (PMID: 40874950). A review of animal model data further summarized these neuroprotective findings across both Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease models (PMID: 38921025). No human clinical data are available among the retrieved abstracts, so the evidence supports repurposing semaglutide for Parkinson's disease only at the preclinical level.

**Key findings:**
- Semaglutide (25 nmol/kg ip every two days for 30 days) improved MPTP-induced motor impairments, rescued tyrosine hydroxylase levels, reduced α-synuclein accumulation, alleviated chronic neuroinflammation, reduced lipid peroxidation, inhibited mitochondrial mitophagy signaling, and increased GDNF expression in the substantia nigra and striatum of chronic MPTP mice, and was more potent than liraglutide in most parameters (PMID: 30741689).
- Semaglutide (25 nmol/kg ip once daily for 7 days) improved MPTP-induced motor impairments, rescued tyrosine hydroxylase levels, alleviated inflammation, reduced lipid peroxidation, inhibited apoptosis, and increased autophagy-related protein expression in the acute MPTP mouse model, outperforming liraglutide in most parameters (PMID: 30017231).
- Semaglutide protected SH-SY5Y cells against 6-OHDA cytotoxicity by increasing autophagy flux and decreasing oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction, and was superior to liraglutide at the same dose in most parameters measured (PMID: 35873704).
- Semaglutide combined with neural stem cell transplantation improved motor function in 6-OHDA PD mice, increased transplanted NSC survival (mean fluorescence photon flux 3.258 × 10⁻¹¹ vs. 0.8192 × 10⁻¹¹ for NSCs alone), reduced C3+ reactive astrocytes in the striatum, and promoted NSC differentiation (PMID: 40874950).
- In a rotenone-induced PD model in diabetic rats, semaglutide significantly lowered blood glucose, HOMA-IR, and oxidative-inflammatory stress biomarkers, enhanced brain antioxidant status, improved cognitive, locomotor, and olfactory functions, and downregulated caspase 3 and GFAP while upregulating NRF2 protein expression (PMID: 40088335).
- A review of preclinical studies reported that semaglutide demonstrated neuroprotective effects in animal models of PD through modulation of neuroinflammation, enhancement of mitochondrial function, promotion of neurogenesis, improvement of motor function, and amelioration of dopaminergic neuronal loss (PMID: 38921025).

### Eye Disorder

#### Literature — Eye Disorder

**Evidence strength:** weak, supports
**Relevant studies:** 5

Across multiple preclinical models spanning diabetic retinopathy, glaucoma, and dry eye disease, semaglutide consistently demonstrates protective effects on ocular tissues. Semaglutide attenuated retinal vascular permeability and basement membrane thickening in diabetic retinopathy models (PMID: 40749340, PMID: 40931416), protected retinal ganglion cells from metabolic insult (PMID: 41665299), and restored lacrimal gland structure and function in age-related dry eye disease (PMID: 41812831). The one mixed finding—showing vascular and astrocytic benefits but no improvement in retinal ganglion cell survival in an ocular hypertension model (PMID: 40597179)—does not offset the otherwise consistent pattern of ocular benefit across diverse disease contexts.

**Key findings:**
- Semaglutide pretreatment prevented rotenone-induced retinal ganglion cell loss in mice and enhanced glucose metabolism, suggesting neuroprotective potential in glaucoma (PMID: 41665299).
- Long-term semaglutide treatment significantly alleviated dry eye disease phenotypes and preserved lacrimal gland structural integrity in aged mice, reducing senescent cell burden and senescence-associated inflammatory programs (PMID: 41812831).
- Semaglutide reversed retinal basement membrane thickening and vascular leakage in diabetic mice by upregulating endothelial AnxA2 and activating MMP-9-dependent extracellular matrix remodeling, an effect not replicated by insulin (PMID: 40749340).
- Semaglutide significantly reduced blood glucose, improved retinal function, alleviated neuronal apoptosis, reduced vascular lesions, and restored tight junction marker ZO-1 expression in a streptozotocin-induced diabetic retinopathy rat model (PMID: 40931416).
- In an ocular hypertension rat model, semaglutide delayed IOP elevation and improved astrocytic fractal dimension and vascular remodeling, but did not improve retinal ganglion cell survival, yielding a mixed outcome for glaucoma neuroprotection (PMID: 40597179).

### Hyperglycemia

#### Literature — Hyperglycemia

**Evidence strength:** moderate, supports
**Relevant studies:** 3

The evidence supports the use of semaglutide for treating hyperglycemia in non-approved populations. In a randomized controlled trial of antipsychotic-treated patients with schizophrenia and prediabetes, semaglutide reduced HbA1c by 0.46% and achieved normoglycemia (HbA1c <5.7%) in 81% of treated patients versus 19% of placebo patients (PMID: 40900607). A systematic review and meta-analysis found that semaglutide (2.4 mg weekly) significantly restored normoglycemia in patients with prediabetes and obesity (OR 4.87, 95% CI 2.61–9.09, p<0.00001) (PMID: 41398448). An open-label controlled case series in individuals with spinal cord injury and abnormal carbohydrate metabolism showed reductions in fasting plasma glucose and HbA1c after 26 weeks of semaglutide (PMID: 37158751).

**Key findings:**
- In a double-blind RCT of SGA-treated patients with schizophrenia, prediabetes, and obesity, semaglutide reduced HbA1c by 0.46% (95% CI -0.56% to -0.36%) versus placebo over 30 weeks, and 81% of semaglutide-treated patients achieved HbA1c <5.7% versus 19% of placebo-treated patients (PMID: 40900607).
- A meta-analysis of 8 RCTs (14,564 participants with prediabetes and obesity) found semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly significantly restored normoglycemia compared to placebo (OR 4.87, 95% CI 2.61–9.09; p<0.00001) (PMID: 41398448).
- In an open-label controlled case series of individuals with spinal cord injury and abnormal carbohydrate metabolism, 26 weeks of semaglutide reduced fasting plasma glucose by an average of 17 mg/dL and HbA1c by 0.2%, while control participants showed increases in both measures (PMID: 37158751).
- Semaglutide improved high-density cholesterol by 10.81 mg/dL and triglycerides by -29.20 mg/dL alongside glycemic improvements in schizophrenia patients with prediabetes (PMID: 40900607).
- In the meta-analysis, both semaglutide and liraglutide were effective at restoring normoglycemia, but exenatide was not; semaglutide was more effective in men (PMID: 41398448).

**Supporting PMIDs:** [37158751](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37158751/), [40900607](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40900607/), [41398448](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41398448/)

### Alzheimer Disease

#### Literature — Alzheimer Disease

**Evidence strength:** strong, contradicts
**Relevant studies:** 18

The highest-quality evidence—two large, well-conducted phase 3 randomised controlled trials (evoke and evoke+, n=3808)—found that oral semaglutide 14 mg did not slow clinical progression of early Alzheimer's disease compared with placebo on the primary CDR-SB endpoint (PMID: 41865758, PMID: 42159155). Supporting evidence consists predominantly of preclinical animal studies and observational/real-world data in diabetic populations without established AD, which cannot offset the direct negative RCT evidence for treating symptomatic Alzheimer's disease (PMID: 39445596, PMID: 40552638, PMID: 37730113); the prevailing interpretation across multiple reviews is that semaglutide may have a prevention-relevant metabolic role but lacks efficacy as a treatment for established amyloid-confirmed AD (PMID: 42325226, PMID: 42785112).

**Key findings:**
- In the evoke trial (n=1855), mean CDR-SB change from baseline to week 104 was 2.3 with semaglutide vs 2.3 with placebo (estimated difference −0.08, 95% CI −0.35 to 0.20, p=0.57); in evoke+ (n=1953) the difference was 0.10 (95% CI −0.17 to 0.38, p=0.46), and both trials were discontinued due to negative clinical outcomes (PMID: 41865758).
- A plain-language summary of the evoke(+) studies confirmed that oral semaglutide up to 14 mg once daily did not slow cognitive or functional decline in early Alzheimer's disease and is not effective as a treatment for early-stage AD (PMID: 42159155).
- A narrative review concluded that the negative evoke/evoke+ outcomes represent a translational paradox and proposed that semaglutide's value may lie in upstream metabolic/inflammatory prevention rather than reversing established amyloid-driven AD pathology, a hypothesis requiring prospective validation (PMID: 42325226).
- Analysis of the evoke(+) trials noted that semaglutide produced a robust reduction in hsCRP and nominally significant CSF biomarker changes (YKL-40, GFAP reduced 7–10%), but these did not translate into clinical benefit, and the discrepancy between real-world and trial data suggests treating symptomatic AD differs from reducing dementia risk in cognitively unimpaired diabetic patients (PMID: 42785112).
- A post-hoc re-analysis of evoke/evoke+ found a statistically significant difference on the ADCS-ADL-MCI test at week 130 (p=0.0039) and trends on ADAS-cog-13 at week 156, but these were secondary, unplanned analyses at time points after the pre-specified primary endpoint was already negative (PMID: 42261705).
- In 5xFAD mice treated preventatively with semaglutide for 2 or 4 months, neither memory/learning tasks, amyloid-β plaque deposition, nor glial cell activation were measurably improved, and incretin pre-treatment did not alter microglial activation after LPS challenge (PMID: 42413499).
- A systematic review and meta-analysis of five studies on brain/cognitive outcomes reported that semaglutide lowered AD risk compared with various treatments, but noted high heterogeneity across studies and that the evidence base was dominated by observational and preclinical data (PMID: 41766347).
- Target trial emulation using US EHR data (n=1,094,761 T2DM patients) found semaglutide associated with 40–70% reduced hazard of first-time AD diagnosis vs other antidiabetic medications, but this was in cognitively unimpaired diabetic patients, not in those with established AD (PMID: 39445596).
- A second real-world target trial emulation (n=1,710,995 T2DM patients) found semaglutide associated with reduced overall ADRD incidence (HR 0.54–0.80 vs comparators), with significant reduction in vascular dementia but no evidence of association with frontotemporal or Lewy body dementias (PMID: 40552638).
- In APP/PS1 transgenic mice, semaglutide improved cognitive performance, reduced Aβ deposition, attenuated NLRP3/NF-κB neuroinflammatory signaling, and improved BBB integrity markers, but these are preclinical findings that did not translate to clinical benefit in RCTs (PMID: 42126676).
- In 3xTg AD mice, semaglutide increased SIRT1 and GLUT4 expression in the hippocampus, improved learning and memory, and decreased Aβ plaques and neurofibrillary tangles via the GLP-1R/SIRT1/GLUT4 pathway, representing mechanistic preclinical evidence only (PMID: 37730113).
- In APP/PS1 mice, semaglutide suppressed microglial NLRP3 inflammasome activation, promoted M2 polarization, and alleviated ferroptosis via NLRP3/NF-κB and Nrf2/HO-1/xCT/GPX4 pathways, providing preclinical mechanistic support that has not translated to clinical efficacy (PMID: 41173224).
- In APP/PS1 mice and human AD brain organoids, semaglutide reduced Aβ, p-Tau, and GFAP and upregulated oxytocin, demonstrating preclinical therapeutic potential that remains unconfirmed in clinical trials (PMID: 39405916).
- In 5xFAD mice on high-fat diet, semaglutide improved metabolic parameters, reduced Aβ40/42, and enhanced spatial memory, but WT mice on regular diet showed a modest cognitive decline with semaglutide, and benefits were context-specific to metabolic dysfunction (PMID: 42353032).
- In human forebrain organoids under cholesterol overload, semaglutide protected neural cells from lipotoxic injury via cAMP-PKA and PI3K-Akt-mTOR pathways and stabilized calcium activity, representing in vitro mechanistic evidence without clinical translation (PMID: 42805933).
- A review of neuroinflammation-targeting with semaglutide acknowledged that while preclinical and biomarker data are promising, Phase 3 trials in early AD did not meet primary cognitive endpoints despite favorable biomarker modulation, and limited BBB penetration remains a key translational challenge (PMID: 41504939).

### Schizophrenia

#### Literature — Schizophrenia

**Evidence strength:** strong, contradicts
**Relevant studies:** 3

The best available evidence from a double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT (PMID: 40900607) shows that semaglutide did not improve schizophrenia symptoms as measured by PANSS-6, and had no significant effect on mental quality of life, though it did improve metabolic outcomes and physical QoL. A secondary analysis of the same trial (PMID: 41702353) confirmed no meaningful effect on PANSS-6 or mental component QoL scores, with the mixed verdict at the paper level reflecting improvement in physical but not psychiatric outcomes. Taken together, the controlled evidence does not support semaglutide as an effective treatment for the core psychiatric symptoms of schizophrenia.

**Key findings:**
- In a 30-week double-blind RCT of 154 patients, semaglutide had no significant effect on PANSS-6 schizophrenia symptom scores compared to placebo (PMID: 40900607).
- Semaglutide significantly reduced HbA1c by 0.46% and body weight by 9.21 kg, and improved physical QoL by 3.75 points on the SF-36v2, but did not improve mental QoL scores (PMID: 40900607).
- Secondary causal mediation analyses of the same RCT found no total or mediated effects of semaglutide on mental component summary scores or PANSS-6, confirming the absence of a psychiatric symptom benefit (PMID: 41702353).
- Semaglutide improved physical QoL with effect sizes exceeding the minimally important difference, but this benefit was not accompanied by any meaningful improvement in mental QoL or schizophrenia symptom severity (PMID: 41702353).

It is unknown whether WEGOVY causes thyroid C-cell tumors, including medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC), in humans as human relevance of semaglutide-induced rodent thyroid C-cell tumors has not been determined [see Warnings and Precautions ( 5.1 ), Nonclinical Toxicology ( 13.1 )] . • WEGOVY is contraindicated in patients with a personal or family history of MTC or in patients with Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2) [see Contraindications ( 4 )] . Counsel patients regarding the potential risk for MTC with the use of WEGOVY and inform them of symptoms of thyroid tumors (e.g., a mass in the neck, dysphagia, dyspnea, persistent hoarseness). Routine monitoring of serum calcitonin or using thyroid ultrasound is of uncertain value for early detection of MTC in patients treated with WEGOVY [see Contraindications ( 4 ), Warnings and Precautions ( 5.1 )] .
