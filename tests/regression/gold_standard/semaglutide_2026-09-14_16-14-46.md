## Drug Safety

_Drug-wide safety signal — applies to the drug generally, not to any single candidate indication below._

**Regulatory label:** FDA label boxed-warning text (5 product labels, LLM-summarized — see appendix for verbatim text):
Across all five labels, the shared core boxed warning is that semaglutide causes dose-dependent and treatment-duration-dependent thyroid C-cell tumors in rodents at clinically relevant exposures; whether this risk extends to humans, including medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC), has not been determined. All labels contraindicate semaglutide in patients with a personal or family history of MTC or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2), and all direct clinicians to counsel patients on MTC risk and symptoms such as a neck mass, dysphagia, dyspnea, or persistent hoarseness. The Ozempic/OZEMPIC and Wegovy labels uniformly state that routine monitoring of serum calcitonin or thyroid ultrasound is of uncertain value for early MTC detection, a statement absent from the abbreviated highlights sections but present in the full boxed-warning text of all products. One substantive difference exists: the first OZEMPIC label (which also references RYBELSUS throughout) explicitly extends the warning and contraindication to cover RYBELSUS (the oral tablet formulation of semaglutide) in addition to OZEMPIC, whereas all other labels apply the warning solely to their respective named product; this is a clinically meaningful scope difference, not merely a wording variation.

Open Targets warning metadata: warning type: Black Box Warning; toxicity categories: carcinogenicity.

## Findings by Disease

### Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

#### Literature — Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 5

Multiple randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled and crossover trials demonstrate that semaglutide improves glycemic control, reduces body weight, and lowers insulin requirements in adults with type 1 diabetes. In the ADJUST-T1D trial, 36% of semaglutide-treated patients versus 0% of placebo patients achieved the composite endpoint of time-in-range >70%, time-below-range <4%, and ≥5% weight reduction (PMID: 40550013). A crossover trial confirmed that semaglutide increased time in target glucose range by a mean 4.8 percentage points versus placebo without increasing hypoglycemia (PMID: 39794615). The evidence supports repurposing semaglutide for type 1 diabetes, though safety considerations including gastrointestinal side effects and rare euglycemic ketosis require monitoring (PMID: 39794615, PMID: 41175929).

**Key findings:**
- In a 26-week double-blind RCT (ADJUST-T1D), 36% of adults with type 1 diabetes and obesity receiving semaglutide achieved the composite primary endpoint (time-in-range >70%, time-below-range <4%, and ≥5% weight reduction) versus 0% in the placebo group (between-group difference 36 percentage points, 95% CI 20.6–52.2, P<0.001) (PMID: 40550013).
- Semaglutide reduced HbA1c by an additional 0.3 percentage points and body weight by 8.8 kg compared to placebo over 26 weeks in adults with type 1 diabetes and obesity (PMID: 40550013).
- In a randomized double-blind crossover trial, semaglutide increased time in target glucose range (3.9–10.0 mmol/L) by a mean 4.8 percentage points versus placebo (P=0.006) without increasing time below 3.9 mmol/L (P=0.19) in adults with type 1 diabetes (PMID: 39794615).
- Two episodes of recurrent euglycemic ketosis without acidosis occurred during semaglutide use in the crossover trial, and no diabetic ketoacidosis or severe hypoglycemia was observed (PMID: 39794615).
- Post-hoc analysis of ADJUST-T1D showed semaglutide produced a significant 22.6% reduction in total daily insulin dose over 26 weeks, driven more by bolus (-30.5%) than basal (-15.6%) insulin reductions, with early effects largely independent of weight loss (PMID: 41429002).
- In a post-hoc analysis of a crossover trial, daily total and bolus insulin and carbohydrate input were significantly reduced by day 7 of semaglutide initiation in adults with type 1 diabetes on insulin pump therapy, while basal insulin was significantly reduced by day 32 (PMID: 41144928).
- In qualitative analyses from a randomized crossover trial, 42% of participants with type 1 diabetes expressed interest in continuing semaglutide outside the study, citing lower insulin requirements, weight loss, and improved glycemic management, while the Diabetes Bowel Symptom Questionnaire showed increased gastrointestinal symptom frequency and severity with semaglutide versus placebo (PMID: 41175929).

### Stroke

#### Literature — Stroke

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 5

The preponderance of evidence supports semaglutide for stroke prevention and treatment. Pooled post-hoc analysis of two randomized cardiovascular outcome trials showed semaglutide significantly reduced first stroke incidence (HR 0.68, p=0.048) versus placebo in type 2 diabetes patients at high cardiovascular risk (PMID: 35582947), and large cardiovascular outcome trials consistently demonstrated reductions in composite MACE endpoints including nonfatal stroke across diverse populations (PMID: 39211948, PMID: 41928037). A phase 2 randomized trial in acute large vessel occlusion showed no significant overall benefit on neurological recovery but suggested possible benefit in patients not receiving thrombolysis, yielding a mixed signal at the acute treatment level (PMID: 41392086), while preclinical data further support neuroprotective mechanisms (PMID: 31465784).

**Key findings:**
- Semaglutide reduced incidence of any first stroke versus placebo (0.8 vs 1.1 events/100 PYO; HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.46–1.00, p=0.048) in pooled post-hoc analysis of SUSTAIN 6 and PIONEER 6 randomized trials, driven primarily by reduction in small-vessel occlusion (HR 0.51, p=0.017) (PMID: 35582947).
- In the FLOW trial, semaglutide reduced the composite of CV death, nonfatal MI, or nonfatal stroke by 18% (HR 0.82, 95% CI 0.68–0.98, p=0.03) in patients with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease, consistently across CKD severity subgroups (PMID: 39211948).
- In the SELECT trial prespecified analysis, semaglutide reduced MACE (including nonfatal stroke) by 26% (HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.63–0.88, p=0.0004) in patients at high risk for liver fibrosis, consistent with the overall SELECT population benefit (PMID: 41928037).
- A phase 2 randomized trial in acute large vessel occlusion patients undergoing endovascular therapy found no significant difference in favorable neurological recovery at 90 days overall (56.5% vs 54.9%; adjusted RR 1.05, p=0.37), though an exploratory subgroup not receiving IV thrombolysis showed a potentially favorable signal (adjusted RR 1.18, 95% CI 1.02–1.36) (PMID: 41392086).
- In a rat middle cerebral artery occlusion model, semaglutide significantly reduced infarct size, neurological impairment scores, neuronal loss in hippocampal regions, neuroinflammation markers, apoptosis signaling, and normalized neurogenesis, supporting neuroprotective mechanisms (PMID: 31465784).

### Nicotine Dependence

#### Literature — Nicotine Dependence

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 1

The available evidence from a single phase 2a randomized clinical trial is mixed: semaglutide did not significantly improve its co-primary outcomes of laboratory smoking resistance or weekly cigarettes per day, yet it did significantly reduce nicotine craving and body weight (PMID: 42189538). Supplementary analyses suggested a potential reduction in laboratory smoking behavior, but these were not the pre-specified primary endpoints, limiting their interpretive weight (PMID: 42189538). Overall, the evidence neither clearly supports nor contradicts semaglutide's efficacy for nicotine dependence, warranting larger confirmatory trials.

**Key findings:**
- Co-primary outcomes of laboratory smoking resistance (β = 0.16, P = .16) and number of cigarettes self-administered (β = -0.08, P = .30) were not significantly improved by semaglutide vs. placebo (PMID: 42189538).
- Semaglutide significantly reduced cigarette craving over the treatment period (treatment-by-time interaction: β = -0.11, P = .01) (PMID: 42189538).
- Semaglutide significantly reduced body weight compared to placebo (β = -0.04, P < .001) (PMID: 42189538).
- Supplementary change score analyses showed significantly greater reductions in laboratory smoking in the semaglutide group (β = -0.69, P = .02, d = 0.67), though these were not pre-specified primary endpoints (PMID: 42189538).
- The trial was small (12 participants per arm) and enrolled non-treatment-seeking smokers, limiting generalizability to cessation-motivated populations (PMID: 42189538).

### Heart Failure

#### Literature — Heart Failure

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 11

Across multiple randomized controlled trials and pooled analyses, semaglutide consistently improved heart failure-related symptoms, physical limitations, exercise function, and body weight compared to placebo in patients with obesity-related HFpEF, with and without type 2 diabetes (PMIDs: 38587233, 37622681, 38599221). - In the FLOW trial (n=3533, T2D and CKD), semaglutide reduced the composite of HF events or cardiovascular death (HR 0.73; P=0.0005), HF events alone (HR 0.73; P=0.0068), and cardiovascular death alone (HR 0.71; P=0.0036), with consistent effects regardless of baseline HF history (PMID: 39217553).

- In a prespecified pooled subanalysis of STEP-HFpEF and STEP-HFpEF DM (n=1145), semaglutide improved KCCQ-CSS and body weight consistently across age groups (<55, 55–64, 65–74, ≥75 years), with no significant treatment-by-age interaction (p-interaction=0.80 and 0.41, respectively) (PMID: 41290376).

### Myocardial Infarction

#### Literature — Myocardial Infarction

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 2

The evidence for repurposing semaglutide to treat or prevent myocardial infarction is mixed. In SUSTAIN-6, subcutaneous semaglutide reduced the composite MACE endpoint significantly, but the reduction in nonfatal MI specifically (2.9% vs 3.9%, HR 0.74) did not reach statistical significance (P=0.12) (PMID: 27633186). In PIONEER 6, oral semaglutide showed a numerically higher rate of nonfatal MI compared to placebo (2.3% vs 1.9%, HR 1.18), though this was also not statistically significant (PMID: 31185157), creating directional inconsistency across the two trials for the MI-specific endpoint.

**Key findings:**
- In SUSTAIN-6, nonfatal MI occurred in 2.9% of semaglutide patients vs 3.9% of placebo patients (HR 0.74; 95% CI 0.51–1.08; P=0.12), a numerically favorable but statistically non-significant result (PMID: 27633186).
- In PIONEER 6, nonfatal MI occurred in 2.3% of oral semaglutide patients vs 1.9% of placebo patients (HR 1.18; 95% CI 0.73–1.90), a numerically unfavorable and statistically non-significant result (PMID: 31185157).
- The overall MACE composite was significantly reduced with subcutaneous semaglutide in SUSTAIN-6 (HR 0.74; 95% CI 0.58–0.95; P<0.001 for noninferiority, with superiority achieved), but oral semaglutide in PIONEER 6 only demonstrated noninferiority (HR 0.79; 95% CI 0.57–1.11) (PMID: 27633186; PMID: 31185157).
- Both trials were conducted in high cardiovascular risk patients with type 2 diabetes, limiting generalizability to a broader MI population, and neither was powered or designed specifically to assess MI as a primary endpoint (PMID: 27633186; PMID: 31185157).

### Eye Disorder

#### Literature — Eye Disorder

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 3

The evidence is mixed: preclinical studies support semaglutide's potential therapeutic benefit for retinal ganglion cell protection in glaucoma models (PMID: 41665299) and age-related dry eye disease via lacrimal gland restoration (PMID: 41812831), but the highest-quality clinical evidence from a large systematic review and meta-analysis of 78 trials (n=73,640) found no benefit for eye disorders or diabetic retinopathy overall, and identified a significant association with increased risk of NAION (PMID: 40810985). The clinical evidence thus raises safety concerns while the preclinical evidence suggests mechanistic therapeutic potential, creating a genuinely mixed picture across different eye conditions and levels of evidence.

**Key findings:**
- A systematic review and meta-analysis of 78 RCTs (n=73,640) found semaglutide did not increase or reduce the risk of eye disorders (OR 1.01; 95% CI 0.91–1.12) or diabetic retinopathy (OR 1.04; 95% CI 0.92–1.17) (PMID: 40810985).
- The same meta-analysis found semaglutide was associated with a significantly increased odds of nonarteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (NAION) (OR 3.92; 95% CI 1.02–15.02), though evidence was insufficient for definitive conclusions due to limited sample size for this outcome (PMID: 40810985).
- In a mouse model, semaglutide pretreatment prevented rotenone-induced retinal ganglion cell loss and enhanced glucose metabolism, suggesting a potential neuroprotective role in glaucoma (PMID: 41665299).
- In a naturally aged mouse model, long-term semaglutide treatment significantly alleviated dry eye disease phenotypes, preserved lacrimal gland structural integrity, reduced senescent cell burden, and attenuated senescence-associated inflammatory programs (PMID: 41812831).

### Smoking Cessation

#### Literature — Smoking Cessation

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 1

The available evidence from a single phase 2a randomized clinical trial is mixed: semaglutide did not significantly improve the co-primary outcomes of laboratory smoking resistance or weekly cigarettes per day, yet it did significantly reduce nicotine craving and body weight (PMID: 42189538). Supplementary analyses suggested a potential reduction in laboratory smoking behavior, but these were not the pre-specified primary endpoints and the trial was small and conducted in non-treatment-seeking smokers (PMID: 42189538). The authors conclude that larger, adequately powered trials are needed before definitive conclusions about semaglutide's efficacy for smoking cessation can be drawn (PMID: 42189538).

**Key findings:**
- Semaglutide did not significantly improve the co-primary outcomes of laboratory smoking resistance (β = 0.16, 95% CI −0.07 to 0.40, P = .16) or number of cigarettes self-administered (β = −0.08, 95% CI −0.25 to 0.08, P = .30) compared to placebo (PMID: 42189538).
- Supplementary change score analyses showed significantly greater reductions in laboratory smoking in the semaglutide group vs. placebo (β = −0.69, 95% CI −1.26 to −0.13, P = .02, d = 0.67), though this was not a pre-specified primary endpoint (PMID: 42189538).
- Semaglutide significantly reduced cigarette craving over the treatment period (treatment-by-time interaction: β = −0.11, 95% CI −0.20 to −0.03, P = .01) (PMID: 42189538).
- Semaglutide significantly reduced body weight compared to placebo (β = −0.04, 95% CI −0.05 to −0.03, P < .001), suggesting cardiometabolic benefit in this population (PMID: 42189538).
- The trial was small (n = 24 randomized, 21 completing primary assessment) and enrolled non-treatment-seeking smokers, limiting generalizability to cessation-motivated populations (PMID: 42189538).

### Alzheimer Disease

#### Literature — Alzheimer Disease

**Evidence strength:** strong, contradicts
**Relevant studies:** 7

The highest-quality evidence—two large phase 3 randomised controlled trials (evoke and evoke+, n=3808)—found that oral semaglutide did not slow clinical progression in early Alzheimer's disease, with no significant difference in CDR-SB scores versus placebo at week 104 (PMID: 41865758). Preclinical evidence from the 5xFAD mouse model similarly showed no effect of semaglutide on memory, amyloid-β plaque deposition, or glial activation (PMID: 42413499). Positive signals from rodent mechanistic studies (PMID: 37730113, PMID: 42126676) and a post-hoc reanalysis suggesting limited effects at later time points (PMID: 42261705) do not offset the consistent null findings from controlled clinical evidence.

**Key findings:**
- In the evoke trial (n=1855), mean CDR-SB change from baseline to week 104 was 2.3 with semaglutide vs. 2.3 with placebo (estimated difference -0.08, 95% CI -0.35 to 0.20, p=0.57); in evoke+ (n=1953), the difference was 0.10 (95% CI -0.17 to 0.38, p=0.46), leading to trial discontinuation due to negative clinical outcome (PMID: 41865758).
- Chronic semaglutide treatment in 5xFAD mice, even when initiated before overt pathology, produced no measurable effects on memory, learning, amyloid-β plaque deposition, or glial cell activation (PMID: 42413499).
- A post-hoc reanalysis of evoke/evoke+ data found a statistically significant difference on the ADCS-ADL-MCI test at week 130 (p=0.0039) and trends on ADAS-cog-13 at week 156, but these were not pre-specified primary endpoints and the overall trial was declared negative (PMID: 42261705).
- A systematic review and meta-analysis found that semaglutide demonstrated neuroprotective effects and lowered risk of AD compared to various treatments, but noted high heterogeneity across available studies (PMID: 41766347).
- In 3xTg mice, semaglutide increased SIRT1 and GLUT4 expression in the hippocampus, improved learning and memory, and decreased Aβ plaques and neurofibrillary tangles via the GLP-1R/SIRT1/GLUT4 pathway (PMID: 37730113).
- In APP/PS1 mice, semaglutide improved cognitive performance, reduced Aβ deposition, attenuated neuroinflammatory signaling (NLRP3, TLR4/NF-κB), and improved blood-brain barrier integrity markers (PMID: 42126676).

Ozempic (effective 20260730):
WARNING: RISK OF THYROID C-CELL TUMORS • In rodents, semaglutide causes dose-dependent and treatment-duration-dependent thyroid C-cell tumors at clinically relevant exposures. It is unknown whether OZEMPIC causes thyroid C-cell tumors, including medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC), in humans as human relevance of semaglutide-induced rodent thyroid C-cell tumors has not been determined [see Warnings and Precautions ( 5.1 ), Nonclinical Toxicology ( 13.1 )] . • OZEMPIC is contraindicated in patients with a personal or family history of MTC or in patients with Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2) [see Contraindications ( 4 )] . Counsel patients regarding the potential risk for MTC with the use of OZEMPIC and inform them of symptoms of thyroid tumors (e.g., a mass in the neck, dysphagia, dyspnea, persistent hoarseness). Routine monitoring of serum calcitonin or using thyroid ultrasound is of uncertain value for early detection of MTC in patients treated with OZEMPIC [see Contraindications ( 4 ), Warnings and Precautions ( 5.1 )] . It is unknown whether OZEMPIC causes thyroid C-cell tumors, including medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC), in humans as the human relevance of semaglutide-induced rodent thyroid C-cell tumors has not been determined ( 5.1 , 13.1 ). • OZEMPIC is contraindicated in patients with a personal or family history of MTC or in patients with Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2). Counsel patients regarding the potential risk of MTC and symptoms of thyroid tumors ( 4 , 5.1 ).
