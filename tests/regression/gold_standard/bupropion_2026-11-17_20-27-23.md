## Findings by Disease

### Attention Deficit-hyperactivity Disorder

#### Literature — Attention Deficit-hyperactivity Disorder

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 13

The preponderance of evidence from multiple randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials supports bupropion as an effective treatment for ADHD across age groups and formulations. Multiple controlled trials in both adults and children demonstrated statistically significant superiority of bupropion over placebo (PMIDs: 11156812, 8885585, 15820237, 25325205), and a Bayesian network meta-analysis confirmed bupropion's significant reduction in ADHD rating scale scores compared to placebo with high certainty of evidence (PMID: 38950507). The two contradicting studies are limited by small samples, lack of statistical power, and a highly specific comorbid population (methadone-maintained patients), which do not offset the consistent positive findings from the broader controlled evidence base (PMIDs: 11791949, 16102908).

**Key findings:**
- A double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomized 6-week trial in adults found bupropion SR produced a 42% reduction in ADHD symptoms vs. 24% for placebo, with 76% of bupropion-treated patients responding vs. 37% on placebo (PMID: 11156812).
- A multisite, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in children aged 6–12 found significant treatment effects for bupropion on conduct problems and hyperactivity on teacher and parent Conners scales and on cognitive performance measures (PMID: 8885585).
- A multisite, placebo-controlled 8-week trial of bupropion XL in 162 adults with ADHD found 53% of bupropion XL patients responded vs. 31% on placebo (p=.004), with an effect size of 0.6 on the ADHD Rating Scale (PMID: 15820237).
- A double-blind, crossover trial in youth aged 7–17 found bupropion and methylphenidate produced equivalent and significantly greater improvement than baseline on teacher and parent ratings, CGI, and cognitive measures (PMID: 7775360).
- A 6-week double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 42 adults found a significant difference in CAARS scores favoring bupropion over placebo at week 6 (PMID: 25325205).
- A Bayesian network meta-analysis of 31 clinical trials found bupropion produced a significant reduction in ADHD rating scale scores compared to placebo (SMD: 0.33; 95% CrI: 0.60, -0.059), with high certainty of evidence, and its efficacy was not significantly different from methylphenidate (PMID: 38950507).
- A randomized, double-blind, parallel-design study comparing bupropion SR, methylphenidate, and placebo in 30 adults found response rates of 64%, 50%, and 27% respectively, but the difference did not reach statistical significance (p=0.14), constituting a negative controlled trial (PMID: 11791949).
- A 12-week double-blind trial in 98 methadone-maintained patients with ADHD found no significant differences in ADHD symptom reduction between sustained-release methylphenidate, sustained-release bupropion, and placebo, with a high placebo response rate of 46% (PMID: 16102908).
- An open trial of bupropion in 19 adults with ADHD found 14 of 19 experienced moderate to marked benefit, with 10 choosing to continue bupropion over their prior medication (PMID: 2115746).
- A 12-week single-blind trial in 11 patients with comorbid cocaine dependence and adult ADHD found significant reductions in attention difficulties, hyperactivity, and impulsivity with bupropion, with improvements comparable to a historical methylphenidate cohort (PMID: 11916368).
- A 5-week open-label trial in 13 adolescent boys with ADHD, conduct disorder, and substance use disorders found significant declines in Conners Hyperactivity Index scores and CGI-S ratings with bupropion (PMID: 9847499).
- A large retrospective cohort study found bupropion was prescribed at 3.75 times the rate in ADHD+MDD patients vs. ADHD-only patients, and bupropion demonstrated clinical outcomes broadly comparable to CNS stimulants for suicidality and mood stabilizer initiation in the ADHD+MDD population (PMID: 42144137).

### Bipolar Disorder

#### Literature — Bipolar Disorder

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 11

The preponderance of evidence from controlled trials, randomized studies, and large observational data supports bupropion as an efficacious treatment for bipolar depression. Multiple double-blind and randomized trials demonstrate significant antidepressant efficacy for bupropion in bipolar depression (PMIDs: 7929019, 10701483, 16880481, 37122643), and a large retrospective study found bupropion monotherapy was associated with significantly lower risk of psychiatric hospitalization than lithium in bipolar disorder (PMID: 29920885). While two smaller uncontrolled reports raised concerns about mood switching (PMIDs: 1487473, 16449476), the controlled evidence consistently shows bupropion carries lower switch risk than comparators such as venlafaxine and desipramine, supporting its relative safety and efficacy in this population.

**Key findings:**
- In a double-blind trial, bupropion and desipramine showed similar antidepressant efficacy in bipolar depression, but mania/hypomania occurred in only 1/9 bupropion-treated patients versus 5/10 desipramine-treated patients (PMID: 7929019).
- In a randomized 10-week trial of 174 bipolar depressed patients, bupropion showed acute response rates of 49-53%, comparable to sertraline and venlafaxine, with significantly lower switch risk than venlafaxine (PMID: 16880481).
- A single-blind randomized study found bupropion SR produced a 59% response rate in bipolar I/II depression with no cases of affective switch (PMID: 12180276).
- A double-blind comparison found both idazoxan and bupropion produced approximately 50% reductions in Hamilton Depression scores in bipolar I depressed patients (PMID: 10701483).
- A large retrospective study of 190,894 individuals found bupropion monotherapy was associated with significantly lower risk of psychiatric hospitalization than lithium (RR=0.80, P=2.80×10⁻⁴) in bipolar disorder (PMID: 29920885).
- A randomized triple-blind trial in bipolar II depression found bupropion and venlafaxine produced equivalent, significant reductions in HDRS scores after 4 weeks (PMID: 37122643).
- An open trial found most bipolar/schizoaffective patients maintained on bupropion alone or with low-dose adjuncts for 1 year or more showed little or no relapse (PMID: 2856918).
- A continuation trial found bupropion had the lowest ratio of threshold mood switches (hypomania/mania) among three antidepressants studied, compared to venlafaxine which had the highest switch risk (PMID: 16449476).
- An uncontrolled case series of 11 bipolar patients found 7/11 had moderate-to-marked improvement with bupropion, but 6/11 experienced manic or hypomanic episodes requiring discontinuation, suggesting caution (PMID: 1487473).

### Cocaine Dependence

#### Literature — Cocaine Dependence

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 7

The evidence for bupropion in cocaine dependence is mixed, with the highest-quality controlled trials generally failing to show a significant overall benefit. The largest RCTs found no statistically significant difference between bupropion and placebo on primary cocaine use outcomes (PMID: 18551884; PMID: 36920397), while one controlled trial suggested benefit only in a specific subgroup (abstinence initiation) and in combination with contingency management (PMID: 16461866; PMID: 36920397). Positive signals come from a small uncontrolled case series in ADHD-comorbid patients (PMID: 11916368) and preclinical rat data (PMID: 39881654), which are insufficient to offset the null findings from controlled trials.

**Key findings:**
- A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (N=70) found no statistically significant differences between bupropion and placebo in urine drug screen results, treatment retention, cocaine craving, or depressive symptoms when combined with cognitive behavioral therapy (PMID: 18551884).
- A 25-week placebo-controlled RCT in methadone-maintained patients found that combining contingency management with bupropion significantly reduced cocaine-positive samples during weeks 3–13, but bupropion alone (without CM) showed no significant improvement in cocaine use (PMID: 16461866).
- A 26-week double-blind RCT (N=80) found no significant overall difference in cocaine abstinence between bupropion SR and placebo; however, in the abstinence initiation subgroup, bupropion SR produced more cocaine-negative samples (30.3 vs. 17.1) and higher end-of-study abstinence rates (66.7% vs. 30.0%, P=.04) (PMID: 36920397).
- A small (N=8) within-subject placebo-controlled laboratory study found that acute bupropion pre-treatment reduced choice of high-dose cocaine versus money but also enhanced positive subjective effects of cocaine, yielding contradictory signals (PMID: 22168398).
- An uncontrolled 12-week single-blind trial (N=11) in cocaine-dependent adults with comorbid ADHD reported significant reductions in self-reported cocaine use, craving, and cocaine-positive toxicologies with bupropion, but lacked a placebo control (PMID: 11916368).
- In male and female Wistar rats, systemic bupropion at 60 mg/kg attenuated cocaine self-administration during extended-access sessions without altering locomotor activity or food intake, with greater efficacy in females during the first hour (PMID: 39881654).

### Neuropathic Pain

#### Literature — Neuropathic Pain

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 6

The totality of evidence supports repurposing bupropion for neuropathic pain. Two randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trials both demonstrated statistically significant pain reduction versus placebo (PMID: 11706096; PMID: 42956041), with the larger trial noting a modest but real effect and positioning bupropion as an alternative for patients intolerant of first-line agents (PMID: 42956041). Preclinical and mechanistic studies consistently corroborate these findings by showing antihyperalgesic effects mediated through spinal noradrenaline and dopamine pathways (PMID: 25427287) and reversal of pain-depressed behavior in rodent models (PMID: 26588213; PMID: 38958173).

**Key findings:**
- In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial of 41 nondepressed patients, bupropion SR reduced mean average pain scores by 1.7 points versus no change with placebo (p < 0.001), with 73% of patients reporting improved or much improved pain relief (PMID: 11706096).
- In a larger randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial of 67 patients with peripheral neuropathic pain, bupropion produced a statistically significant reduction in daily average pain of -0.5 NRS points versus placebo (95% CI -0.8 to -0.1, p = 0.005), though NNT for 50% pain reduction was 15, higher than first-line treatments (PMID: 42956041).
- Intrathecal bupropion produced dose-dependent antihyperalgesic effects in a rat spinal nerve ligation model, mediated through spinal α2-adrenoceptor and dopamine D2 receptor pathways, with microdialysis confirming increased noradrenaline and dopamine in the dorsal horn (PMID: 25427287).
- Bupropion reversed formalin-induced depression of intracranial self-stimulation and mechanical allodynia in rats, with effects sustained during repeated treatment, consistent with its clinical efficacy in neuropathic pain (PMID: 26588213).
- Co-administration of bupropion and citicoline produced additive anti-nociceptive effects in sciatic nerve-ligated mice, as measured by tail-flick and hot-plate latencies (PMID: 38958173).

**Supporting PMIDs:** [11706096](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11706096/), [42956041](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42956041/), [25427287](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25427287/), [38958173](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38958173/), [26588213](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26588213/)

### Alcohol Dependence

#### Literature — Alcohol Dependence

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 4

Evidence on bupropion monotherapy for alcohol use disorder is mixed. The only randomized controlled trial found that bupropion alone (Placebo + Bupropion arm) did not significantly reduce either primary outcome (B-PEth or %HDD) compared to placebo, as the statistical hierarchy was precluded after the bupropion-alone arm was not formally tested (PMID: 40487775). The sole preclinical study found that bupropion alone at two doses also failed to reduce voluntary alcohol intake in alcohol-preferring rats, though combination with naltrexone was effective (PMID: 29222923).

**Key findings:**
- In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n=384), the Placebo + Bupropion arm did not demonstrate statistically significant reductions in B-PEth or %HDD versus placebo, as the sequential hierarchical analysis was stopped before bupropion-alone comparisons could be made (PMID: 40487775).
- The combination of Varenicline + Bupropion reduced both B-PEth (Cohen's d=0.39, p=0.004) and %HDD (d=0.31, p=0.008) versus placebo, but the effect was not significantly different from Varenicline alone, making bupropion's independent contribution unclear (PMID: 40487775).
- Bupropion alone at doses of 10.0 mg/kg and 20.0 mg/kg failed to reduce voluntary alcohol intake in alcohol-preferring (P) rats, while the combination of low-dose naltrexone + bupropion significantly reduced alcohol consumption (PMID: 29222923).
- Bupropion reduced varenicline-induced nausea incidence (36/100 vs 49/96, p=0.048) and duration in the RCT, suggesting a tolerability benefit in combination but not a standalone efficacy signal for AUD (PMID: 40487775).

**Supporting PMIDs:** [40487775](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40487775/)

**Contradicting PMIDs:** [40487775](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40487775/), [29222923](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29222923/)

**Context (non-efficacy) PMIDs:** [31064683](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31064683/), [30817015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30817015/)

### Narcolepsy

#### Literature — Narcolepsy

**Evidence strength:** weak, supports
**Relevant studies:** 2

Two uncontrolled case reports describe bupropion improving narcolepsy-related symptoms including excessive daytime sleepiness, cataplexy, depression, and REM-sleep propensity in individual patients (PMID: 24930928; PMID: 9614600). In one case, objective sleep latency normalized and REM-sleep propensity reversed with bupropion, reverting upon discontinuation (PMID: 9614600). The evidence supports repurposing bupropion for narcolepsy, though it is limited to case-level observations.

**Key findings:**
- A 58-year-old woman with narcolepsy-cataplexy syndrome unresponsive to imipramine and modafinil became asymptomatic after 3 months on bupropion 300 mg/day, with Epworth Sleepiness Scale score improving from 14 at baseline (PMID: 24930928).
- A 46-year-old narcoleptic woman treated with bupropion 100 mg t.i.d. showed normalization of mood (BDI from 24 to 6), objective sleepiness (mean sleep latency from 1.8 to 9.1 minutes), and REM-sleep propensity, with all parameters reverting to pretreatment levels upon discontinuation (PMID: 9614600).
- The authors of PMID 9614600 proposed that bupropion's reversal of sleepiness and REM-sleep propensity in narcolepsy may be due to blockade of dopamine or norepinephrine reuptake, and called for further investigation in controlled trials.

### Stroke Disorder

**Evidence strength:** weak, supports
**Relevant studies:** 1

A single animal study found that bupropion exerted neuroprotective effects against cerebral ischemia/reperfusion injury in rats, reducing oxidative stress and inflammation and improving behavioral outcomes (PMID: 40066118). ### Binge Eating Disorder

#### Literature — Binge Eating Disorder

**Evidence strength:** moderate, contradicts
**Relevant studies:** 3

The evidence for bupropion in binge eating disorder is mixed. A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (PMID: 23656848) found that bupropion did not improve binge eating frequency, food craving, or eating disorder features relative to placebo, failing its primary endpoint for BED. A retrospective cohort study (PMID: 21927936) found that bupropion reduced binge frequency alongside depressive symptoms, though this was an uncontrolled comparison in depressed BED patients. An animal study (PMID: 41679557) found that bupropion alone and in combination suppressed binge-like intake of chocolate in rats, providing preclinical support for the hypothesis.

**Key findings:**
- In a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial of 61 overweight/obese women with BED, bupropion 300 mg/day for 8 weeks produced no significant improvement in binge-eating frequency, food craving, or eating disorder psychopathology compared to placebo, failing its primary BED endpoint (PMID: 23656848).
- Bupropion did produce significantly greater weight loss than placebo (1.8% vs 0.6% BMI loss; p=0.002) in overweight/obese women with BED, though this was a secondary outcome (PMID: 23656848).
- In a retrospective cohort study of depressed BED patients, bupropion 150 mg/day reduced binge frequency and anxious-depressive symptoms over 24 weeks, with greater weight loss than sertraline (PMID: 21927936).
- In a rat model of binge-like eating, bupropion alone and in combination with naltrexone suppressed binge-like intake of chocolate, suggesting preclinical activity against binge eating behavior (PMID: 41679557).

### Post-traumatic Stress Disorder

#### Literature — Post-traumatic Stress Disorder

**Evidence strength:** moderate, contradicts
**Relevant studies:** 5

The preponderance of evidence contradicts the repurposing of bupropion for PTSD. A placebo-controlled trial found no significant effect of bupropion SR on PTSD symptoms (PMID: 17414245), a systematic review and meta-analysis found bupropion failed to differentiate from placebo (PMID: 27126398), and a network meta-analysis found bupropion similar to placebo for sleep quality disturbances in PTSD (PMID: 35395322). The only supporting evidence comes from a small case series of four patients with comorbid PTSD and methamphetamine use disorder (PMID: 39175424), which is insufficient to offset the consistent negative findings from higher-quality controlled studies.

**Key findings:**
- A placebo-controlled 8-week trial of bupropion SR (n=30) found no significant between-group differences in PTSD symptom reduction compared to placebo (PMID: 17414245).
- A systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized clinical trials found that bupropion failed to differentiate from placebo in the treatment of PTSD (PMID: 27126398).
- A systematic review and network meta-analysis of RCTs found bupropion similar to placebo for improving sleep quality in PTSD patients (MD -2.28, 95% CrI -4.75 to 0.21, very low certainty of evidence) (PMID: 35395322).
- A small case series of four patients with comorbid PTSD and methamphetamine use disorder reported greater reductions in PTSD checklist scores and lower relapse rates with bupropion-containing regimens compared to serotonergic agents alone, but the very small sample and uncontrolled design severely limit the generalizability of this finding (PMID: 39175424).

### Schizophrenia

#### Literature — Schizophrenia

**Evidence strength:** moderate
**Relevant studies:** 1

A systematic review (PMID: 24201231) covering 13 RCTs and multiple observational reports found that bupropion added to stable antipsychotic regimens produced marked clinical improvement in 229 schizophrenic patients across domains including depression, negative symptoms, cognition, and smoking cessation, without precipitating psychosis. However, 30 cases of bupropion-induced psychosis were also documented, predominantly with the immediate-release formulation and in patients not receiving concomitant antipsychotics, indicating meaningful safety concerns under certain conditions (PMID: 24201231). The evidence thus points in both directions, supporting potential benefit when bupropion is combined with antipsychotics while also documenting risk of psychosis without antipsychotic cover (PMID: 24201231).

**Key findings:**
- 229 schizophrenic patients on stable antipsychotic regimens were successfully treated with bupropion and experienced marked clinical improvement without developing psychosis (PMID: 24201231).
- 30 cases of bupropion-induced psychosis were published; 57% were associated with the immediate-release formulation and 93% occurred without concomitant antipsychotic medication (PMID: 24201231).
- The systematic review incorporated 13 randomized controlled trials (28 publications), 3 open prospective evaluations, 5 multiple case reports, and 22 single case reports evaluating bupropion in schizophrenia (PMID: 24201231).
- Pharmacokinetic interactions between bupropion and antipsychotics were rare, but electroencephalographic abnormalities occurred frequently (PMID: 24201231).
- The authors concluded that the risk of bupropion-induced psychosis in schizophrenic patients treated with bupropion in addition to antipsychotics seems negligible, and that further trials should focus on negative symptoms and cognitive deficits (PMID: 24201231).

Anyone considering the use of APLENZIN or any other antidepressant in a child, adolescent, or young adult must balance this risk with the clinical need. The majority of these reactions occurred during bupropion treatment, but some occurred in the context of discontinuing treatment. In many cases, a causal relationship to bupropion treatment is not certain, because depressed mood may be a symptom of nicotine withdrawal. However, some of the cases occurred in patients taking bupropion who continued to smoke. Although APLENZIN is not approved for smoking cessation, observe all patients for neuropsychiatric reactions. Instruct the patient to contact a healthcare provider if such reactions occur [see Warnings and Precautions (5.2) ]. Anyone considering the use of WELLBUTRIN XL ® * or any other antidepressant in a child, adolescent, or young adult must balance this risk with the clinical need. Short-term studies did not show an increase in the risk of suicidality with antidepressants compared to placebo in adults beyond age 24; there was a reduction in risk with antidepressants compared to placebo in adults aged 65 and older. Depression and certain other psychiatric disorders are themselves associated with increases in the risk of suicide. Patients of all ages who are started on antidepressant therapy should be monitored appropriately and observed closely for clinical worsening, suicidality, or unusual changes in behavior. (See WARNINGS: Clinical Worsening and Suicide Risk in Treating Psychiatric Disorders , PRECAUTIONS: Information for Patients , and PRECAUTIONS: Pediatric Use .) Use in Smoking Cessation Treatment: WELLBUTRIN ® *, WELLBUTRIN SR and WELLBUTRIN XL are not approved for smoking cessation treatment, but bupropion under the name ZYBAN ® * is approved for this use. Serious neuropsychiatric events, including but not limited to depression, suicidal ideation, suicide attempt, and completed suicide have been reported in patients taking bupropion for smoking cessation. Some cases may have been complicated by the symptoms of nicotine withdrawal in patients who stopped smoking. Depressed mood may be a symptom of nicotine withdrawal. Depression, rarely including suicidal ideation, has been reported in smokers undergoing a smoking cessation attempt without medication. However, some of these symptoms have occurred in patients taking bupropion who continued to smoke. All patients treated with bupropion for smoking cessation treatment should be observed for neuropsychiatric symptoms including changes in behavior, hostility, agitation, depressed mood, and suicide-related events, including ideation, behavior, and attempted suicide. These symptoms, as well as worsening of pre-existing psychiatric illness and completed suicide have been reported in some patients attempting to quit smoking while taking ZYBAN in the post-marketing experience. When symptoms were reported, most were during treatment with ZYBAN, but some were following discontinuation of treatment with ZYBAN. These events have occurred in patients with and without pre-existing psychiatric disease; some have experienced worsening of their psychiatric illnesses. Patients with serious psychiatric illness such as schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, and major depressive disorder did not participate in the pre-marketing studies of ZYBAN. Advise patients and caregivers that the patient using bupropion for smoking cessation should stop taking bupropion and contact a healthcare provider immediately if agitation, hostility, depressed mood, or changes in thinking or behavior that are not typical for the patient are observed, or if the patient develops suicidal ideation or suicidal behavior. In many post-marketing cases, resolution of symptoms after discontinuation of ZYBAN was reported, although in some cases the symptoms persisted; therefore, ongoing monitoring and supportive care should be provided until symptoms resolve. The risks of using bupropion for smoking cessation should be weighed against the benefits of its use. ZYBAN has been demonstrated to increase the likelihood of abstinence from smoking for as long as six months compared to treatment with placebo. The health benefits of quitting smoking are immediate and substantial. (See WARNINGS: Neuropsychiatric Symptoms and Suicide Risk in Smoking Cessation Treatment and PRECAUTIONS: Information for Patients .)
