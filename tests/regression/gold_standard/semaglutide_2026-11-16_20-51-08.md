## Drug Safety

_Drug-wide safety signal — applies to the drug generally, not to any single candidate indication below._

**Regulatory label:** FDA label boxed-warning text (7 product labels, LLM-summarized — see appendix for verbatim text):
Across all seven labels reviewed, the sole boxed-warning hazard is the risk of thyroid C-cell tumors, including medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC): in rodents, semaglutide causes dose-dependent and treatment-duration-dependent thyroid C-cell tumors at clinically relevant exposures, and it is unknown whether semaglutide causes such tumors in humans because human relevance has not been determined. All labels contraindicate use in patients with a personal or family history of MTC or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2), require counseling patients on the potential MTC risk and symptoms of thyroid tumors (e.g., a neck mass, dysphagia, dyspnea, persistent hoarseness), and state that routine monitoring of serum calcitonin or thyroid ultrasound is of uncertain value for early detection of MTC. One substantive difference in scope exists: the OZEMPIC label (first product listed) includes RYBELSUS (the oral semaglutide tablet formulation) within the boxed warning text itself — explicitly naming both RYBELSUS and OZEMPIC as contraindicated and as subjects of the MTC uncertainty statement — whereas all other labels restrict their boxed-warning language solely to the named injectable product (Ozempic or Wegovy). No other differences in clinical substance, risk population, age cutoffs, or direction of effect were identified across the labels.

## Findings by Disease

### Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

#### Literature — Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 15

Multiple controlled trials and real-world studies consistently show that semaglutide improves glycemic control, reduces body weight, and lowers insulin requirements in adults with type 1 diabetes, particularly those with overweight or obesity. The ADJUST-T1D double-blind RCT (PMID: 40550013) demonstrated that 36% of semaglutide-treated patients achieved the composite primary endpoint (TIR >70%, TBR <4%, ≥5% weight loss) versus 0% on placebo, and a separate randomized crossover trial (PMID: 39794615) confirmed increased time in range without excess hypoglycemia. A systematic review and meta-analysis (PMID: 41398803) and multiple real-world cohort studies (PMIDs: 38444317, 39745353, 40318459, 40712645) further support these findings, though semaglutide is not currently FDA-approved for type 1 diabetes and gastrointestinal adverse events and hypoglycemia risk require monitoring (PMID: 41804758).

**Key findings:**
- In a 26-week double-blind RCT (ADJUST-T1D), 36% of semaglutide-treated adults with T1D and obesity achieved the composite endpoint of TIR >70%, TBR <4%, and ≥5% weight loss versus 0% in the placebo group (between-group difference 36 percentage points, P<0.001); mean weight reduction was -8.8 kg with semaglutide vs placebo (PMID: 40550013).
- A randomized double-blind crossover trial showed semaglutide increased time in target glucose range (3.9–10.0 mmol/L) by a mean 4.8 percentage points compared to placebo (P=0.006) without increasing time below range, with no severe hypoglycemia or DKA (PMID: 39794615).
- Discontinuation of semaglutide in T1D was associated with a greater decline in time in range (median change -3.6% vs +1.5% for placebo, adjusted p=0.044) and increased glycemic variability, suggesting benefits are not fully sustained after withdrawal (PMID: 42675550).
- A post-hoc analysis of ADJUST-T1D found semaglutide produced a 22.6% reduction in total daily insulin dose over 26 weeks, driven more by bolus (-30.5%) than basal (-15.6%) insulin reductions, with early effects largely independent of weight loss (PMID: 41429002).
- A systematic review and meta-analysis of 8 studies (N=274 adults with T1D and overweight/obesity) found semaglutide reduced body weight by a mean -6.53% at 6 months, reduced HbA1c by -0.55% at 6 months, and improved time in range by 4.62% at 3 months (PMID: 41398803).
- A retrospective chart review of 50 OW/OB T1D patients on semaglutide showed significantly greater declines in BMI (7.9%), body weight (15.9 lbs), HbA1c, CGM glucose SD, and coefficient of variation, and increased time in range compared to matched controls (PMID: 38444317).
- A 2-year real-world study in 67 adults with T1D showed semaglutide improved TIR from 46% to 71%, reduced HbA1c from 8.2% to 7.1%, and decreased total daily insulin dose from 1.4 to 0.7 IU/kg/day (all p<0.001), with no DKA hospitalizations (PMID: 40318459).
- Real-world use of semaglutide in 50 adults with T1D resulted in 9.1% body weight loss and HbA1c reduction of -0.54% (P=0.0001) over 12 months (PMID: 39745353).
- In a real-world study of 42 overweight/obese adults with T1D, semaglutide produced 13.3% mean relative weight loss at 12 months, with 76.4% achieving ≥5% weight loss, HbA1c reduction of 0.4%, and reduction in MASLD prevalence from 82.6% to 30.4% (PMID: 40712645).
- Post-hoc analysis of a crossover trial showed total daily and bolus insulin were significantly reduced by day 7 of semaglutide initiation in T1D patients on pump therapy, with basal insulin reduced by day 32, and median time in hypoglycemia rarely exceeding 4% (PMID: 41144928).
- Two adolescents (ages 12 and 17) with T1D and obesity treated with semaglutide achieved notable improvements in glycemic control, weight, and insulin requirements after 12 months without adverse events (PMID: 42097623).
- In a survey of 230 adults with T1D using semaglutide or tirzepatide, symptomatic hypoglycemia was reported in 13.4% of semaglutide users, and gastrointestinal adverse events were comparable between groups; individualized risk assessment and close insulin dose monitoring were recommended (PMID: 41804758).
- Qualitative analysis from a crossover RCT found 42% of T1D participants expressed interest in continued semaglutide use outside the study, citing lower insulin requirements, weight loss, and improved glycemic management, though the Diabetes Bowel Symptom Questionnaire showed increased GI symptom frequency and severity with semaglutide (PMID: 41175929).
- Transcriptomic analysis in diabetic Akita mice (a T1D model) showed GLP-1 receptor agonism with semaglutide restored 25% of diabetes-induced differentially expressed genes in renal S3 segments, reducing cellular stress response and proliferation associated with restored SGLT1 membrane expression (PMID: 42213650).

### Stroke Disorder

#### Literature — Stroke Disorder

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 10

The preponderance of evidence from randomized controlled trials, post-hoc analyses, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and real-world data supports semaglutide in reducing stroke risk. The SUSTAIN 6 trial demonstrated a significant reduction in nonfatal stroke (HR 0.61, 95% CI 0.38–0.99) (PMID: 27633186), and a post-hoc pooled analysis confirmed semaglutide reduced any first stroke versus placebo (HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.46–1.00) driven by small-vessel occlusion prevention (PMID: 35582947). While one meta-analysis found no significant effect on nonfatal stroke in isolation (PMID: 41272444) and a phase 2 trial in large vessel occlusion showed a neutral primary outcome overall (PMID: 41392086), these are outweighed by consistent positive findings across multiple higher-powered systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and real-world studies showing significant stroke risk reductions (PMID: 41188987, 39396098, 38340253).

**Key findings:**
- In SUSTAIN 6, nonfatal stroke occurred in 1.6% of semaglutide patients versus 2.7% of placebo patients (HR 0.61, 95% CI 0.38–0.99, p=0.04) (PMID: 27633186).
- A post-hoc pooled analysis of SUSTAIN 6 and PIONEER 6 found semaglutide reduced any first stroke versus placebo (0.8 vs 1.1 events/100 PYO; HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.46–1.00; p=0.048), primarily driven by significant reductions in small-vessel occlusion (HR 0.51, 95% CI 0.29–0.89; p=0.017) (PMID: 35582947).
- A systematic review and meta-analysis of 17 studies (n=40,632) found semaglutide significantly reduced nonfatal stroke by 32% (HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.56–0.83) (PMID: 41188987).
- A meta-analysis in overweight/obese patients found semaglutide reduced stroke risk by 35% in patients with diabetes (RR 0.65, 95% CI 0.44–0.97) (PMID: 39396098).
- A real-world US claims database study found semaglutide initiators had significantly lower stroke risk than DPP4i initiators in T2D (HR 0.63, 95% CI 0.41–0.95) and T2D plus ASCVD (HR 0.45, 95% CI 0.24–0.86) (PMID: 38340253).
- A meta-analysis of 4 RCTs (n=27,617) found no significant effect of semaglutide on nonfatal stroke specifically, though overall MACE was significantly reduced (RR 0.81, 95% CI 0.74–0.88) (PMID: 41272444).
- A meta-analysis of oral semaglutide found no significant difference for nonfatal stroke specifically, though overall cardiovascular events were significantly reduced (RR 0.86, 95% CI 0.78–0.95) (PMID: 40752805).
- A phase 2 RCT in large vessel occlusion patients receiving endovascular therapy found no significant difference in the primary outcome of favorable neurological recovery (56.5% vs 54.9%; adjusted RR 1.05, 95% CI 0.95–1.15), though a subgroup not receiving intravenous thrombolysis showed potential benefit (adjusted RR 1.18, 95% CI 1.02–1.36) (PMID: 41392086).
- In a preclinical rat MCAO/R model, semaglutide inhibited ferroptosis via GLP-1R-mediated modulation of FoXO1/GPX4 and DRP1/ACSL4 pathways, reducing neuronal apoptosis and improving mitochondrial function, providing mechanistic support for neuroprotection in ischemic stroke (PMID: 40748430).

### Nicotine Dependence

#### Literature — Nicotine Dependence

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 3

The evidence is mixed overall. The only dedicated randomized clinical trial of semaglutide for nicotine/cigarette use (PMID: 42189538) failed to meet its co-primary endpoints of smoking resistance and cigarettes per day, though it did show significant reductions in craving and body weight, yielding a mixed paper-level verdict. Supportive signals come from a secondary finding in an alcohol use disorder RCT showing semaglutide reduced cigarettes per day in a smoking subsample (PMID: 39937469), and from a large real-world target trial emulation associating semaglutide with lower tobacco use disorder-related healthcare encounters (PMID: 39074369), but these are indirect or observational in nature and do not offset the null primary endpoints from the only direct controlled trial.

**Key findings:**
- In a phase 2a RCT of semaglutide vs. placebo in daily cigarette smokers, the co-primary outcomes of laboratory smoking resistance (P=.16) and cigarettes per day (P=.30) were not significantly improved by semaglutide (PMID: 42189538).
- The same RCT found semaglutide significantly reduced nicotine craving (treatment-by-time interaction β=-0.11, P=.01) and body weight (P<.001), and a supplementary change-score analysis showed greater reductions in laboratory smoking (P=.02, d=0.67) (PMID: 42189538).
- In a phase 2 RCT of semaglutide for alcohol use disorder, a significant treatment-by-time interaction indicated semaglutide predicted greater reductions in cigarettes per day in a subsample of current cigarette users (β=-0.10, P=.005) (PMID: 39937469).
- A nationwide target trial emulation found semaglutide was associated with significantly lower risk of medical encounters for tobacco use disorder diagnosis compared with other antidiabetes medications, including other GLP-1RAs (HR 0.88, CI 0.81–0.96), with most differences occurring within 30 days of prescription initiation (PMID: 39074369).
- The real-world study also found semaglutide was associated with reduced smoking cessation medication prescriptions and counseling, suggesting fewer TUD-related clinical events, though documentation bias and residual confounding are acknowledged limitations (PMID: 39074369).

#### Literature — Polycystic Ovary Syndrome

**Evidence strength:** moderate, supports
**Relevant studies:** 9

Multiple studies across human and animal designs support the use of semaglutide for polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). A meta-analysis of 8 RCTs (526 patients) found semaglutide significantly reduced BMI and improved lipid profiles in PCOS patients (PMID: 40960939), and a retrospective clinical study of 58 obese women with PCOS showed significant weight loss, reduced blood pressure, HbA1c, and cholesterol, with 50% of those with oligomenorrhoea reporting improved menstrual cycles (PMID: 41283946). Preclinical studies further demonstrate that semaglutide alleviates ovarian inflammation via AMPK/SIRT1/NF-κB signaling (PMID: 39247793), reduces oxidative stress and autophagy via PI3K/AKT/mTOR (PMID: 40433568), and reverses ovarian granulosa cell ferroptosis through epigenetic modulation of GPX4 (PMID: 41803302), collectively supporting repurposing of semaglutide for PCOS.

**Key findings:**
- A meta-analysis of 8 RCTs (526 patients) found semaglutide significantly reduced BMI (MD: -2.20 kg/m²), total cholesterol, triglycerides, and LDL-C in PCOS patients, with greater efficacy at doses ≥1.0 mg/week and in patients with baseline BMI >28 kg/m² (PMID: 40960939).
- In a retrospective study of 58 obese women with PCOS, semaglutide produced mean weight loss of 12.6 kg (11.4% total body weight), with 81% losing >5% body weight, and significant reductions in BMI, systolic blood pressure, HbA1c, and total cholesterol (PMID: 41283946).
- 50% of women with oligomenorrhoea reported subjective improvement in menstrual cycles, and 33% with acne and 29% with hirsutism reported symptom improvements during semaglutide treatment (PMID: 41283946).
- A scoping review of 25 studies found semaglutide produced clinically relevant weight reduction (mean 7.6–11.5 kg over 3–6 months) with concurrent improvements in fasting insulin and HOMA-IR, and improvements in menstrual regularity were reported by several studies (PMID: 42652226).
- In PCOS mice, semaglutide restored estrous rhythm, reduced ovarian vesicle number, corrected serum hormone imbalance, improved glucose tolerance, and alleviated ovarian oxidative stress and autophagy via the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway (PMID: 40433568).
- Semaglutide alleviated ovarian inflammation in PCOS mice via the AMPK/SIRT1/NF-κB signaling pathway, reducing serum testosterone and inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β) and improving follicular cystic dilation (PMID: 39247793).
- In a letrozole plus high-fat diet mouse model of PCOS, semaglutide alleviated PCOS phenotypes, reversed ovarian ferroptosis markers, restored GPX4 expression, and reduced GPX4 promoter hypermethylation and DNMT levels, with efficacy comparable to 5-azacytidine (PMID: 41803302).
- In PCOS mice, semaglutide (alongside liraglutide) ameliorated glucose metabolism, partially reversed reproductive endocrine disorder, improved steroidogenic enzyme mRNA levels, decreased inflammatory mediators, and raised brown adipose tissue marker expression in white adipose tissue (PMID: 36930063).
- A broad review noted semaglutide showed promise in managing PCOS-related obesity and insulin resistance as an off-label application (PMID: 40904035).
- A retrospective cohort study of 41,070 propensity-matched patients with polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome found no statistically significant difference in generalized anxiety disorder or suicidal ideation between semaglutide and metformin users, though minor statistically significant differences in insomnia and major depressive disorder rates were observed (PMID: 42741750).

### Heart Failure

#### Literature — Heart Failure

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 19

The totality of evidence from multiple large randomized controlled trials, prespecified subgroup analyses, pooled analyses, and meta-analyses consistently supports semaglutide as beneficial in heart failure, particularly HFpEF. In the STEP-HFpEF and STEP-HFpEF DM trials, semaglutide significantly improved KCCQ scores, body weight, 6-minute walk distance, and hierarchical composite endpoints versus placebo (PMIDs 37622681, 38587233, 38599221), and a pooled analysis across four RCTs demonstrated that semaglutide reduced the composite of cardiovascular death or worsening heart failure events by 31% in HFpEF patients (PMID 39222642). The single contradicting meta-analysis (PMID 41272444) found no significant effect on heart failure hospitalizations in its pooled MACE-focused analysis, but this is outweighed by the consistent, direct, and more granular evidence from dedicated heart failure trials and larger pooled analyses showing significant reductions in HF events.

**Key findings:**
- In the STEP-HFpEF trial (529 patients with HFpEF and obesity, no diabetes), semaglutide 2.4 mg improved KCCQ-CSS by 16.6 vs 8.7 points (difference 7.8 points, p<0.001), reduced body weight by 13.3% vs 2.6%, improved 6-minute walk distance by 21.5 vs 1.2 m, and produced a win ratio of 1.72 on the hierarchical composite endpoint (PMID 37622681).
- In the STEP-HFpEF DM trial (616 patients with HFpEF, obesity, and type 2 diabetes), semaglutide improved KCCQ-CSS by 13.7 vs 6.4 points (difference 7.3 points, p<0.001), reduced body weight by 9.8% vs 3.4%, improved 6-minute walk distance by 14.3 m, and produced a win ratio of 1.58 on the hierarchical composite endpoint (PMID 38587233).
- A prespecified pooled analysis of STEP-HFpEF and STEP-HFpEF DM (1145 participants) confirmed consistent improvements in KCCQ-CSS (difference 7.5 points, p<0.0001), body weight (-8.4%, p<0.0001), 6-minute walk distance (17.1 m), and hierarchical composite (win ratio 1.65), with serious adverse events occurring in 161 vs 301 patients (PMID 38599221).
- A post-hoc pooled participant-level analysis of SELECT, FLOW, STEP-HFpEF, and STEP-HFpEF DM (3743 HFpEF patients) showed semaglutide reduced the composite of cardiovascular death or worsening heart failure events (HR 0.69, 95% CI 0.53–0.89, p=0.0045) and worsening heart failure events alone (HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.41–0.82, p=0.0019) (PMID 39222642).
- In the SELECT trial prespecified analysis (4286 patients with prevalent heart failure), semaglutide reduced MACE (HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.60–0.87), the composite heart failure endpoint (HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.64–0.98), cardiovascular death (HR 0.76), and all-cause death (HR 0.81), with benefits seen in both HFpEF and HFrEF subgroups (PMID 39181597).
- In the FLOW trial prespecified analysis (3533 patients with type 2 diabetes and CKD), semaglutide reduced the composite of HF events or CV death (HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.62–0.87, p=0.0005), HF events alone (HR 0.73, p=0.0068), and CV death alone (HR 0.71, p=0.0036), with consistent effects in those with and without baseline HF (PMID 39217553).
- A secondary analysis of the SOUL trial (9650 participants with T2D and atherosclerotic CV disease and/or CKD) found oral semaglutide reduced the composite HF outcome in those with HF history (HR 0.78, 95% CI 0.63–0.96), with particular benefit in HFpEF (HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.39–0.86) but not HFrEF (HR 0.98) (PMID 41627802).
- A prespecified pooled subanalysis of STEP-HFpEF trials across age groups (n=1145) showed consistent efficacy of semaglutide on KCCQ-CSS (p-interaction=0.80) and body weight (p-interaction=0.41) across age groups <55, 55–64, 65–74, and ≥75 years, with a consistent safety profile (PMID 41290376).
- A meta-analysis of 8 RCTs (39,204 patients) found semaglutide significantly reduced worsening heart failure (HR 0.84, 95% CI 0.73–0.98, p=0.0245), all-cause mortality (HR 0.84), CV mortality (HR 0.83), and MACE (HR 0.82) compared to placebo (PMID 42186086).
- A meta-analysis of 6 RCTs and 2 observational studies (28,762 patients) found semaglutide associated with reduced risk of HF events (OR 0.74, 95% CI 0.58–0.94, I²=45%) compared to placebo or control (PMID 38908729).
- A systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs in HFpEF patients (3 studies, 1463 patients) found semaglutide significantly improved 6-minute walk distance (MD 16.20 m, p<0.01), reduced systolic blood pressure, CRP, and NT-proBNP compared to placebo (PMID 39907981).
- A meta-analysis of 6 RCTs (n=4216) in HFpEF and obesity found semaglutide significantly reduced NT-proBNP (mean difference -119.7 pg/ml, p<0.001), improved KCCQ scores (+8.27 points, p<0.001), and reduced heart failure hospitalization (OR 0.81, 95% CI 0.75–0.88, p<0.001) (PMID 42342009).
- A meta-analysis of 19 studies in overweight/obese patients found semaglutide reduced hospitalization due to heart failure by 76% (RR 0.24, 95% CI 0.12–0.57), cardiovascular death by 17%, and all-cause death by 21% (PMID 39396098).
- In the STEP-HFpEF pooled analysis, semaglutide reduced NT-proBNP at 52 weeks (estimated treatment ratio 0.82, 95% CI 0.74–0.91, p=0.0002), with greater improvements in health status in those with higher baseline NT-proBNP (p-interaction=0.02) (PMID 38819334).
- A systematic review of 4 studies (3 RCTs, 1 observational) found semaglutide significantly improved KCCQ-CSS, body weight, 6-minute walk distance, and CRP in HF patients, with a significant reduction in MACE (HR 0.76, 95% CI 0.62–0.92) (PMID 39149678).
- The one contradicting meta-analysis (4 RCTs, 27,617 patients) found no significant effect of semaglutide on heart failure hospitalizations or admissions for unstable angina, though it confirmed significant reductions in MACE, CV death, and nonfatal MI; this finding is outweighed by dedicated HF trial evidence (PMID 41272444).
- In the SELECT trial prespecified analysis of HbA1c subgroups, the heart failure composite endpoint (HF hospitalization, urgent medical visit, or CV mortality) was consistently reduced by semaglutide irrespective of baseline HbA1c or HbA1c change, supporting broad applicability (PMID 38907684).

### Prader-willi Syndrome

#### Literature — Prader-willi Syndrome

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 5

The evidence on semaglutide for Prader-Willi syndrome is mixed overall. Several case reports demonstrate meaningful weight loss and glycemic improvement with semaglutide in PWS patients with type 2 diabetes (PMIDs 38553173, 39996062, 35538810), while the larger observational cohort study found no significant overall weight loss—only weight stabilization—though it did show a significant reduction in hyperphagia scores (PMID 42755517). **Key findings:**
- In a retrospective observational cohort of 12 adults with PWS, semaglutide produced weight stabilization but not significant overall weight loss (post -3.1 ± 9.9% vs. pre +5.7 ± 5.6%), though hyperphagia scores significantly decreased (-7.3 ± 6.1 on the Hyperphagia Questionnaire, P=0.003) (PMID 42755517).

- In four PWS patients with type 2 diabetes, semaglutide demonstrated beneficial effects on weight loss and glycemic control when administered as weekly subcutaneous progressive doses (PMID 38553173).
- A 27-year-old male with PWS, obesity, and type 2 diabetes treated with once-weekly semaglutide (up to 0.5 mg) achieved body weight reduction from 79 kg to 71 kg over 24 months while maintaining excellent glycemic control (HbA1c 38 mmol/mol) with no adverse events (PMID 39996062).
- A 33-year-old man with PWS and poorly controlled diabetes showed a reduction in HbA1c from 11.1% to 7.2% and body weight from 99.5 kg to 94.3 kg after 12 months of semaglutide 1 mg/week, even after prior failure with liraglutide (PMID 35538810).
- Mild gastrointestinal side effects were reported in 25% of patients in the observational cohort but did not lead to treatment discontinuation, suggesting reasonable tolerability (PMID 42755517).

### Myocardial Infarction

#### Literature — Myocardial Infarction

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 18

The preponderance of evidence from multiple large randomized controlled trials and meta-analyses consistently supports semaglutide's efficacy in reducing myocardial infarction and broader major adverse cardiovascular events. Large RCTs including SELECT (PMID: 37952131), SOUL (PMID: 40162642), and FLOW (PMID: 39211948) demonstrated significant reductions in MACE including nonfatal MI, and multiple meta-analyses confirmed an 18–25% reduction in nonfatal MI risk with semaglutide versus placebo (PMID: 42186086, PMID: 41286875, PMID: 39396098). The two papers assigned a contradicting or mixed verdict (PMID: 31185157, PMID: 40968407) showed numerically non-significant trends for nonfatal MI specifically, but did not demonstrate harm, and are outweighed by the consistent positive signal across the broader, higher-powered evidence base.

**Key findings:**
- In the SELECT trial of 17,604 patients with obesity and established cardiovascular disease but without diabetes, semaglutide 2.4 mg reduced the composite of CV death, nonfatal MI, or nonfatal stroke by 20% (HR 0.80; 95% CI 0.72–0.90; P<0.001) versus placebo (PMID: 37952131).
- In the SOUL trial of 9,650 patients with type 2 diabetes and atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease or CKD, oral semaglutide significantly reduced MACE (HR 0.86; 95% CI 0.77–0.96; P=0.006) versus placebo (PMID: 40162642).
- In the FLOW trial, semaglutide reduced CV death/MI/stroke by 18% (HR 0.82; 95% CI 0.68–0.98; P=0.03) in patients with type 2 diabetes and CKD, consistently across CKD severity subgroups (PMID: 39211948).
- A meta-analysis of 8 RCTs (n=39,204) found semaglutide significantly reduced nonfatal MI (HR 0.75; 95% CI 0.68–0.84; P<0.0001) and MACE (HR 0.82; 95% CI 0.77–0.87; P<0.0001) versus placebo (PMID: 42186086).
- A systematic review and meta-analysis of 50 trials (n=54,972) found high-certainty evidence that semaglutide reduced myocardial infarction (RR 0.77; 95% CI 0.69–0.85; P<0.01) and all-cause mortality (RR 0.85; 95% CI 0.79–0.91) with no heterogeneity (PMID: 41286875).
- A meta-analysis of 17 studies (n=40,632) found semaglutide reduced nonfatal MI by 18% (HR 0.82; 95% CI 0.68–0.92) and MACE by 18% (HR 0.82; 95% CI 0.75–0.90) in type 2 diabetes patients (PMID: 41188987).
- A meta-analysis of RCTs in overweight/obese patients found semaglutide significantly reduced acute myocardial infarction risk (RR 0.72; 95% CI 0.60–0.85) compared with controls (PMID: 40890879).
- A meta-analysis of patients with overweight or obesity found semaglutide reduced non-fatal myocardial infarction by 24% (RR 0.76; 95% CI 0.66–0.88) and CV death by 17% (PMID: 39396098).
- A meta-analysis of 4 RCTs (n=27,617) found semaglutide significantly reduced MACE (RR 0.81; 95% CI 0.74–0.88; P<0.001) and showed benefits for nonfatal MI and CV death (PMID: 41272444).
- A meta-analysis of 4 RCTs (n=19,663) in T2DM patients found semaglutide significantly reduced nonfatal MI (HR 0.79; 95% CI 0.67–0.92) and the primary cardiovascular outcome (HR 0.83; 95% CI 0.76–0.91) (PMID: 41472878).
- A pooled post hoc analysis of SUSTAIN 6 and PIONEER 6 found semaglutide significantly reduced primary and expanded MACE versus placebo in T2DM patients regardless of prior CVD status or baseline BMI, with effects independent of body weight changes (PMID: 40704485).
- In SUSTAIN 6 (n=3,297), subcutaneous semaglutide reduced the composite of CV death, nonfatal MI, or nonfatal stroke (HR 0.74; 95% CI 0.58–0.95), with nonfatal MI occurring in 2.9% vs 3.9% (HR 0.74; 95% CI 0.51–1.08; P=0.12, non-significant individually) (PMID: 27633186).
- In PIONEER 6 (n=3,183), oral semaglutide showed a numerically higher rate of nonfatal MI versus placebo (2.3% vs 1.9%; HR 1.18; 95% CI 0.73–1.90), though the overall MACE was non-inferior and CV death was significantly reduced (HR 0.49; 95% CI 0.27–0.92) (PMID: 31185157).
- A meta-analysis of oral semaglutide in T2DM found significant reduction in overall cardiovascular events (RR 0.86; 95% CI 0.78–0.95) but no significant effect on nonfatal MI individually, highlighting the need for further investigation (PMID: 40752805).

- A meta-analysis of 5 RCTs (n=12,785) in CKD patients found semaglutide significantly reduced MACE (RR 0.78; 95% CI 0.70–0.87; P<0.00001) and cardiovascular mortality (RR 0.74; 95% CI 0.62–0.88; P=0.0008) (PMID: 41276951).
- In SELECT, semaglutide reduced cardiovascular events consistently across all baseline HbA1c subgroups (normoglycemic through prediabetic range), confirming that cardiovascular benefits including MI reduction are independent of glycemic status (PMID: 38907684).

### Parkinson Disease

#### Literature — Parkinson Disease

**Evidence strength:** weak, supports
**Relevant studies:** 7

All seven relevant abstracts are preclinical (animal or in vitro) studies demonstrating neuroprotective effects of semaglutide in models of Parkinson's disease. Semaglutide improved motor function, reduced dopaminergic neuron loss, decreased α-synuclein accumulation, and attenuated neuroinflammation in MPTP mouse models, 6-OHDA rat/mouse models, and cell-based PD models (PMID: 30017231, 30741689, 35873704, 36419409, 40874950, 40088335). A review of preclinical studies in AD and PD animal models also summarized beneficial effects of semaglutide on neuroinflammation, mitochondrial function, and motor outcomes (PMID: 38921025). The evidence supports repurposing semaglutide for Parkinson's disease, but is currently limited to non-human preclinical data with no human clinical studies in this body of evidence.

**Key findings:**
- Semaglutide (25 nmol/kg ip, once every two days for 30 days) improved MPTP-induced motor impairments, rescued tyrosine hydroxylase levels, reduced α-synuclein accumulation, alleviated chronic neuroinflammation, reduced lipid peroxidation, inhibited mitochondrial mitophagy signaling, and increased GDNF expression in the substantia nigra and striatum of chronic MPTP mice, with semaglutide being more potent than liraglutide in most parameters (PMID: 30741689).
- Semaglutide (25 nmol/kg ip, once daily for 7 days) improved MPTP-induced motor impairments, rescued tyrosine hydroxylase levels, alleviated neuroinflammation, reduced lipid peroxidation, inhibited apoptosis, and increased autophagy-related protein expression in the MPTP mouse model of PD, outperforming liraglutide in most parameters (PMID: 30017231).
- Semaglutide protected SH-SY5Y cells against 6-OHDA cytotoxicity by increasing autophagy flux and decreasing oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction, and was superior to liraglutide at the same dose in most parameters measured in this PD cell model (PMID: 35873704).
- Semaglutide (25 nmol/kg ip, daily for 30 days) reduced apomorphine-induced rotational behavior, alleviated dopamine depletion, reduced IL-1β and TNF-α levels, protected dopaminergic neurons, increased TH expression in the substantia nigra, and reduced monomer and aggregated α-synuclein levels in the 6-OHDA rat model of PD (PMID: 36419409).
- Semaglutide combined with neural stem cell transplantation improved motor function in 6-OHDA PD mice, increased transplanted NSC survival (mean fluorescence photon flux 3.258 × 10⁻¹¹ vs. 0.8192 × 10⁻¹¹ for NSCs alone), and reduced C3+ reactive astrocytes in the striatum (PMID: 40874950).
- Semaglutide administered to diabetic rats with rotenone-induced PD significantly improved cognitive, locomotor, and olfactory functions, reduced oxidative-inflammatory stress biomarkers, downregulated caspase 3 and GFAP, and upregulated NRF2 protein expression compared to the DM+PD group (PMID: 40088335).
- A review of preclinical studies reported that semaglutide demonstrated neuroprotective effects in animal models of PD through modulation of neuroinflammation, enhancement of mitochondrial function, promotion of neurogenesis, improvement of motor function, and amelioration of dopaminergic neuronal loss (PMID: 38921025).

### Eye Disorder

#### Literature — Eye Disorder

**Evidence strength:** weak, supports
**Relevant studies:** 6

Across multiple preclinical models, semaglutide consistently demonstrates protective effects against eye disorders including diabetic retinopathy, glaucoma-related retinal ganglion cell loss, and age-related dry eye disease. Semaglutide reduced retinal vascular permeability and basement membrane thickening in diabetic retinopathy models (PMID: 40749340, PMID: 40931416), protected retinal ganglion cells from metabolic insult (PMID: 41665299), and restored lacrimal gland structure and function in aged mice (PMID: 41812831). One study in an ocular hypertension model showed partial benefits (vascular and astrocytic improvements) without significant retinal ganglion cell survival benefit, yielding a mixed verdict at the paper level (PMID: 40597179), but this does not offset the otherwise consistent pattern of supporting evidence across diverse ocular pathologies.

**Key findings:**
- Semaglutide pretreatment prevented rotenone-induced retinal ganglion cell loss in mice and enhanced glucose metabolism, suggesting neuroprotective potential in glaucoma (PMID: 41665299).
- Long-term semaglutide treatment significantly alleviated dry eye disease phenotypes and preserved lacrimal gland structural integrity in aged mice, reducing senescent cell burden and senescence-associated inflammatory programs (PMID: 41812831).
- Semaglutide reversed retinal basement membrane thickening and vascular leakage in diabetic mice via upregulation of endothelial AnxA2 and MMP-9-dependent extracellular matrix remodeling, with effects distinct from insulin (PMID: 40749340).
- Semaglutide significantly reduced blood glucose, improved retinal function, alleviated neuronal apoptosis, reduced vascular lesions, and restored tight junction markers (ZO-1) in a streptozotocin-induced diabetic retinopathy rat model (PMID: 40931416).
- In an ocular hypertension rat model, semaglutide delayed intraocular pressure elevation and improved astrocytic fractal dimension and vascular remodeling, but did not significantly improve retinal ganglion cell survival, yielding only partial neuroprotective effects (PMID: 40597179).

### Hyperglycemia

#### Literature — Hyperglycemia

**Evidence strength:** moderate, supports
**Relevant studies:** 1

A single real-world observational study (PMID: 40450456) explicitly evaluated semaglutide for the treatment of hyperglycemia in individuals with type 2 diabetes and obesity, reporting additional reductions in HbA1c at 6, 12, and 18 months after switching from dulaglutide to semaglutide. The remaining abstracts were contaminated because they treated FDA-approved indications (type 2 diabetes mellitus, obesity, chronic kidney disease) or other non-approved conditions (T1DM, MODY) as the primary therapeutic target, with hyperglycemia measured only as a secondary manifestation. The available drug-specific evidence supports semaglutide's use for hyperglycemia, though it rests on a single uncontrolled observational study.

**Key findings:**
- Switching from dulaglutide to semaglutide in obese patients with type 2 diabetes produced additional reductions in HbA1c of -0.43% at 6 months, -0.54% at 12 months, and -0.38% at 18 months compared to prior dulaglutide treatment, with no significant increase in adverse effects (PMID: 40450456).
- Weight reductions of -2.7 kg, -3.7 kg, -5.4 kg, and -4.2 kg were observed at 6, 12, 18, and 24 months respectively after switching to semaglutide, alongside the glycemic improvements (PMID: 40450456).
- The study explicitly described semaglutide as indicated for 'the treatment of hyperglycemia in individuals with type 2 diabetes mellitus and obesity,' and the switch was motivated in part by insufficient HbA1c reduction on dulaglutide (PMID: 40450456).

### Schizophrenia

#### Literature — Schizophrenia

**Evidence strength:** strong, contradicts
**Relevant studies:** 2

The best available evidence from a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (PMID: 40900607) shows that semaglutide did not improve schizophrenia symptoms as measured by PANSS-6, and had no significant effect on mental quality of life, though it did improve physical quality of life and metabolic parameters. A secondary analysis of the same trial (PMID: 41702353) confirmed no meaningful effect on PANSS-6 or mental component quality of life. Together, these findings indicate semaglutide does not demonstrate efficacy for the core psychiatric symptoms of schizophrenia.

**Key findings:**
- In the HISTORI RCT, semaglutide had no significant effect on PANSS-6 schizophrenia symptom scores compared to placebo over 30 weeks (PMID: 40900607).
- Semaglutide had no significant effect on mental quality of life (SF-36v2 Mental Component Summary) in the RCT (PMID: 40900607).
- Secondary mediation analyses confirmed no total or mediated effects of semaglutide on mental QoL (MCS) or PANSS-6 scores (PMID: 41702353).
- Semaglutide did improve physical quality of life by 3.75 points on the SF-36v2 PCS (95% CI, 1.52–5.98; P = .001) and reduced HbA1c and body weight significantly, but these are metabolic rather than antipsychotic effects (PMID: 40900607).
- Approximately half of the physical QoL improvement at week 30 was estimated to be mediated through weight loss, but no psychiatric symptom benefit was observed (PMID: 41702353).

⚠️ **Indication-specific safety:** Disease-scoped literature for semaglutide in schizophrenia reported: Exacerbated paranoid delusions and elevated ziprasidone levels attributed to semaglutide in a patient with schizophrenia — "we report a case of a 43-year-old male with schizophrenia and diabetes with exacerbated paranoid delusions upon semaglutide administration for weight loss; symptoms peaked at higher doses and subsided after dose reduction. ### Alzheimer Disease

#### Literature — Alzheimer Disease

**Evidence strength:** strong, contradicts
**Relevant studies:** 21

The highest-quality evidence—two large, well-conducted, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 3 trials (evoke and evoke+, n=3808)—found that oral semaglutide 14 mg did not slow clinical progression of early Alzheimer's disease on the primary endpoint (CDR-SB) at 104 weeks, leading to trial discontinuation due to negative outcomes (PMID: 41865758; PMID: 42159155). Supporting evidence consists predominantly of preclinical animal studies and observational/real-world data in diabetic populations without AD, which cannot offset the direct, controlled clinical trial evidence of no efficacy in symptomatic AD (PMID: 39445596; PMID: 40552638). Several preclinical studies also failed to demonstrate benefit in AD mouse models, further reinforcing the negative picture for treatment of established disease (PMID: 39216535; PMID: 42413499).

**Key findings:**
- In the evoke trial (n=1855), mean CDR-SB change from baseline to week 104 was 2.3 with semaglutide vs. 2.3 with placebo (estimated difference −0.08, 95% CI −0.35 to 0.20, p=0.57), and in evoke+ (n=1953) it was 2.2 vs. 2.1 (difference 0.10, 95% CI −0.17 to 0.38, p=0.46), demonstrating no clinical benefit; both trials were discontinued for negative outcomes (PMID: 41865758).
- A plain-language summary of the evoke(+) studies confirmed that oral semaglutide up to 14 mg once daily did not slow cognitive or functional decline in early Alzheimer's disease and is not effective as a treatment for early-stage AD (PMID: 42159155).
- A review of the evoke(+) trials noted that semaglutide produced a statistically significant reduction in high-sensitivity C-reactive protein and nominally significant reductions in CSF biomarkers (YKL-40, GFAP) of 7–10%, but these did not translate into any correlative effect on AD clinical progression (PMID: 42785112).
- A post-hoc reanalysis of evoke/evoke+ data found a statistically significant difference on the ADCS-ADL-MCI test in the evoke trial at week 130 (p=0.0039) and trends on ADAS-cog-13 at week 156, but these were unplanned secondary analyses at non-primary timepoints and the trials had already been declared negative on their pre-specified primary endpoint (PMID: 42261705).
- A critical review concluded that the negative Phase 3 EVOKE and EVOKE+ outcomes represent a translational paradox, and proposed that semaglutide's value may lie in prevention of upstream metabolic/inflammatory drivers rather than reversing established amyloid-driven AD pathology—a hypothesis requiring prospective validation (PMID: 42325226).
- Semaglutide and tirzepatide did not alter amyloid plaque density, IBA1/GFAP expression, or neurobehavioral/cognitive function in 12-month-old and 6-month-old 5XFAD and APP/PS1 mice despite reducing body weight and improving glucose tolerance (PMID: 39216535).
- Preventative semaglutide treatment for 2 or 4 months in 5xFAD mice did not produce measurable effects on memory, learning, amyloid-β plaque deposition, or glial cell activation, and did not alter microglial activation following LPS administration (PMID: 42413499).
- Real-world target trial emulation in 1,094,761 US patients with type 2 diabetes found semaglutide associated with 40–70% reduced risk of first-time AD diagnosis compared with other antidiabetic medications, but this was in cognitively unimpaired diabetic patients, not in those with established AD (PMID: 39445596).
- A nationwide real-world study found semaglutide associated with reduced risk of overall Alzheimer's disease-related dementia incidence (HR 0.54–0.80 vs. comparators) in T2D patients, with significant reduction in vascular dementia but no evidence of association with frontotemporal or Lewy body dementias (PMID: 40552638).
- A systematic review and meta-analysis of 23 studies (18 on adipose tissue, 5 on brain/cognition) found semaglutide associated with neuroprotective effects and lower AD risk, but noted high heterogeneity across studies and that the cognitive evidence base was limited (PMID: 41766347).
- Semaglutide improved cognitive performance, reduced Aβ deposition, and attenuated neuroinflammatory signaling in APP/PS1 transgenic mice, with associated improvements in BBB integrity markers, but these are preclinical findings (PMID: 42126676).
- Semaglutide improved cognition and reduced Aβ plaques, tau phosphorylation, and NLRP3 inflammasome activation in APP/PS1 mice via NF-κB and Nrf2/HO-1/xCT/GPX4 pathways, but these are preclinical findings (PMID: 41173224).
- Semaglutide improved cognitive capabilities and reduced amyloid plaque deposition and GFAP/Iba1 expression in APP/PS1 mice and human AD brain organoid models, with upregulation of oxytocin, but these are preclinical findings (PMID: 39405916).
- Semaglutide improved motor coordination and associative memory and reduced cortical phosphorylated tau in rTg4510 tauopathy mice, but did not affect hippocampal changes, spontaneous locomotion, or spatial working memory (PMID: 40882702).
- Semaglutide ameliorated cognitive decline and reduced tau hyperphosphorylation in P301S tauopathy mice via autophagy/ACE2/SIRT1/FOXO1-mediated microglial polarization, but these are preclinical findings (PMID: 39875022).
- Semaglutide improved working memory and spatial reference memory and promoted microglial M1-to-M2 transition in 3xTg-AD mice, but these are preclinical findings (PMID: 39547338).
- Semaglutide improved glucose metabolism and reduced Aβ plaques and tau tangles in 3xTg mice via the GLP-1R/SIRT1/GLUT4 pathway, but these are preclinical findings (PMID: 37730113).
- In 5xFAD mice on high-fat diet, semaglutide reduced Aβ40/42 levels and improved spatial memory, but WT mice on regular diet showed a modest decline in Y-maze performance, suggesting context-dependent and potentially adverse effects under normal metabolic conditions (PMID: 42353032).
- In an STZ-induced sporadic AD rat model, semaglutide reduced p-tau, reactive astrocytes, and microglial activation and attenuated ventriculomegaly, but did not completely halt neurodegeneration and had no effect on doublecortin-positive cells in the dentate gyrus (PMID: 42351685).
