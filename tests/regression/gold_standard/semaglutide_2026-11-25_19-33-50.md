## Drug Safety

_Drug-wide safety signal — applies to the drug generally, not to any single candidate indication below._

**Regulatory label:** FDA label boxed-warning text (7 product labels, LLM-summarized — see appendix for verbatim text):
Across all seven labels, the sole boxed-warning hazard is the risk of thyroid C-cell tumors, including medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC): in rodents, semaglutide causes dose-dependent and treatment-duration-dependent thyroid C-cell tumors at clinically relevant exposures, and it is unknown whether the drug causes such tumors in humans because human relevance has not been determined. All labels contraindicate use in patients with a personal or family history of MTC or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2), require counseling about the potential MTC risk and symptoms of thyroid tumors (e.g., a neck mass, dysphagia, dyspnea, persistent hoarseness), and state that routine monitoring of serum calcitonin or thyroid ultrasound is of uncertain value for early MTC detection. No label introduces a different hazard, a different risk population, an age cutoff, or a different direction of effect; the only substantive variation across labels is that the OZEMPIC label (first product listed) names both RYBELSUS and OZEMPIC as the products covered by the warning, whereas all other labels name only the single product on that label (Ozempic or Wegovy), reflecting a label revision that separated the shared warning into product-specific versions rather than a difference in clinical substance.

## Findings by Disease

### Heart Failure

#### Literature — Heart Failure

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 12

The preponderance of evidence from multiple large randomized controlled trials and meta-analyses consistently supports semaglutide as beneficial in heart failure, particularly HFpEF. The STEP-HFpEF and STEP-HFpEF DM trials demonstrated significant improvements in symptoms, physical function, and body weight (PMID: 37622681, PMID: 38587233), and a pooled analysis of four RCTs showed semaglutide reduced the composite of cardiovascular death or worsening heart failure events by 31% in HFpEF patients (PMID: 39222642). The single contradicting meta-analysis (PMID: 41272444) found no significant effect on heart failure hospitalizations overall, but this finding is outweighed by the more specific, larger, and more recent controlled trial evidence directly targeting heart failure populations.

**Key findings:**
- In the STEP-HFpEF trial, semaglutide improved KCCQ-CSS by 16.6 points vs 8.7 points with placebo (difference 7.8 points, P<0.001) and reduced body weight by 13.3% vs 2.6% in patients with HFpEF and obesity (PMID: 37622681).
- In the STEP-HFpEF DM trial, semaglutide improved KCCQ-CSS by 13.7 points vs 6.4 points with placebo (difference 7.3 points, P<0.001) in patients with obesity-related HFpEF and type 2 diabetes (PMID: 38587233).
- A pooled analysis of SELECT, FLOW, STEP-HFpEF, and STEP-HFpEF DM trials showed semaglutide reduced the composite of cardiovascular death or worsening heart failure events (HR 0.69, 95% CI 0.53-0.89, p=0.0045) and worsening heart failure events alone (HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.41-0.82, p=0.0019) in HFpEF patients (PMID: 39222642).
- In the SELECT trial prespecified analysis, semaglutide reduced the composite heart failure endpoint (HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.64-0.98) and MACE (HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.60-0.87) in patients with a history of heart failure, regardless of HF subtype (PMID: 39181597).
- In the FLOW trial, semaglutide reduced the composite of HF events or CV death (HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.62-0.87, P=0.0005), HF events alone (HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.58-0.92), and CV death alone (HR 0.71, 95% CI 0.56-0.89) in patients with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease (PMID: 39217553).
- A pooled analysis of STEP-HFpEF and STEP-HFpEF DM showed semaglutide improved KCCQ-CSS (between-group difference 7.5 points, P<0.0001), 6-minute walk distance (17.1 m, P<0.0001), and hierarchical composite endpoint (win ratio 1.65, P<0.0001), with serious adverse events reported in 161 vs 301 patients (PMID: 38599221).
- In the SOUL trial secondary analysis, oral semaglutide reduced the composite HF outcome in participants with HF history (HR 0.78, 95% CI 0.63-0.96), with particular benefit in those with preserved ejection fraction (HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.39-0.86) (PMID: 41627802).
- Semaglutide reduced NT-proBNP at 52 weeks in obesity-related HFpEF (estimated treatment ratio 0.82, 95% CI 0.74-0.91, P=0.0002), with greater symptom improvements in those with higher baseline NT-proBNP (PMID: 38819334).
- A systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs and cohorts in HFpEF patients found semaglutide significantly increased 6-minute walk distance (MD 16.20 m, P<0.01) and reduced NT-proBNP, systolic blood pressure, and CRP compared to placebo (PMID: 39907981).
- Efficacy of semaglutide on KCCQ-CSS and body weight was consistent across the age spectrum (p-interaction=0.80 and 0.41, respectively) in the STEP-HFpEF programme, including patients aged 75 years or over (PMID: 41290376).
- A meta-analysis of four RCTs found semaglutide significantly reduced MACE (RR 0.81, P<0.001) but showed no significant effect on heart failure hospitalizations specifically, representing the primary contradicting finding (PMID: 41272444).
- Cardiovascular risk reduction with semaglutide in SELECT was consistent regardless of baseline HbA1c, including for the heart failure composite endpoint of HF hospitalization, urgent medical visit, or cardiovascular mortality (PMID: 38907684).

### Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

#### Literature — Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 10

The evidence supports repurposing semaglutide for type 1 diabetes mellitus, particularly in adults with overweight or obesity. Multiple randomized controlled trials demonstrate that semaglutide as adjunct to automated insulin delivery significantly improved time in range, reduced HbA1c, and promoted weight loss compared to placebo in adults with T1D (PMID: 40550013, PMID: 39794615), with a meta-analysis of 8 studies confirming reductions in body weight, HbA1c, and insulin requirements (PMID: 41398803). Real-world and post-hoc analyses further corroborate these glycemic and metabolic benefits, though gastrointestinal adverse events are common and careful insulin dose management is required (PMID: 41804758, PMID: 41175929).

**Key findings:**
- In a 26-week double-blind RCT (ADJUST-T1D), 36% of semaglutide-treated adults with T1D and obesity achieved the composite primary endpoint (TIR >70%, TBR <4%, ≥5% weight loss) vs. 0% in placebo; HbA1c decreased by 0.3 percentage points and body weight by 8.8 kg (PMID: 40550013)
- In a double-blind randomized crossover trial, semaglutide increased time in target glucose range (3.9–10.0 mmol/L) by a mean 4.8 percentage points vs. placebo without increasing hypoglycemia time, and no DKA or severe hypoglycemia occurred (PMID: 39794615)
- A post-hoc analysis of ADJUST-T1D showed semaglutide produced a 22.6% reduction in total daily insulin dose over 26 weeks, driven more by bolus (-30.5%) than basal (-15.6%) insulin reductions (PMID: 41429002)
- A systematic review and meta-analysis of 8 studies (N=274 adults with T1D and overweight/obesity) found semaglutide reduced body weight by 6.53% and HbA1c by 0.55% at 6 months, and improved time in range by 4.62% at 3 months (PMID: 41398803)
- Discontinuation of semaglutide after the ADJUST-T1D trial was associated with a greater decline in time in range (-3.6% vs. +1.5% for placebo) and increased glycemic variability, suggesting benefits are not fully sustained after withdrawal (PMID: 42675550)
- A post-hoc analysis of a crossover trial showed total daily and bolus insulin were significantly reduced by day 7 of semaglutide initiation in T1D on pump therapy, with basal insulin reduced by day 32, and time in hypoglycemia rarely exceeded 4% (PMID: 41144928)
- A retrospective chart review of 50 OW/OB adults with T1D showed significantly greater declines in BMI (7.9%), body weight (15.9 lbs), HbA1c, CGM glucose SD and CV, and increased TIR with semaglutide vs. matched controls over 1 year (PMID: 38444317)
- A retrospective chart review of 100 adults with T1D showed semaglutide use was associated with 9.1% body weight loss and HbA1c reduction of 0.54% over 12 months, with no change in controls (PMID: 39745353)
- A 2-year real-world study of semaglutide in 67 adults with T1D showed TIR improved from 46% to 71%, HbA1c from 8.2% to 7.1%, and total daily insulin dose halved from 1.4 to 0.7 IU/kg/day, with no DKA hospitalizations (PMID: 40318459)
- A real-world 12-month study of semaglutide in 42 overweight/obese adults with T1D showed 13.3% mean relative weight loss, HbA1c reduction of 0.4%, and MASLD prevalence reduction from 82.6% to 30.4% (PMID: 40712645)
- Two adolescent case reports (ages 12 and 17) with T1D and obesity showed notable improvements in glycemic control, weight, and insulin requirements after 12 months of semaglutide without adverse events (PMID: 42097623)
- A real-world comparison study found semaglutide produced 9.9% weight loss and 0.33% HbA1c reduction in adults with T1D and obesity over 12 months, with no severe hypoglycemia or DKA reported (PMID: 41048008)
- A qualitative and PRO analysis from a crossover RCT found that semaglutide did not improve diabetes distress, hypoglycemia fear, or treatment satisfaction scores vs. placebo, but 42% of participants expressed interest in continued use; increased GI symptom frequency and severity were the only questionnaire difference (PMID: 41175929)
- A survey of 230 adults with T1D using semaglutide or tirzepatide found symptomatic hypoglycemia was reported by 13.4% of semaglutide users vs. 29% of tirzepatide users, while gastrointestinal AEs were comparable between groups (PMID: 41804758)

### Stroke Disorder

#### Literature — Stroke Disorder

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 5

The evidence on semaglutide for stroke is mixed. Two large RCTs and a post-hoc pooled analysis demonstrate statistically significant reductions in nonfatal stroke risk with semaglutide versus placebo (PMID: 27633186; PMID: 35582947), and a systematic review and meta-analysis of 17 studies reports a 32% reduction in nonfatal stroke (PMID: 41188987); however, a separate meta-analysis of four RCTs found no significant effect of semaglutide on nonfatal stroke incidence (PMID: 41272444), and a phase 2 RCT in acute large vessel occlusion showed no significant improvement in the primary neurological outcome overall (PMID: 41392086). Because comparably credible controlled evidence points in both directions—with one meta-analysis of RCTs showing null stroke results directly contradicting another meta-analysis and individual RCT data showing benefit—the net evidence is mixed.

**Key findings:**
- In SUSTAIN 6, nonfatal stroke occurred in 1.6% of semaglutide patients versus 2.7% of placebo patients (HR 0.61, 95% CI 0.38–0.99, P=0.04) (PMID: 27633186).
- A post-hoc pooled analysis of SUSTAIN 6 and PIONEER 6 found semaglutide reduced incidence of any first stroke versus placebo (0.8 vs. 1.1 events/100 PYO; HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.46–1.00; P=0.048), driven primarily by reductions in small-vessel occlusion (HR 0.51, 95% CI 0.29–0.89; P=0.017) (PMID: 35582947).
- A systematic review and meta-analysis of 17 studies (n=40,632) reported semaglutide significantly reduced nonfatal stroke by 32% (HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.56–0.83) (PMID: 41188987).
- A pre-registered meta-analysis of four RCTs (n=27,617) found semaglutide showed no significant effect on nonfatal stroke incidence, despite significant reductions in overall MACE (RR 0.81, 95% CI 0.74–0.88) (PMID: 41272444).
- A phase 2 RCT in patients with acute large vessel occlusion undergoing endovascular therapy found no significant difference in favorable neurological recovery at 90 days between semaglutide and standard therapy (56.5% vs. 54.9%; adjusted RR 1.05, 95% CI 0.95–1.15, P=0.37), though a subgroup not receiving intravenous thrombolysis showed a potential benefit (PMID: 41392086).

### Polycystic Ovary Syndrome

#### Literature — Polycystic Ovary Syndrome

**Evidence strength:** moderate, supports
**Relevant studies:** 4

Multiple studies across human clinical and preclinical designs indicate that semaglutide supports repurposing for polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). A meta-analysis of 8 RCTs (PMID: 40960939) found semaglutide significantly reduced BMI and improved lipid profiles in PCOS patients, and a scoping review of 25 studies (PMID: 42652226) reported clinically relevant weight reduction and improvements in menstrual regularity, though predominantly from low-quality or observational designs. Preclinical animal studies further demonstrate that semaglutide alleviates ovarian inflammation via the AMPK/SIRT1/NF-κB pathway (PMID: 39247793), reduces ovarian oxidative stress and autophagy via PI3K/AKT/mTOR (PMID: 40433568), and reverses ovarian granulosa cell ferroptosis through epigenetic modulation of GPX4 (PMID: 41803302), extending the therapeutic rationale beyond metabolic benefits.

**Key findings:**
- A meta-analysis of 8 RCTs (526 patients) found semaglutide significantly reduced BMI (MD: -2.20 kg/m², 95% CI: -2.42 to -1.97, P < 0.001), total cholesterol, triglycerides, and LDL-C in PCOS patients, with greater efficacy at doses ≥1.0 mg/week and in patients with baseline BMI >28 kg/m² (PMID: 40960939).
- A scoping review of 25 studies reported semaglutide produced mean weight reduction of 7.6–11.5 kg over 3–6 months, with ≥5% weight loss in approximately 80% of patients in responsive cohorts, and improvements in menstrual regularity were reported by several studies (PMID: 42652226).
- In a retrospective real-world study of 58 obese women with PCOS, semaglutide was associated with mean weight loss of 12.6 ± 9.6 kg (11.4% total body weight), reduced BMI from 40.7 to 36.2 kg/m², reduced systolic blood pressure by 6.3 mmHg, reduced HbA1C, and 50% of those with oligomenorrhoea reported subjective improvement in menstrual cycles (PMID: 41283946).
- In a PCOS mouse model, semaglutide restored estrous cycle rhythm, decreased ovarian vesicle number, corrected serum hormone imbalance, improved glucose tolerance, and alleviated ovarian oxidative stress and autophagy via the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway (PMID: 40433568).
- In PCOS mice, semaglutide reduced weight, improved insulin resistance, restored estrous cycle, significantly decreased serum testosterone and inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β), improved follicular cystic dilation, and alleviated ovarian inflammation via the AMPK/SIRT1/NF-κB signaling pathway (PMID: 39247793).
- In a letrozole plus high-fat diet PCOS mouse model and in DHT-stimulated primary granulosa cells in vitro, semaglutide alleviated PCOS phenotypes, reversed ovarian ferroptosis markers, restored GPX4 expression, and reduced GPX4 promoter hypermethylation and DNMT levels, suggesting direct ovarian protection via epigenetic modulation (PMID: 41803302).
- A narrative review noted semaglutide showed promise in managing PCOS-related obesity and insulin resistance, with improvements in menstrual regularity and hormonal parameters reported across studies (PMID: 40904035).

### Nicotine Dependence

#### Literature — Nicotine Dependence

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 2

Evidence on semaglutide for nicotine dependence is mixed. The only randomized controlled trial (PMID: 42189538) failed to meet its co-primary endpoints of laboratory smoking resistance and weekly cigarettes per day, though it did show significant reductions in craving and body weight. A large real-world target trial emulation (PMID: 39074369) found semaglutide associated with significantly lower rates of TUD-related healthcare encounters compared to other antidiabetic medications, but this observational design cannot establish causation and is subject to confounding.

**Key findings:**
- In a phase 2a RCT, semaglutide did not significantly improve co-primary outcomes of laboratory smoking resistance (β = 0.16, P = .16) or weekly cigarettes per day (β = -0.08, P = .30) compared to placebo (PMID: 42189538).

- The observational study was limited by documentation bias, residual confounding, and missing data on current smoking behavior and medication adherence, reducing the strength of its conclusions (PMID: 39074369).

### Coronary Artery Disorder

#### Literature — Coronary Artery Disorder

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 6

The evidence supports repurposing semaglutide for coronary artery disorder. A prespecified analysis of a large RCT (SELECT) showed semaglutide reduced cardiovascular events including coronary revascularization and expanded MACE irrespective of baseline HbA1c in patients with overweight/obesity and atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (PMID: 38907684), and a systematic review and meta-analysis of 45 RCTs found semaglutide significantly reduced the broad CAD-related composite (RR=0.80), ACS (RR=0.77), MI (RR=0.70), unstable angina (RR=0.82), and angina pectoris (RR=0.73) in adults with T2DM and/or obesity (PMID: 42469143). Large animal models of coronary artery disease further demonstrated that oral semaglutide improved myocardial perfusion, left ventricular ejection fraction, arteriolar density, and endothelial function through AMPK/eNOS pathways, independently of diabetes or obesity (PMID: 39211263, 39665144, 41553720), and a real-world observational study found semaglutide associated with significantly lower MACE risk versus tirzepatide in patients with established ASCVD (PMID: 41491349).

**Key findings:**
- In the SELECT trial prespecified analysis, semaglutide reduced cardiovascular events including expanded MACE (coronary revascularization, unstable angina hospitalization, cardiovascular mortality, nonfatal MI, or stroke) consistently across all baseline HbA1c subgroups in patients with overweight/obesity and atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease without diabetes (PMID: 38907684).
- A meta-analysis of 45 RCTs found semaglutide significantly reduced the broad CAD-related composite (RR=0.80, 95% CI 0.73–0.87, p<0.001), ACS (RR=0.77), MI (RR=0.70), unstable angina (RR=0.82), and angina pectoris (RR=0.73) in adults with T2DM and/or obesity (PMID: 42469143).
- In a Yorkshire swine ameroid constrictor model of coronary artery disease, oral semaglutide improved left ventricular ejection fraction at rest and during pacing (both p<0.03), increased perfusion to the most ischemic myocardial region (both p<0.03), and reduced perivascular and interstitial fibrosis (both p<0.03) and apoptosis (p=0.008), associated with AMPK pathway activation and increased eNOS (PMID: 39211263).
- A published version of the same large animal CAD model confirmed that oral semaglutide improved LVEF, myocardial perfusion, reduced fibrosis and apoptosis, and increased AMPK and eNOS activation in chronically ischemic myocardium (PMID: 39665144).
- In a swine model of CAD with metabolic syndrome, semaglutide enhanced left ventricular filling, end diastolic volume, stroke volume, and cardiac index (all p<0.05), increased arteriolar density (p<0.001), improved microvascular endothelium-dependent vasodilation (p<0.01), and augmented vascular remodeling markers including eNOS (p<0.01) (PMID: 41553720).
- In a real-world US claims database study of patients with overweight/obesity and established ASCVD without diabetes, semaglutide was associated with a statistically significant 29% reduction in rMACE-3 (HR 0.71, p=0.046) and 22% reduction in rMACE-5 including coronary revascularization (HR 0.78, p=0.040) compared with tirzepatide (PMID: 41491349).

### Myocardial Infarction

#### Literature — Myocardial Infarction

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 6

The preponderance of evidence from large randomized controlled trials and multiple systematic reviews and meta-analyses supports that semaglutide reduces the risk of nonfatal myocardial infarction and broader major adverse cardiovascular events compared to placebo. The SUSTAIN-6 trial showed a hazard ratio of 0.74 for nonfatal MI (PMID: 27633186), and meta-analyses confirm an 18–25% reduction in nonfatal MI risk across tens of thousands of patients (PMID: 41188987, PMID: 41272444, PMID: 42186086). The one trial showing a numerically higher nonfatal MI rate with oral semaglutide (PMID: 31185157) was underpowered for individual components and did not reach statistical significance, and is outweighed by the consistent positive signal across higher-level evidence.

**Key findings:**
- SUSTAIN-6 RCT (n=3297) showed nonfatal MI occurred in 2.9% of semaglutide patients vs. 3.9% of placebo patients (HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.51–1.08), with the composite MACE endpoint significantly lower for semaglutide (HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.58–0.95, P<0.001 for noninferiority) (PMID: 27633186).
- PIONEER 6 RCT (n=3183) showed nonfatal MI occurred in 2.3% of oral semaglutide patients vs. 1.9% of placebo patients (HR 1.18, 95% CI 0.73–1.90), a non-significant numerical increase; however, the overall MACE composite favored semaglutide (HR 0.79) and the trial was not powered for individual components (PMID: 31185157).
- A systematic review and meta-analysis of 17 studies (n=40,632) found semaglutide significantly reduced nonfatal MI by 18% (HR 0.82, 95% CI 0.68–0.92) and MACE by 18% (HR 0.82, 95% CI 0.75–0.90) (PMID: 41188987).
- A meta-analysis of 4 RCTs (n=27,617) found semaglutide significantly reduced MACE (RR 0.81, 95% CI 0.74–0.88, p<0.001) with observed benefits for nonfatal myocardial infarction specifically (PMID: 41272444).
- A meta-analysis of 8 RCTs (n=39,204) found semaglutide significantly reduced nonfatal MI (HR 0.75, 95% CI 0.68–0.84, p<0.0001), MACE (HR 0.82, 95% CI 0.77–0.87), and CV mortality (HR 0.83, 95% CI 0.72–0.95) compared to placebo (PMID: 42186086).
- A meta-analysis of 5 RCTs on oral semaglutide (n=13,875) found a significant reduction in overall cardiovascular events (RR 0.86, 95% CI 0.78–0.95; HR 0.85, 95% CI 0.77–0.95), though nonfatal MI individually did not reach significance (PMID: 40752805).

### Binge Eating Disorder

#### Literature — Binge Eating Disorder

**Evidence strength:** moderate, supports
**Relevant studies:** 3

The available evidence consistently supports semaglutide as a beneficial treatment for binge eating disorder. A systematic review found semaglutide effective and well-tolerated in BED populations, acting on both weight and cognitive symptoms of the disease (PMID: 40577093), a retrospective cohort study showed semaglutide-only patients had greater reductions in Binge Eating Scale scores compared to lisdexamfetamine or topiramate (PMID: 37990682), and a randomized controlled trial found that participants with baseline binge episodes or full/subthreshold BED were more likely to achieve abstinence with semaglutide compared to placebo (PMID: 43035836). Although one RCT yielded mixed findings on overall binge frequency reductions, its subgroup results and the convergent direction of the other studies tip the net evidence toward support.

**Key findings:**
- A systematic review of 37 studies concluded that semaglutide showed great interest in the treatment of BED, acting not only on weight decrease but also on cognitive symptoms linked to the disease (PMID: 40577093).
- In a retrospective cohort study, patients receiving semaglutide only exhibited greater reductions in Binge Eating Scale scores compared to those receiving lisdexamfetamine or topiramate, and combined pharmacotherapy did not outperform semaglutide alone (PMID: 37990682).
- A randomized controlled trial found that mean reductions in loss of control, global eating disorder pathology, weight and shape concern, drive for thinness, and body dissatisfaction were significantly greater with semaglutide compared to placebo at several timepoints (PMID: 43035836).
- In the same RCT, participants reporting baseline binge episodes (n=31) and those with full or subthreshold binge-eating disorder (n=18) were more likely to achieve abstinence with semaglutide compared to placebo, though overall binge frequency reductions did not differ significantly between groups (PMID: 43035836).
- The systematic review noted that semaglutide is effective and well-tolerated in various psychiatric populations, though it flagged potential risks including depressive symptoms and suicidal ideation requiring monitoring (PMID: 40577093).
- The RCT noted that reliable deteriorations in eating psychopathology, new onset of binge eating, and compensatory behaviors were rare and symptom emergence did not appear higher with semaglutide, supporting its safety profile in this population (PMID: 43035836).

### Prader-willi Syndrome

#### Literature — Prader-willi Syndrome

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 5

The evidence on semaglutide for Prader-Willi syndrome is mixed: several case reports and small series report meaningful weight loss and glycemic improvement (PMIDs 38553173, 39996062, 35538810), while the largest observational cohort (n=12) found no significant overall weight loss but did show weight stabilization and significant reduction in hyperphagia (PMID 42755517), and a three-patient case series showed variable outcomes ranging from weight stabilization to modest weight loss (PMID 39906041). All studies are small, uncontrolled, and of low evidence quality, precluding a definitive conclusion in either direction.

**Key findings:**
- In a retrospective observational cohort of 12 adults with PWS, semaglutide produced no significant overall weight loss but stabilized weight (post -3.1 ± 9.9% vs. pre +5.7 ± 5.6%, P=0.037) and significantly reduced hyperphagia scores (-7.3 ± 6.1, P=0.003), with good tolerability (PMID 42755517).
- A three-patient case series showed variable efficacy with semaglutide: one patient had only prevention of further weight gain, while the other two achieved weight losses of 14.4% and 11% relative to baseline (PMID 39906041).
- In four patients with PWS and type 2 diabetes, semaglutide was associated with improvements in weight loss and glycemic control (PMID 38553173).
- A single case report of a 27-year-old male with PWS, obesity, and T2D showed body weight reduction from 79 kg to 71 kg over 24 months and sustained excellent glycemic control (HbA1c 38 mmol/mol) on once-weekly semaglutide, with no adverse events (PMID 39996062).
- A case report of a 33-year-old man with PWS and poorly controlled diabetes showed HbA1c reduction from 11.1% to 7.2% and body weight reduction from 99.5 kg to 94.3 kg after 12 months of semaglutide 1 mg/week, even after prior liraglutide failure (PMID 35538810).

### Infertility

#### Literature — Infertility

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 5

The evidence is mixed: a small randomized trial in men found semaglutide significantly improved sperm morphology and preserved sperm concentration compared to testosterone replacement therapy, supporting a fertility benefit in obese men with functional hypogonadism (PMID: 39511836). However, a large real-world multicenter cohort study in women with overweight and obesity found that semaglutide pretreatment was associated with significantly lower clinical pregnancy and live birth rates compared to matched controls (PMID: 42001071). These findings point in opposite directions across sex-specific populations and study designs, precluding a unified conclusion.

**Key findings:**
- In a 24-week randomized trial of 25 obese men with type 2 diabetes and functional hypogonadism, semaglutide significantly increased morphologically normal sperm from 2% to 4% (p=0.012), while sperm concentration and total number decreased significantly in the testosterone replacement therapy group (PMID: 39511836).
- Semaglutide-treated men had significantly higher sperm concentration, total sperm number, and morphologically normal sperm compared to men receiving testosterone replacement therapy (PMID: 39511836).
- In a retrospective multicenter cohort of 1,936 women with infertility, semaglutide pretreatment was associated with lower pregnancy rates in women with overweight and class I obesity, and significantly lower live birth rates in women with class I and class II–III obesity compared to matched controls (PMID: 42001071).
- Time-to-event analyses in the female cohort showed lower cumulative probabilities of pregnancy and live birth among semaglutide users, though hazard ratios were not consistently significant, and miscarriage rates did not differ between groups (PMID: 42001071).

### Parkinson Disease

#### Literature — Parkinson Disease

**Evidence strength:** weak, supports
**Relevant studies:** 5

All six relevant abstracts are preclinical (animal or in vitro) studies demonstrating neuroprotective effects of semaglutide in models of Parkinson's disease. Semaglutide improved motor function, rescued dopaminergic neurons, reduced α-synuclein accumulation, and attenuated neuroinflammation and oxidative stress in MPTP and 6-OHDA mouse models (PMID: 30741689, PMID: 30017231, PMID: 35873704, PMID: 40874950, PMID: 40088335). A review of preclinical studies in AD and PD animal models also summarized beneficial effects of semaglutide on neuroinflammation, mitochondrial function, and motor outcomes (PMID: 38921025). The evidence supports repurposing semaglutide for Parkinson's disease but is limited entirely to non-human preclinical models with no human clinical data available in these abstracts.

**Key findings:**
- Semaglutide (25 nmol/kg ip every two days for 30 days) improved MPTP-induced motor impairments, rescued tyrosine hydroxylase levels, reduced α-synuclein accumulation, alleviated chronic neuroinflammation, reduced lipid peroxidation, inhibited mitochondrial mitophagy signaling, and increased GDNF expression in the substantia nigra and striatum of chronic MPTP mice (PMID: 30741689).
- Semaglutide was more potent than liraglutide in most neuroprotective parameters measured in the chronic MPTP mouse model of Parkinson's disease (PMID: 30741689).
- Semaglutide (25 nmol/kg ip once daily for 7 days) improved motor impairments, rescued tyrosine hydroxylase levels, alleviated inflammation, reduced lipid peroxidation, inhibited apoptosis, and increased autophagy-related protein expression in the acute MPTP mouse model (PMID: 30017231).
- Semaglutide protected SH-SY5Y cells against 6-OHDA cytotoxicity by increasing autophagy flux and decreasing oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction, and was superior to liraglutide at the same dose in most parameters (PMID: 35873704).
- Semaglutide combined with neural stem cell transplantation improved motor function in 6-OHDA PD mice, increased transplanted cell survival (mean fluorescence photon flux 3.258×10⁻¹¹ vs 0.8192×10⁻¹¹ for NSCs alone), and reduced C3+ reactive astrocytes in the striatum (PMID: 40874950).
- In a rotenone-induced PD model in diabetic rats, semaglutide significantly improved cognitive, locomotor, and olfactory functions, reduced oxidative-inflammatory stress biomarkers, downregulated caspase 3 and GFAP, and upregulated NRF2 protein expression (PMID: 40088335).
- A review of preclinical studies reported that semaglutide demonstrated neuroprotective effects in PD animal models through modulation of neuroinflammation, enhancement of mitochondrial function, promotion of neurogenesis, improvement of motor function, and amelioration of dopaminergic neuronal loss (PMID: 38921025).

**Supporting PMIDs:** [30741689](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30741689/), [30017231](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30017231/), [35873704](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35873704/), [40874950](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40874950/), [40088335](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40088335/)

### Eye Disorder

#### Literature — Eye Disorder

**Evidence strength:** weak, supports
**Relevant studies:** 5

Across multiple preclinical models, semaglutide consistently demonstrates protective effects against eye disorders including diabetic retinopathy, glaucoma-related retinal ganglion cell loss, and age-related dry eye disease. Semaglutide reduced retinal vascular permeability and basement membrane thickening in diabetic retinopathy models (PMID: 40749340, PMID: 40931416), protected retinal ganglion cells from metabolic insult (PMID: 41665299), and restored lacrimal gland structure and function in aged mice (PMID: 41812831). One study in an ocular hypertension model showed partial benefits (vascular and astrocytic improvements) without significant retinal ganglion cell survival benefit, yielding a mixed verdict at the paper level (PMID: 40597179), but this does not offset the otherwise consistent preclinical support across multiple eye conditions.

**Key findings:**
- Semaglutide pretreatment prevented rotenone-induced retinal ganglion cell loss in mice and enhanced glucose metabolism, suggesting neuroprotective potential in glaucoma (PMID: 41665299).
- Long-term semaglutide treatment significantly alleviated dry eye disease phenotypes and preserved lacrimal gland structural integrity in aged mice by reducing senescent cell burden and senescence-associated transcriptional programs (PMID: 41812831).
- Semaglutide reversed retinal basement membrane thickening and vascular leakage in diabetic mice by restoring endothelial AnxA2 expression and activating MMP-9-dependent extracellular matrix remodeling, an effect not replicated by insulin (PMID: 40749340).
- Semaglutide significantly reduced blood glucose, improved retinal function, alleviated neuronal apoptosis, reduced vascular lesions, and restored tight junction markers in a streptozotocin-induced diabetic retinopathy rat model (PMID: 40931416).
- In an ocular hypertension rat model, semaglutide delayed intraocular pressure elevation and improved astrocytic fractal dimension and vascular remodeling, but did not significantly improve retinal ganglion cell survival, yielding only partial neuroprotective effects (PMID: 40597179).

### Schizophrenia

#### Literature — Schizophrenia

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 1

The available evidence from a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial yields a mixed picture for repurposing semaglutide in schizophrenia. Semaglutide improved physical quality of life beyond the minimally important difference, with weight loss potentially mediating part of this benefit, but no meaningful effects were found on mental quality of life or psychiatric symptom severity (PANSS-6) (PMID: 41702353). The paper-level verdict is mixed, reflecting meaningful gains in physical outcomes alongside a lack of effect on core schizophrenia symptoms, making the overall evidence neither clearly supportive nor contradictory for the psychiatric indication.

**Key findings:**
- Semaglutide improved Physical Component Summary (PCS) scores at weeks 15 and 30 with effect sizes exceeding the minimally important difference in a 30-week randomized controlled trial of adults with schizophrenia spectrum disorder (PMID: 41702353).
- No total or mediated effects of semaglutide were found for the Mental Component Summary (MCS) score, indicating no improvement in mental quality of life (PMID: 41702353).
- No meaningful effects on psychiatric symptom severity as measured by the PANSS-6 were observed, either directly or mediated through weight loss (PMID: 41702353).
- Weight loss partially mediated the physical QoL benefit (approximately half of the total PCS effect at week 30), but this indirect effect was not statistically significant (PMID: 41702353).

### Alzheimer Disease

#### Literature — Alzheimer Disease

**Evidence strength:** strong, contradicts
**Relevant studies:** 15

The highest-quality evidence—two large phase 3 randomized controlled trials (evoke and evoke+, n=3808)—found that oral semaglutide 14 mg did not slow clinical progression in early Alzheimer's disease, with no significant difference in CDR-SB scores versus placebo at week 104 (PMID: 41865758; PMID: 42159155). Supporting signals come only from observational real-world studies in diabetic populations and a meta-analysis with high heterogeneity, which reflect prevention of incident AD in cognitively unimpaired patients rather than treatment of established disease, and cannot offset the consistent null results from controlled trials (PMID: 39445596; PMID: 40552638; PMID: 41766347). A post-hoc reanalysis suggesting limited effects at later time points is methodologically weaker and does not change the primary trial conclusion (PMID: 42261705).

**Key findings:**
- In the evoke trial, mean CDR-SB change was 2.3 in both semaglutide and placebo groups (estimated difference −0.08, 95% CI −0.35 to 0.20, p=0.57), and in evoke+ the difference was 0.10 (95% CI −0.17 to 0.38, p=0.46), indicating no clinical benefit of oral semaglutide 14 mg over 104 weeks in early Alzheimer's disease (PMID: 41865758).
- Both evoke and evoke+ trials were discontinued due to negative clinical outcomes, and the plain language summary confirmed that oral semaglutide did not slow cognitive or functional decline in early Alzheimer's disease (PMID: 42159155).
- A commentary on the evoke(+) trials noted that semaglutide produced statistically significant reductions in hsCRP and small CSF biomarker changes (7–10% reductions in YKL-40 and GFAP), but these did not translate into any correlative effect on AD clinical progression (PMID: 42785112).
- A post-hoc reanalysis of evoke/evoke+ data found a nominally significant difference on the ADCS-ADL-MCI test at week 130 (p=0.0039) and trends on ADAS-cog-13 at week 156, but these were unplanned secondary analyses at non-primary time points and the author acknowledged the trials were declared failures at the pre-specified primary endpoint (PMID: 42261705).
- A target trial emulation using US electronic health records of 1,094,761 T2DM patients found semaglutide associated with 40–70% reduced risk of first-time AD diagnosis compared to other antidiabetic medications, but this was an observational study in cognitively unimpaired diabetic patients, not a treatment trial in established AD (PMID: 39445596).
- A real-world target trial emulation in 1,710,995 T2DM patients showed semaglutide associated with reduced overall ADRD incidence (HR 0.54–0.80 vs. comparators), though this reflects incident dementia prevention in diabetic patients rather than treatment of symptomatic Alzheimer's disease (PMID: 40552638).
- A systematic review and meta-analysis of 23 studies (5 on brain/cognition) found neuroprotective signals and lower AD risk with semaglutide, but noted high heterogeneity across studies and the cognitive findings were largely driven by observational data (PMID: 41766347).
