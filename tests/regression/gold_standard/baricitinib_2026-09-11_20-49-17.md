## Findings by Disease

### Psoriasis

#### Literature — Psoriasis

**Evidence strength:** moderate, supports
**Relevant studies:** 1

A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 2b trial evaluated baricitinib in patients with moderate-to-severe psoriasis, finding that the 8 mg and 10 mg doses achieved statistically significant PASI-75 responses at 12 weeks compared to placebo (PMID: 26800231). The evidence supports repurposing baricitinib for psoriasis, though this indication is not currently FDA-approved (PMID: 26800231).

**Key findings:**
- At week 12, 43% of patients in the 8 mg baricitinib group and 54% in the 10 mg group achieved PASI-75, compared to 17% in the placebo group (P < 0.05) among North American patients (PMID: 26800231).
- Statistically significant PASI-90 responses were achieved in the 8 mg and 10 mg baricitinib groups at both 8 and 12 weeks (PMID: 26800231).
- More than 81% of PASI-75 responders maintained their scores through 24 weeks (PMID: 26800231).
- All baricitinib-treated groups had greater mean changes from baseline in PASI scores (P < 0.05) at 12 weeks compared to placebo (PMID: 26800231).
- No opportunistic infections were observed in any treatment group; dose-dependent changes in laboratory values were noted (PMID: 26800231).

**Supporting PMIDs:** [26800231](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26800231/)

### Systemic Lupus Erythematosus

#### Literature — Systemic Lupus Erythematosus

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 9

The evidence for baricitinib in SLE is mixed: baricitinib 4 mg met the primary SRI-4 endpoint in one phase 3 trial (SLE-BRAVE-I, PMID 36848918) and in the phase 2 trial (PMID 30043749), but failed to meet the primary endpoint in the second phase 3 trial (SLE-BRAVE-II, PMID 36848919), and neither trial met key secondary endpoints. Meta-analyses confirm a statistically significant benefit of baricitinib 4 mg on some disease activity measures (PMID 37950554, 37853751, 37581759), but the effect at week 52 did not consistently reach significance and was accompanied by a higher rate of serious adverse events and serious infections with the 4 mg dose (PMID 37853751, 37581759).

**Key findings:**
- In SLE-BRAVE-I, baricitinib 4 mg achieved a significantly higher SRI-4 response rate at week 52 (57%) versus placebo (46%; OR 1.57, p=0.016), but no major secondary endpoints were met (PMID 36848918).
- In SLE-BRAVE-II, baricitinib 4 mg failed to meet the primary SRI-4 endpoint at week 52 (47% vs 46% placebo; OR 1.07, p not significant), and no secondary endpoints were met (PMID 36848919).
- In the phase 2 trial, baricitinib 4 mg significantly improved resolution of arthritis or rash at week 24 (67% vs placebo; OR 1.8, p=0.0414), but the 2 mg dose did not (PMID 30043749).
- A meta-analysis of three RCTs found baricitinib 4 mg significantly improved SRI-4 at week 24 (RR=1.19, p<0.01) but not at week 52 (RR=1.13, p=0.15); baricitinib 2 mg showed no benefit at either timepoint (PMID 37581759).
- A meta-analysis found baricitinib 4 mg significantly reduced SLEDAI-2K score by ≥4 points (OR=1.407, p=0.003) and improved arthritis/rash remission (OR=1.327, p=0.014), but was associated with significantly higher rates of serious adverse events (OR=1.493, p=0.049) and serious infections (OR=2.303, p=0.019) compared to placebo (PMID 37853751).
- A meta-analysis of three RCTs found a beneficial effect of baricitinib 4 mg on SRI-4 (OR=1.370, p=0.006) but noted that trial sequential analysis indicated the need for additional RCTs to confirm the role of baricitinib in SLE (PMID 37950554).
- The safety profile of baricitinib in SLE was consistent with its known profile across all trials, with no new safety signals, though serious infections were more frequent with the 4 mg dose (PMID 36848918, 36848919, 30043749).

**Supporting PMIDs:** [36848918](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36848918/), [30043749](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30043749/), [37950554](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37950554/), [37581759](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37581759/), [37853751](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37853751/)

#### Clinical Trials

**Development stage:** Phase 3 completed for this indication
Baricitinib has been evaluated in systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) across multiple clinical trials. A completed Phase 2 study (NCT02708095) assessed baricitinib in SLE patients and provided early evidence informing the subsequent Phase 3 program. Two Phase 3 trials followed: SLE-BRAVE II (NCT03616964), which completed as planned, and a second Phase 3 study (NCT03616912) that was terminated by the sponsor due to insufficient evidence to support a positive benefit:risk profile in SLE patients. No active trials in this indication are currently on record.

THROMBOSIS Thrombosis, including deep venous thrombosis and pulmonary embolism, has been observed at an increased incidence in patients treated with OLUMIANT compared to placebo. In addition, there were cases of arterial thrombosis. Many of these adverse events were serious and some resulted in death. In RA patients 50 years of age and older with at least one cardiovascular risk factor treated with another JAK inhibitor, a higher rate of thrombosis was observed when compared with TNF blockers. Avoid OLUMIANT in patients at risk. Patients with symptoms of thrombosis should discontinue OLUMIANT and be promptly evaluated. [see Warnings and Precautions ( 5.5 )].
