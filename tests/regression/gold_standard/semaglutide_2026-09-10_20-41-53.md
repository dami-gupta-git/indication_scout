**Regulatory label:** FDA label boxed-warning text (5 product labels, LLM-summarized — see appendix for verbatim text):
Across all five labels, the shared core boxed warning is that semaglutide causes dose-dependent and treatment-duration-dependent thyroid C-cell tumors in rodents at clinically relevant exposures; whether this risk extends to humans, including medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC), has not been determined. All labels contraindicate semaglutide in patients with a personal or family history of MTC or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2), and all direct clinicians to counsel patients on MTC risk and symptoms such as a neck mass, dysphagia, dyspnea, or persistent hoarseness. All labels except WEGOVY's abbreviated summary statement also explicitly note that routine monitoring of serum calcitonin or thyroid ultrasound is of uncertain value for early MTC detection. One substantive difference in product scope exists: the first label (OZEMPIC) also names RYBELSUS (the oral tablet formulation of semaglutide) throughout its boxed warning text, applying the same contraindications and counseling requirements to that product as well, whereas the remaining four labels restrict their warnings solely to the injectable product each label covers (Ozempic or Wegovy).

## Findings by Disease

### Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

#### Literature — Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 5

Multiple randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled and crossover trials demonstrate that semaglutide improves glycemic control, reduces body weight, and lowers insulin requirements in adults with type 1 diabetes. In the ADJUST-T1D trial, 36% of semaglutide-treated patients versus 0% of placebo patients achieved the composite endpoint of time-in-range >70%, time-below-range <4%, and ≥5% weight reduction (PMID: 40550013). A crossover trial showed semaglutide increased time in target glucose range by a mean 4.8 percentage points versus placebo without increasing hypoglycemia (PMID: 39794615), and post-hoc analyses confirmed rapid, sustained reductions in total daily insulin dose and carbohydrate intake (PMID: 41429002, PMID: 41144928). The evidence supports repurposing semaglutide for type 1 diabetes, though gastrointestinal side effects and rare euglycemic ketosis were noted safety considerations (PMID: 39794615, PMID: 41175929).

**Key findings:**
- In a 26-week double-blind RCT (ADJUST-T1D), 36% of semaglutide-treated adults with type 1 diabetes and obesity achieved the composite endpoint (time-in-range >70%, time-below-range <4%, and ≥5% weight reduction) versus 0% in the placebo group (between-group difference 36 percentage points, 95% CI 20.6–52.2, P<0.001) (PMID: 40550013).
- Semaglutide reduced HbA1c by an additional 0.3 percentage points and body weight by 8.8 kg compared to placebo over 26 weeks in adults with type 1 diabetes and obesity (PMID: 40550013).
- In a randomized double-blind crossover trial, semaglutide increased time in target glucose range (3.9–10.0 mmol/L) by a mean 4.8 percentage points versus placebo (P=0.006) without increasing time below 3.9 mmol/L (P=0.19) in adults with type 1 diabetes (PMID: 39794615).
- Two episodes of recurrent euglycemic ketosis without acidosis occurred during semaglutide use in the crossover trial, and no diabetic ketoacidosis or severe hypoglycemia was observed (PMID: 39794615).
- Post-hoc analysis of ADJUST-T1D showed semaglutide produced a significant 22.6% reduction in total daily insulin dose over 26 weeks, driven more by bolus (-30.5%) than basal (-15.6%) insulin reductions, with early effects largely independent of weight loss (PMID: 41429002).
- Daily carbohydrate intake decreased from 137 g at baseline to 107 g at 26 weeks in the semaglutide group in adults with type 1 diabetes and obesity (PMID: 41429002).
- In a post-hoc analysis of a crossover trial, total and bolus insulin requirements were significantly reduced by day 7 of semaglutide initiation in adults with type 1 diabetes on insulin pump therapy, while basal insulin was significantly reduced by day 32 (PMID: 41144928).
- In qualitative analyses from a randomized crossover trial, 42% of participants with type 1 diabetes expressed interest in continuing semaglutide outside the study, citing lower insulin requirements, weight loss, and improved glycemic management, while the Diabetes Bowel Symptom Questionnaire showed increased gastrointestinal symptom frequency and severity with semaglutide versus placebo (PMID: 41175929).

### Metabolic Dysfunction-associated Steatohepatitis

#### Literature — Metabolic Dysfunction-associated Steatohepatitis

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 7

The preponderance of evidence from controlled trials supports semaglutide for treating MASH/NASH, with the highest-quality evidence coming from a large phase 3 RCT showing significant improvements in both steatohepatitis resolution and fibrosis reduction (PMID: 40305708). A phase 2 RCT confirmed NASH resolution benefit though not fibrosis improvement (PMID: 33185364), and a phase 2 combination trial found a nominally significant fibrosis benefit for semaglutide monotherapy (PMID: 42456707). The one clearly contradicting trial (PMID: 36934740) was conducted in a distinct, more advanced population (compensated cirrhosis/F4), limiting its generalizability to the broader MASH population with F2–F3 fibrosis.

**Key findings:**
- In a phase 3 RCT of 800 patients with MASH and fibrosis stage F2–F3, semaglutide 2.4 mg achieved steatohepatitis resolution without fibrosis worsening in 62.9% vs. 34.3% with placebo (P<0.001), and fibrosis reduction without steatohepatitis worsening in 36.8% vs. 22.4% (P<0.001) (PMID: 40305708).
- In a phase 2 RCT of 320 NASH patients (F1–F3), semaglutide 0.4 mg achieved NASH resolution in 59% vs. 17% with placebo (P<0.001), but did not show a significant difference in fibrosis stage improvement (43% vs. 33%, P=0.48) (PMID: 33185364).
- In a phase 2 combination trial of MASH patients with F2–F4c fibrosis, semaglutide 2.4 mg monotherapy achieved the primary endpoint (fibrosis improvement without MASH worsening) in 30% vs. 16% with placebo (nominally significant, P=0.024) (PMID: 42456707).
- In a phase 2 RCT of patients with NASH-related compensated cirrhosis (F4), semaglutide 2.4 mg did not significantly improve fibrosis (11% vs. 29% with placebo, p=0.087), representing a contradicting result in this advanced subpopulation (PMID: 36934740).
- In a randomized trial using MRI endpoints in NAFLD patients, semaglutide significantly reduced liver steatosis (estimated treatment ratio 0.47, P<0.0001 at week 48) but did not significantly improve liver stiffness by MRE compared to placebo (PMID: 34570916).

### Stroke

#### Literature — Stroke

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 4

The totality of evidence supports semaglutide for stroke prevention and treatment. Pooled post-hoc analysis of two randomized cardiovascular outcome trials showed semaglutide significantly reduced first stroke incidence (HR 0.68, p=0.048) versus placebo in type 2 diabetes patients at high cardiovascular risk (PMID: 35582947), and a systematic review and meta-analysis of 17 studies found a 32% reduction in nonfatal stroke risk (HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.56–0.83) (PMID: 41188987). A phase 2 randomized trial in acute large vessel occlusion showed no significant overall benefit on 90-day functional outcome but suggested possible benefit in patients not receiving thrombolysis, with a mixed verdict reflecting uncertainty in the acute treatment setting rather than contradicting the prevention evidence (PMID: 41392086), while preclinical data further support biological plausibility (PMID: 31465784).

**Key findings:**
- Semaglutide reduced incidence of any first stroke versus placebo (0.8 vs. 1.1 events/100 PYO; HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.46–1.00; p=0.048) in pooled post-hoc analysis of SUSTAIN 6 and PIONEER 6 randomized trials, driven primarily by reduction in small-vessel occlusion (HR 0.51, p=0.017) (PMID: 35582947).
- A systematic review and meta-analysis of 17 studies (n=40,632) found semaglutide reduced nonfatal stroke risk by 32% (HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.56–0.83) and MACE by 18% (HR 0.82, 95% CI 0.75–0.90) in type 2 diabetes patients (PMID: 41188987).
- A phase 2 randomized trial of semaglutide in acute large vessel occlusion patients undergoing endovascular therapy found no significant difference in favorable neurological recovery at 90 days overall (56.5% vs. 54.9%; adjusted RR 1.05, p=0.37), though an exploratory subgroup suggested benefit in patients not receiving intravenous thrombolysis (adjusted RR 1.18, 95% CI 1.02–1.36) (PMID: 41392086).

The only available clinical trial evidence is a small phase 2a RCT (PMID: 42189538) that yielded mixed results: semaglutide failed to meet its co-primary endpoints of laboratory smoking resistance and weekly cigarettes per day, yet did significantly reduce nicotine craving and body weight. ### Liver Disorder

#### Literature — Liver Disorder

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 2

Evidence on semaglutide for liver disorder is mixed, with a large phase 3 RCT (PMID: 40305708) demonstrating significant improvements in steatohepatitis resolution and fibrosis reduction in patients with MASH and fibrosis stage 2 or 3, while a phase 2 RCT (PMID: 36934740) found no significant improvement in fibrosis or NASH resolution in patients with NASH-related compensated cirrhosis. The divergence likely reflects differences in disease stage and population, with positive results in moderate fibrosis (F2/F3) but not in advanced cirrhotic disease, representing comparably credible controlled evidence pointing in opposite directions.

**Key findings:**
- In a phase 3 double-blind RCT of 800 patients with MASH and fibrosis stage 2 or 3, semaglutide 2.4 mg achieved steatohepatitis resolution without fibrosis worsening in 62.9% vs 34.3% with placebo (p<0.001) (PMID: 40305708).
- In the same phase 3 trial, reduction in liver fibrosis without worsening of steatohepatitis occurred in 36.8% of semaglutide patients vs 22.4% with placebo (p<0.001) (PMID: 40305708).
- In a phase 2 double-blind RCT of 71 patients with NASH-related compensated cirrhosis, semaglutide 2.4 mg did not significantly improve fibrosis by one stage or more without NASH worsening compared to placebo (11% vs 29%; p=0.087) (PMID: 36934740).
- There was also no significant difference in NASH resolution between semaglutide and placebo groups in patients with cirrhosis (p=0.29) (PMID: 36934740).
- Mean body weight reduction was significantly greater with semaglutide (-10.5%) than placebo (-2.0%) in the phase 3 MASH trial (PMID: 40305708).
- Gastrointestinal adverse events, including nausea (45% vs 17%) and vomiting (17% vs 0%), were more common with semaglutide than placebo in the cirrhosis trial, though no new safety signals emerged (PMID: 36934740).

### Coronary Artery Disorder

#### Literature — Coronary Artery Disorder

**Evidence strength:** weak
**Relevant studies:** 1

One modeling study applied risk reduction estimates from the SELECT trial to MESA participants without diabetes or established cardiovascular disease, finding that semaglutide allocation guided by coronary artery calcium (CAC) scoring could substantially reduce major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) in those with high CAC scores (PMID: 39797878). The analysis showed that individuals with CAC ≥300 had a 2.2-fold higher risk for MACE compared to CAC = 0, and the 5-year number-needed-to-treat for MACE was 79 for CAC ≥300 versus 653 for CAC = 0, suggesting semaglutide may provide meaningful benefit for coronary artery disorder risk reduction in high-CAC individuals (PMID: 39797878). The evidence supports repurposing semaglutide for coronary artery disorder prevention, though it is based on a single observational modeling study rather than a direct interventional trial.

**Key findings:**
- CAC ≥300 conferred a 2.2-fold higher risk for MACE compared to CAC = 0 (HR: 2.16 [95% CI: 1.57-2.99]; P < 0.001) in MESA participants eligible for semaglutide (PMID: 39797878).
- The 5-year number-needed-to-treat for MACE with semaglutide was 79 for individuals with CAC ≥300 versus 653 for those with CAC = 0, indicating substantially greater absolute benefit in high-CAC individuals (PMID: 39797878).
- CAC ≥300 was also associated with higher risks for heart failure (HR: 2.80), chronic kidney disease (HR: 1.59), and all-cause mortality (HR: 1.35) compared to CAC = 0, with correspondingly lower NNTs for semaglutide benefit across these outcomes (PMID: 39797878).
- The study modeled semaglutide allocation in 3,129 MESA participants without diabetes or clinical CVD who met BMI criteria, applying SELECT trial risk reduction estimates to observed incidence rates (PMID: 39797878).

### Heart Failure

#### Literature — Heart Failure

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 11

Across multiple randomized controlled trials and pooled analyses, semaglutide consistently improved heart failure-related symptoms, physical limitations, exercise function, and body weight compared to placebo in patients with obesity-related HFpEF, with and without type 2 diabetes (PMIDs: 38587233, 37622681, 38599221). A pooled post-hoc analysis further demonstrated that semaglutide significantly reduced the composite of cardiovascular death or worsening heart failure events (HR 0.69) and worsening heart failure events alone (HR 0.59) in patients with HFpEF across four randomized trials (PMID: 39222642), and a dedicated renal outcomes trial in T2D and CKD showed semaglutide reduced HF events and CV death regardless of baseline HF status (PMID: 39217553). These benefits were consistent across age, sex, baseline HbA1c, diuretic use, and baseline exercise function subgroups, and were accompanied by favorable cardiac structural remodeling and a lower rate of serious adverse events with semaglutide versus placebo (PMIDs: 41290376, 38913003, 39848268, 38739118, 39217567, 41045908).

**Key findings:**
- In the STEP-HFpEF trial (n=529, obesity-related HFpEF without T2D), semaglutide improved KCCQ-CSS by an estimated 7.8 points more than placebo (P<0.001), reduced body weight by 10.7 percentage points more (P<0.001), improved 6-minute walk distance by 20.3 m (P<0.001), and produced a win ratio of 1.72 on the hierarchical composite endpoint (P<0.001) (PMID: 37622681).
- In the STEP-HFpEF DM trial (n=616, obesity-related HFpEF with T2D), semaglutide improved KCCQ-CSS by an estimated 7.3 points more than placebo (P<0.001), reduced body weight by 6.4 percentage points more (P<0.001), improved 6-minute walk distance by 14.3 m (P=0.008), and produced a win ratio of 1.58 on the hierarchical composite endpoint (P<0.001) (PMID: 38587233).
- In a prespecified pooled analysis of STEP-HFpEF and STEP-HFpEF DM (n=1145), semaglutide improved KCCQ-CSS by 7.5 points (P<0.0001), reduced body weight by 8.4% (P<0.0001), improved 6-minute walk distance by 17.1 m (P<0.0001), and produced a win ratio of 1.65 on the hierarchical composite endpoint (P<0.0001), with serious adverse events reported in 161 vs. 301 participants (PMID: 38599221).
- A post-hoc pooled analysis of four randomized trials (SELECT, FLOW, STEP-HFpEF, STEP-HFpEF DM; n=3743 with HFpEF history) found semaglutide reduced the composite of cardiovascular death or worsening heart failure events (HR 0.69, 95% CI 0.53–0.89; P=0.0045) and worsening heart failure events alone (HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.41–0.82; P=0.0019), though the effect on cardiovascular death alone was not statistically significant (HR 0.82; P=0.25) (PMID: 39222642).
- In the FLOW trial (n=3533, T2D and CKD), semaglutide reduced the composite of HF events or CV death (HR 0.73; P=0.0005), HF events alone (HR 0.73; P=0.0068), and CV death alone (HR 0.71; P=0.0036), with consistent effects regardless of baseline HF history (PMID: 39217553).
- In an echocardiography substudy of the STEP-HFpEF Program (n=491), semaglutide attenuated left atrial volume progression (estimated mean difference -6.13 mL; P=0.0013), reduced right ventricular end-diastolic area (P=0.016), and improved E/A ratio and E-wave velocity compared to placebo (PMID: 39217567).
- In a prespecified pooled subanalysis of the STEP-HFpEF Program across age groups (<55, 55–64, 65–74, ≥75 years), semaglutide's efficacy on KCCQ-CSS (P-interaction=0.80) and body weight (P-interaction=0.41) was consistent across the age spectrum, with no treatment effect heterogeneity for secondary endpoints (PMID: 41290376).
- In a prespecified analysis of pooled STEP-HFpEF data by sex (n=1145; 570 women), semaglutide improved KCCQ-CSS similarly in women (+7.6 points) and men (+7.5 points; P-interaction=0.94), with greater weight reduction in women (-9.6% vs. -7.2%; P-interaction=0.006) and consistent improvements in 6-minute walk distance and the hierarchical composite endpoint regardless of sex (PMID: 38913003).
- In a prespecified analysis of pooled STEP-HFpEF data by baseline diuretic use, semaglutide consistently reduced body weight across all diuretic categories (interaction P=0.39), reduced loop diuretic dose by 17% vs. a 2.4% increase with placebo (P<0.0001), and was more likely to result in loop diuretic dose reduction (OR 2.67; P<0.001) (PMID: 38739118).

- In a prespecified secondary analysis of pooled STEP-HFpEF Program data, semaglutide improved 6-minute walk distance vs. placebo at 20 weeks (treatment difference 14.6 m; P<0.0001) and 52 weeks (17.1 m; P<0.0001), with consistent effects across all subgroups and a strong correlation between greater weight loss and greater improvement in 6MWD (PMID: 41045908).

#### Literature — Myocardial Infarction

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 2

The evidence for repurposing semaglutide to treat or prevent myocardial infarction is mixed. In SUSTAIN-6, subcutaneous semaglutide reduced the composite MACE endpoint significantly, but the reduction in nonfatal MI specifically (2.9% vs 3.9%, HR 0.74) did not reach statistical significance (P=0.12) (PMID: 27633186). ### Hyperglycemia

#### Literature — Hyperglycemia

**Evidence strength:** moderate, supports
**Relevant studies:** 2

Two clinical studies provide evidence that semaglutide reduces hyperglycemia (as measured by fasting plasma glucose and HbA1c) in humans. In a small controlled case series of individuals with spinal cord injury and abnormal carbohydrate metabolism, 26 weeks of semaglutide reduced fasting plasma glucose by 17 mg/dL and HbA1c by 0.2% compared to increases in controls (PMID: 37158751). **Key findings:**

- In a double-blinded RCT of antipsychotic-treated patients with schizophrenia and prediabetes, semaglutide reduced HbA1c by 0.46% (95% CI, -0.56% to -0.36%) compared to placebo over 30 weeks (PMID: 40900607).
- 81% of semaglutide-treated patients achieved HbA1c <5.7% (normoglycemia) versus 19% of placebo-treated patients (P < .001) in the HISTORI trial (PMID: 40900607).
- Semaglutide also improved HDL cholesterol by 10.81 mg/dL and reduced triglycerides by 29.20 mg/dL in the RCT population with prediabetes (PMID: 40900607).

### Eye Disorder

#### Literature — Eye Disorder

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 3

The evidence is mixed overall. Preclinical studies support semaglutide's potential therapeutic benefit for retinal ganglion cell protection in glaucoma models (PMID: 41665299) and age-related dry eye disease via lacrimal gland restoration (PMID: 41812831), but the highest-quality clinical evidence from a large systematic review and meta-analysis of 78 trials finds no benefit for eye disorders or diabetic retinopathy, and raises a safety signal for NAION (PMID: 40810985). The positive preclinical findings cannot offset the null-to-concerning clinical evidence from controlled trials.

**Key findings:**
- A systematic review and meta-analysis of 78 RCTs (73,640 participants) found semaglutide did not increase or reduce the risk of eye disorders (OR 1.01; 95% CI 0.91–1.12) or diabetic retinopathy (OR 1.04; 95% CI 0.92–1.17) (PMID: 40810985).
- The same meta-analysis identified a significant association between semaglutide and NAION (OR 3.92; 95% CI 1.02–15.02), though trial sequential analysis indicated insufficient sample size for definitive conclusions on NAION (PMID: 40810985).
- In a mouse model, semaglutide pretreatment prevented rotenone-induced retinal ganglion cell loss and enhanced glucose metabolism, suggesting a potential neuroprotective role in glaucoma (PMID: 41665299).
- In a naturally aged mouse model, long-term semaglutide treatment significantly alleviated dry eye disease phenotypes, preserved lacrimal gland structural integrity, reduced senescent cell burden, and attenuated senescence-associated inflammatory programs (PMID: 41812831).

### Alzheimer Disease

#### Literature — Alzheimer Disease

**Evidence strength:** strong, contradicts
**Relevant studies:** 5

The highest-quality evidence—two large phase 3 randomised controlled trials (evoke and evoke+, n=3808)—found that oral semaglutide did not slow clinical progression in early Alzheimer's disease, with no significant difference in CDR-SB scores versus placebo at week 104 (PMID: 41865758). Preclinical evidence in the 5xFAD mouse model similarly showed no effect of semaglutide on memory, amyloid-β plaque deposition, or glial activation (PMID: 42413499). Positive signals are limited to a single preclinical mechanistic study in 3xTg mice (PMID: 37730113), a post-hoc reanalysis of secondary endpoints from the failed trials that did not alter the primary negative conclusion (PMID: 42261705), and a meta-analysis of largely observational and heterogeneous data (PMID: 41766347); none of these offset the definitive negative controlled clinical evidence.

**Key findings:**
- In two phase 3 RCTs (evoke and evoke+, n=3808), oral semaglutide 14 mg showed no significant difference versus placebo in CDR-SB score change at week 104 (estimated difference −0.08 [95% CI −0.35 to 0.20], p=0.57 in evoke; 0.10 [−0.17 to 0.38], p=0.46 in evoke+); both trials were discontinued due to negative clinical outcomes (PMID: 41865758).
- In the 5xFAD mouse model, chronic semaglutide treatment—even when initiated before overt pathology—produced no measurable effects on memory, learning, amyloid-β plaque deposition, or glial cell activation (PMID: 42413499).
- A post-hoc reanalysis of the evoke/evoke+ data found a statistically significant difference on the ADCS-ADL-MCI test at week 130 (p=0.0039) and trends on ADAS-cog-13 at week 156, but these were secondary, unplanned analyses and did not reverse the primary negative verdict (PMID: 42261705).
- A systematic review and meta-analysis reported neuroprotective effects and lower AD risk with semaglutide compared to various treatments, but noted high heterogeneity across included studies and relied heavily on observational and non-clinical data (PMID: 41766347).
- In the 3xTg mouse model, semaglutide improved cognition, reduced Aβ plaques and neurofibrillary tangles, and improved brain glucose metabolism via the GLP-1R/SIRT1/GLUT4 pathway, but this is preclinical mechanistic evidence only (PMID: 37730113).

**Supporting PMIDs:** [41766347](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41766347/), [42261705](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42261705/), [37730113](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37730113/)
