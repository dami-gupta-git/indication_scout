## Findings by Disease

### Heart Failure

#### Literature — Heart Failure

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 7

The evidence is mixed, with several small randomized controlled trials demonstrating improvements in exercise capacity, hemodynamics, diastolic function, and cardiac geometry with sildenafil in systolic heart failure (PMIDs 16275194, 18036451, 18381181, 21036891), while larger or more rigorous trials found no significant benefit on symptoms, exercise capacity, or quality of life in HFrEF with pulmonary hypertension (PMID 35596935) or HFpEF (PMID 25782985), and one trial found no improvement in blood pressure or 6-minute walk distance (PMID 23241003). The positive studies are generally small, single-center, and may be underpowered, whereas the contradicting evidence comes from multicenter or more adequately powered trials, creating a genuine conflict between comparably credible bodies of evidence.

**Key findings:**
- Sildenafil 50 mg acutely increased cardiac index by 0.37 L/min/m² and reduced total systemic resistance by 479 dynes·s·cm⁻⁵ in a randomized crossover study of 20 HFrEF patients (PMID 16275194).
- Six months of sildenafil 50 mg twice daily significantly improved peak VO2 (14.8 to 18.7 mL/min/kg), VE/VCO2 slope, and endothelial function in a 46-patient RCT of CHF patients (PMID 18036451).
- Four weeks of sildenafil 50 mg three times daily improved maximal oxygen uptake, ventilatory efficiency, and reduced pulmonary artery systolic pressure in a double-blind placebo-controlled trial of 19 CHF patients (PMID 18381181).
- One year of sildenafil 50 mg three times daily improved LV ejection fraction, diastolic function (E/E' ratio from 13.1 to 9.4), LV mass index, and quality of life in a 45-patient RCT of systolic HF patients (PMID 21036891).
- The multicenter SilHF trial (n=69 HFrEF patients with pulmonary hypertension) found sildenafil did not improve symptoms, quality of life, PASP, or 6-minute walk distance compared to placebo, and those on sildenafil had numerically more serious adverse events (33% vs. 21%) (PMID 35596935).
- A 12-week RCT of 106 systolic HF patients found sildenafil did not significantly reduce mean blood pressure (primary endpoint) or improve 6-minute walk distance (+14 m, P=0.67) compared to placebo (PMID 23241003).
- In a substudy of the RELAX trial (n=48 HFpEF patients), sildenafil reduced arterial elastance but also reduced LV contractility by 11–16%, with no effect on pulmonary artery systolic pressure, suggesting opposing vascular and ventricular effects (PMID 25782985).

### Raynaud Disease

#### Literature — Raynaud Disease

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 4

The overall body of evidence supports sildenafil as an efficacious treatment for Raynaud's phenomenon, with the majority of studies demonstrating improvements in attack frequency, duration, blood flow, and symptom severity compared to placebo or baseline. Two controlled trials showed statistically significant reductions in attack frequency and improved microvascular blood flow (PMID: 21360507; PMID: 28281457), and an uncontrolled pilot study reported significant symptom improvement (PMID: 22833239). The two mixed-verdict studies acknowledge efficacy signals but note limitations such as modest aggregate effect sizes or heterogeneous patient responses (PMID: 30383134; PMID: 21360507), which do not negate the net positive direction across the evidence base.

**Key findings:**
- A double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT found that modified-release sildenafil produced a significantly greater mean percentage reduction in Raynaud's attack frequency compared to placebo (-44.0% vs. -18.1%, P=0.034) in patients with limited cutaneous systemic sclerosis (PMID: 21360507).
- A double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT demonstrated that sildenafil 100 mg/day for 8 weeks significantly improved finger blood flow before and after cold stimulus (p=0.026 and p=0.028, respectively) and reduced duration of RP attacks and VAS severity scores compared to placebo in SSc patients (PMID: 28281457).
- An n-of-1 randomized, double-blind trial series found a greater than 90% probability that sildenafil at 40 mg or 80 mg was superior to placebo for most outcomes, though aggregate effect sizes were not clinically relevant and substantial inter-patient heterogeneity was observed (PMID: 30383134).
- An open-label, uncontrolled pilot study reported statistically significant improvement in severity of Raynaud's phenomenon after 3 months of oral sildenafil 25 mg three times daily, with no new digital infarcts and healing of existing ulcers in SSc patients (PMID: 22833239).

**Supporting PMIDs:** [30383134](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30383134/), [21360507](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21360507/), [28281457](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28281457/), [22833239](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22833239/)

### Infertility

#### Literature — Infertility

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 3

The evidence on sildenafil for treating infertility (specifically thin endometrium and implantation failure) is mixed. A systematic review and meta-analysis of nine studies found significantly improved endometrial thickness, clinical pregnancy rate, and biochemical pregnancy rate with sildenafil (PMID: 33176524), but a randomized controlled trial found no significant improvement in endometrial thickness or pregnancy outcomes in repeated IVF failure patients (PMID: 31710189), and another controlled study found sildenafil failed to improve endometrial thickness compared to placebo (PMID: 15266759). Comparably credible evidence thus points in both directions with no clear net resolution.

**Key findings:**
- A meta-analysis of 9 studies (n=1452) found sildenafil significantly increased endometrial thickness (WMD: 1.22; 95% CI: 1.07–1.38), improved clinical pregnancy rate (RR: 1.31; 95% CI: 1.11–1.53), and improved biochemical pregnancy rate (RR: 1.45; 95% CI: 1.11–1.89) compared to control (PMID: 33176524).
- A randomized placebo-controlled trial in women with repeated IVF/ICSI failures found no statistically significant difference in endometrial thickness on hCG injection day between sildenafil and placebo groups, and the observed increase in chemical pregnancy rate with sildenafil was clinically meaningful but statistically non-significant due to small sample size (PMID: 31710189).
- A randomized controlled study found that neither vaginal sildenafil nor vaginal estradiol significantly improved endometrial thickness or blood flow in women with a history of thin endometria, failing to corroborate prior positive claims for sildenafil (PMID: 15266759).

**Supporting PMIDs:** [33176524](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33176524/)

**Contradicting PMIDs:** [15266759](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15266759/), [31710189](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31710189/)

⚠️ **Indication-specific safety:** Disease-scoped literature for sildenafil in infertility reported: Increased adverse effects (notably headache) attributable to sildenafil in women treated for unexplained infertility — "More women in the study group reported adverse effects compared with the control group (17 [42.5%] vs 9 [22.5%]; P=0.034), with headache the most common adverse effect in the study group, reported by 8 (20.0%) patients."; Mild headache attributed to sildenafil — "Most people who take sildenafil end up with a mild headache." (PMIDs: [32286686](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32286686/), [37241011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37241011/)).

### Covid-19

#### Literature — Covid-19

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 2

The available evidence from two small randomized controlled trials points in a generally positive direction for sildenafil in COVID-19 but with inconsistent statistical significance across endpoints, warranting a mixed verdict. One trial found significant reductions in mortality and intubation risk only after PAH adjustment, with a non-significant trend on unadjusted primary endpoints (PMID: 37243243), while a pilot trial found no improvement in primary oxygenation parameters but did show significant reductions in invasive mechanical ventilation requirement and hospital length of stay (PMID: 34980198). Both studies are small and limited in generalizability, and the pattern of selectively significant secondary outcomes without consistent primary endpoint significance prevents a clear positive or negative conclusion.

**Key findings:**
- In a double-blind RCT of 150 ICU patients with severe COVID-19 and PAH, sildenafil reduced one-week mortality (4% vs. 13.3%, p=0.078) and intubation rate (8% vs. 18.7%, p=0.09) without reaching statistical significance on unadjusted primary endpoints, but significantly reduced ICU stay (15 vs. 19 days, p<0.001) and significantly reduced mortality and intubation risk after PAH adjustment (OR=0.21 and OR=0.26, respectively) (PMID: 37243243).
- In a pilot triple-blinded RCT of 40 COVID-19 inpatients with perfusion mismatch on sCTA, sildenafil showed no significant difference in primary oxygenation outcomes (PaO2/FiO2 ratio and A-a gradient, p=0.67 and p=0.69) (PMID: 34980198).
- In the same pilot RCT, no patients in the sildenafil arm required invasive mechanical ventilation compared to 4 in the placebo arm (logrank p=0.04), and sildenafil patients had a significantly shorter median hospital stay (9 vs. 12 days, p=0.04) (PMID: 34980198).
- Both trials are small (n=150 and n=40), limiting the strength of conclusions, and both authors called for larger confirmatory studies (PMID: 37243243, PMID: 34980198).

### Hypertensive Disorder

#### Literature — Hypertensive Disorder

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 4

The preponderance of controlled trial evidence supports sildenafil as an effective antihypertensive agent. Two randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trials demonstrated significant reductions in ambulatory and clinic blood pressure in hypertensive patients (PMID: 16940217, PMID: 32366927), and a proof-of-concept study confirmed acute BP-lowering effects in treatment-resistant hypertension (PMID: 20479335). The single contradicting study (PMID: 27873388) focused on a specialized HFpEF/pulmonary hypertension population and did not refute sildenafil's antihypertensive properties, as it actually observed reduced peak blood pressure with sildenafil versus placebo.

**Key findings:**
- In a randomized, double-blind, crossover trial of 22 hypertensive subjects, sildenafil 50 mg three times daily for 16 days significantly reduced daytime ambulatory systolic BP by 8 mmHg and diastolic BP by 6 mmHg versus placebo (PMID: 16940217).
- Sildenafil also significantly reduced clinic systolic BP by 5 mmHg and diastolic BP by 5 mmHg compared with placebo in the same crossover trial (PMID: 16940217).
- In a randomized crossover placebo-controlled trial of 50 hypertensive patients with erectile dysfunction, one month of sildenafil 50 mg twice daily significantly reduced 24-hour ambulatory diastolic blood pressure (P=0.0002) as well as daytime and nighttime diastolic BP (PMID: 32366927).
- In a proof-of-concept study of 6 treatment-resistant hypertension patients, sildenafil 50 mg alone produced acute brachial and central aortic BP reductions, and the combination of sildenafil with isosorbide mononitrate produced a maximum brachial BP reduction of 26/18 mmHg versus placebo (PMID: 20479335).
- In a 12-week randomized trial of HFpEF patients with pulmonary hypertension, sildenafil did not improve cardiac structure, function, or quality of life versus placebo, and the VE/VCO2 slope improved in the placebo but not sildenafil group, representing a negative outcome in this specialized population (PMID: 27873388).
- Despite the overall negative findings in HFpEF, sildenafil did reduce peak exercise blood pressure by 13.8/7.3 mmHg versus placebo in that same trial, consistent with its antihypertensive mechanism (PMID: 27873388).

### Alzheimer Disease

#### Literature — Alzheimer Disease

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 9

Multiple large observational studies and meta-analyses consistently show that sildenafil use is associated with a significantly reduced risk of developing Alzheimer's disease (AD), with hazard ratios ranging from approximately 0.31 to 0.47 across studies (PMID: 35572351, PMID: 40096550, PMID: 38427489, PMID: 37203519, PMID: 38795271). Preclinical studies in transgenic AD mouse models and patient-derived iPSC neurons demonstrate mechanistic plausibility, including reductions in amyloid-beta levels, tau hyperphosphorylation, neuroinflammation, and improvements in cognitive performance (PMID: 23685322, PMID: 25063079, PMID: 32959702, PMID: 38728184, PMID: 32467879). A small uncontrolled human study also showed that a single dose of sildenafil acutely increased cerebral blood flow and cerebral metabolic rate of oxygen in AD patients (PMID: 29036811); however, all human evidence to date is observational and no randomized controlled trial has been conducted, so causality remains unestablished.

**Key findings:**
- Sildenafil usage was associated with a 69% reduced risk of AD (HR=0.31, 95% CI 0.25-0.39, P<1.0×10-8) in a retrospective pharmacoepidemiologic analysis of 7.23 million individuals, and sildenafil increased neurite growth and decreased phospho-tau in AD patient iPSC-derived neurons (PMID: 35572351).
- A meta-analysis of 5 studies comprising 885,380 patients found sildenafil use was associated with a two-fold reduced risk of developing AD (HR: 0.47, 95% CI: 0.27-0.82, p<0.001) (PMID: 40096550).
- A systematic review of preclinical evidence found that sildenafil rescued CREB phosphorylation and memory deficits, reduced Aβ levels, suppressed neuroinflammation, decreased tau hyperphosphorylation, and increased neurogenesis in transgenic AD mouse models (PMID: 32467879).
- Sildenafil reversed neuropathological AD phenotype in PSEN1 E280A cholinergic-like neurons, reducing intracellular Aβ fragments by 87%, decreasing tau phosphorylation at Ser202/Thr205 by 35%, and downregulating apoptotic markers (PMID: 38728184).
- Sildenafil (7.5 mg/kg for 4 weeks) attenuated learning and memory impairments in SAMP8 mice and reduced BACE1 and cathepsin B levels, leading to decreased hippocampal Aβ42 levels (PMID: 25063079).
- In an aluminum-induced AD rat model, sildenafil ameliorated histopathological changes, decreased VCAM-1, TNF-α, and oxidative stress markers, and increased VEGF-A and neurogenesis marker nestin (PMID: 32959702).
- A single oral dose of 50 mg sildenafil significantly increased global cerebral blood flow (p=0.03) and cerebral metabolic rate of oxygen (p=0.05) in 12 AD patients, with the most pronounced CBF increases in bilateral medial temporal lobes (PMID: 29036811).
- Sildenafil reversed memory deficits and reduced soluble Aβ1-40 and Aβ1-42 levels in the hippocampus of 15-month-old Tg APP/PS1 mice via cGMP/PKG/pCREB signaling and anti-inflammatory mechanisms (PMID: 23685322).
- Real-world patient data analysis showed sildenafil usage was associated with a 54% reduced incidence of AD (HR=0.46, 95% CI 0.32-0.66) compared to spironolactone in MarketScan, and sildenafil reduced tau hyperphosphorylation (pTau181 and pTau205) dose-dependently in both familial and sporadic AD patient iPSC-derived neurons (PMID: 38427489).
- A meta-analysis of 6 studies (8,337,313 participants) found PDE5 inhibitor use was associated with a significantly reduced risk of AD (HR: 0.53, 95% CI: 0.32-0.86, p=0.008), with sildenafil specifically showing significant risk reduction (HR: 0.46, 95% CI: 0.31-0.70, p<0.001) (PMID: 38795271).
- Analysis of the IBM MarketScan Database (>30 million individuals) showed sildenafil use was associated with a 60% risk reduction of developing AD (HR=0.40; 95% CI: 0.38-0.44; P<.0001), with significant associations in both male and female subgroups (PMID: 37203519).

### Lower Urinary Tract Symptom

#### Literature — Lower Urinary Tract Symptom

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 11

The preponderance of evidence from randomized controlled trials and controlled studies supports sildenafil as effective for reducing lower urinary tract symptoms. Multiple placebo-controlled trials demonstrated significant improvements in IPSS scores with sildenafil versus placebo (PMIDs 17296414, 28590828, 18597830), and a self-controlled trial showed sildenafil improved IPSS, PVR, and QoL comparably or better than FDA-approved tadalafil for BPH (PMID 37245088). **Key findings:**
- In a 12-week double-blind placebo-controlled RCT, sildenafil significantly improved IPSS (-6.32 vs -1.93, p<0.0001) and BPH Impact Index (-2.0 vs -0.9, p<0.0001) versus placebo, though urinary flow rates did not differ significantly (PMID 17296414).
- A post hoc analysis of the same placebo-controlled trial confirmed sildenafil improved LUTS independent of baseline BMI or LUTS severity, with significant IPSS improvements across all BMI subgroups (p≤0.02), though maximal urinary flow rate did not change significantly (PMID 18597830).
- A multicenter double-blind RCT found sildenafil 25 mg qd and 25 mg bid significantly improved IPSS versus placebo (p=0.020 and p=0.025, respectively), and sildenafil 50 mg qd specifically improved nocturia significantly versus placebo (p=0.027); Qmax and PVR did not change significantly in any group (PMID 28590828).
- A randomized controlled trial of ureteral stent patients found sildenafil significantly reduced total USSQ scores and all domains except work performance versus control (p<0.001), with less dysuria and urgency in the sildenafil group (PMID 29766318).
- A single-arm self-controlled trial showed sildenafil significantly improved PVR (p<0.001), IPSS (p<0.001), and IPSS-QoL index (p<0.001), and was more effective than tadalafil in reducing PVR and improving QoL index (PMID 37245088).
- A randomized uroflowmetric study showed a single 50 mg dose of sildenafil significantly increased Qmax (15.6 to 19.3 cc/s, p<0.0001) and average flow rate (7.3 to 9.1 cc/s, p<0.0001) versus untreated controls in men with ED and BPH-LUTS (PMID 18649004).
- An uncontrolled prospective study found sildenafil improved mean IPSS by 4.6 points (p=0.013) and QoL score by 1.4 points (p=0.025) in men with LUTS, with 60% of men showing IPSS improvement (PMID 16839322).
- A small prospective controlled randomized open study found sildenafil improved IPSS from 12.8 to 8.6 (32.8% change), reduced PVR, and increased Qmax significantly in BPH/LUTS patients (PMID 29693865).
- In a three-arm RCT, sildenafil monotherapy improved IPSS by 28.2% (p<0.001 vs baseline) and significantly improved erectile function, though it was less effective than tamsulosin or combination therapy for voiding parameters including Qmax and PVR (PMID 19855976).
- A pilot RCT found sildenafil monotherapy improved IPSS by 11.8% (significant vs baseline), while the alfuzosin-sildenafil combination was superior (-24.1%) for LUTS; sildenafil alone did not significantly improve Qmax, frequency, nocturia, or PVR (PMID 17258855).
- An open comparative multicenter study found sildenafil monotherapy (on demand) significantly improved IIEF-5 scores but did not significantly improve IPSS or QoL scores, whereas the combination of sildenafil plus doxazosin GITS improved all three outcomes (PMID 21602833).

### Coronary Artery Disorder

#### Literature — Coronary Artery Disorder

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 3

The evidence on sildenafil for coronary artery disorder is mixed. A small pilot RCT in coronary slow flow phenomenon showed significant improvements in angina severity, functional capacity, and Duke treadmill scores with sildenafil versus placebo (PMID: 39214536), while two controlled studies in ischemic heart disease patients showed beneficial vascular and hemodynamic effects (epicardial dilation, improved endothelial function, platelet inhibition, improved adenosine-mediated myocardial blood flow reserve) but failed to demonstrate a clear anti-ischemic benefit during exercise or cold pressor stress comparable to active comparators (PMID: 12383570, PMID: 16205453). ### Chronic Kidney Disease

#### Literature — Chronic Kidney Disease

**Evidence strength:** weak, supports
**Relevant studies:** 5

The preponderance of evidence supports sildenafil as renoprotective in chronic kidney disease contexts. Across multiple preclinical models—including DOCA-salt hypertension (PMID: 23171857), adenine-induced CKD (PMID: 30236848), adriamycin nephropathy and hyperglycemia-induced renal injury (PMID: 27895156), and streptozocin-induced diabetic nephropathy (PMID: 41482414)—sildenafil consistently improved renal functional and histological parameters. The single mixed-verdict study (PMID: 39478527) was an uncontrolled veterinary case series in cats that showed some benefit on glomerular filtration but limited effect on fibrosis biomarkers, which does not offset the consistent positive preclinical controlled evidence.

**Key findings:**
- Sildenafil attenuated decreased creatinine clearance, increased albumin-to-creatinine ratio, glomerulosclerosis, and tubulointerstitial fibrosis in DOCA-salt hypertensive rats via anti-inflammatory, antifibrotic, and antiapoptotic mechanisms (PMID: 23171857).
- Sildenafil prevented proteinuria and increased TRPC6 expression in rats with adriamycin-induced nephropathy and mice with hyperglycemia-induced renal injury through a cGMP/PKG/PPAR-γ axis, suggesting antiproteinuric clinical potential (PMID: 27895156).
- In adenine-induced CKD rats, sildenafil produced dose-dependent improvements in creatinine clearance, plasma urea and creatinine, uremic toxins, inflammatory cytokines, oxidative stress indices, and renal histopathological markers of inflammation and fibrosis (PMID: 30236848).
- Sildenafil significantly reduced renal expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines TNF-α and IL-6 and apoptotic marker Bax, while increasing anti-apoptotic Bcl-2, in streptozocin-induced diabetic nephropathic rats (PMID: 41482414).
- In cats with acute on chronic kidney disease, sildenafil showed a beneficial effect on glomerular filtration but was ineffective in mitigating renal damage and fibrosis via the TGF-β/SMAD pathway, yielding a mixed result in this uncontrolled veterinary study (PMID: 39478527).

### Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease

#### Literature — Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease

**Evidence strength:** strong, contradicts
**Relevant studies:** 5

The preponderance of controlled trial evidence does not support sildenafil as an effective treatment for COPD. Four randomized controlled trials found no meaningful improvement in exercise capacity, gas exchange, or quality of life with sildenafil (PMIDs: 39389522, 23429918, 22360383, 18342559), with one trial even showing worsened gas exchange and quality of life (PMID: 22360383). The single supporting trial (PMID: 21545068) reported a large improvement in six-minute walk distance in severe COPD, but its findings are outweighed by the consistent null or negative results across the other four controlled studies.

**Key findings:**
- Acute oral sildenafil (50 mg or 100 mg) failed to improve gas exchange efficiency, excess exercise ventilation, dyspnea, or exercise tolerance in patients with mild-to-moderate COPD in a randomized crossover trial (PMID: 39389522).
- In a double-blind RCT of severe COPD patients with moderately increased pulmonary arterial pressure undergoing pulmonary rehabilitation, sildenafil did not improve cycle endurance time, incremental exercise performance, six-minute walk distance, or quality of life compared to placebo (PMID: 23429918).
- In a randomized crossover trial of COPD patients with emphysema, sildenafil had no effect on six-minute walk distance or peak oxygen consumption, and significantly worsened the alveolar-arterial oxygen gradient, symptoms, and quality of life while increasing adverse events (PMID: 22360383).
- A single-dose study using right heart catheterization found that sildenafil attenuated exercise-induced increases in mean pulmonary artery pressure in COPD patients, but this was not accompanied by improved stroke volume, cardiac output, or maximal exercise capacity (PMID: 18342559).
- One randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in severe COPD reported a significant improvement in six-minute walk distance (median change +190 m vs. 0 m for placebo) and a reduction in pulmonary artery pressure after 12 weeks of sildenafil (PMID: 21545068).

### Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy

#### Literature — Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy

**Evidence strength:** moderate, contradicts
**Relevant studies:** 5

The evidence for sildenafil in Duchenne muscular dystrophy is mixed: multiple animal studies demonstrate benefits including reduced diaphragm weakness and fibrosis (PMID: 22653783), reversal of cardiac dysfunction in mdx mice (PMID: 20956307), reduced muscle damage and improved endurance training performance (PMID: 30946638), and enhanced AAV9-mediated gene therapy efficacy (PMID: 37500816), but the single randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled human trial found that sildenafil did not improve cardiomyopathy in adults with Duchenne/Becker muscular dystrophy and was terminated early due to a higher proportion of subjects worsening on sildenafil (PMID: 25042693). The controlled human trial (PMID: 25042693) carries greater weight for clinical translation than the preclinical animal studies, indicating that the promising animal findings have not translated to human benefit for cardiomyopathy.

**Key findings:**
- In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, 29% of subjects receiving sildenafil had a ≥10% increase in left ventricular end-systolic volume (worsening) compared to 13% on placebo, leading to early termination; sildenafil did not improve cardiac function in adults with Duchenne/Becker muscular dystrophy (PMID: 25042693).
- A 14-week treatment with sildenafil significantly reduced diaphragm muscle weakness and fibrosis in mdx mice, reduced MMP-13 expression, normalized TNFα, and decreased Evans Blue tracer accumulation indicative of improved muscle health (PMID: 22653783).
- Chronic sildenafil treatment reduced functional cardiac deficits in aged mdx mice, and when started after cardiomyopathy had developed, established symptoms were rapidly reversed within a few days (PMID: 20956307).
- Sildenafil attenuated the increase in MRI T2 (a marker of muscle damage) after downhill running in mdx mice and improved endurance training performance, with treated mdx completing >90% of target running distance versus 60% for untreated mdx (PMID: 30946638).
- Sildenafil pre-treatment in mdx mice significantly increased micro-dystrophin gene expression delivered by AAV9, improved forelimb grip strength and rotarod performance, decreased serum creatine kinase levels, and reduced fibrosis (PMID: 37500816).

### Acute Kidney Injury

#### Literature — Acute Kidney Injury

**Evidence strength:** moderate, contradicts
**Relevant studies:** 7

The evidence for sildenafil in acute kidney injury is mixed: multiple animal studies demonstrate renoprotective effects via anti-apoptotic, antioxidant, and anti-inflammatory mechanisms in ischemia-reperfusion and glycerol-induced AKI models (PMID: 26850186, PMID: 19474186, PMID: 26377490, PMID: 38748227), but two randomized controlled trials in humans—one in robotic partial nephrectomy (PMID: 27613357) and one in cardiac surgery (PMID: 32245569)—found no significant renoprotective benefit, with the cardiac surgery trial additionally noting a statistically significant increase in multiple organ dysfunction scores in the sildenafil group. A network meta-analysis of pediatric congenital heart disease surgery also found no significant effect of sildenafil on AKI incidence (PMID: 40968005). The preclinical data support a biological rationale, but the available human RCT evidence does not confirm clinical benefit.

**Key findings:**
- In a rat ischemia-reperfusion model, sildenafil (0.5 and 1.0 mg/kg i.p.) significantly protected against AKI as measured by creatinine clearance, BUN, plasma uric acid, and oxidative stress markers, with protection abolished by a PPAR-γ antagonist (PMID: 26850186).
- Sildenafil pretreatment in rats with renal ischemia-reperfusion injury significantly lowered BUN and serum creatinine, increased ERK phosphorylation and eNOS/iNOS expression, reduced the Bax/Bcl-2 ratio, and decreased caspase-3 activity and apoptotic cells (PMID: 19474186).
- In a rat renal I/R model, sildenafil (1 mg/kg oral) significantly attenuated serum creatinine and BUN, upregulated antioxidant genes (Nrf2, HO-1, NQO-1) and antiapoptotic gene Bcl-2, and reduced proinflammatory cytokine genes TNF-α, IL-1β, and ICAM-1 (PMID: 26377490).
- In a glycerol-induced acute renal failure rat model, sildenafil alone and in combination with furosemide improved renal function markers, reduced KIM-1 and NGAL tubular expression, and decreased caspase-3 and IL-1β immunohistochemistry scores (PMID: 38748227).
- In a double-blind RCT of 30 patients undergoing robotic partial nephrectomy, sildenafil 100 mg oral versus placebo showed no significant difference in eGFR change at 1 day (-8% vs -10%, P=0.53), 2 days (-9% vs -9%, P=0.77), 1 month (-4% vs -6%, P=0.31), or intermediate follow-up (-8% vs -1%, P=0.16) (PMID: 27613357).
- In a double-blind RCT of 129 patients undergoing cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass, i.v. sildenafil citrate did not significantly reduce serum creatinine versus placebo (MD: 0.88 μmol/L [-5.82, 7.59]), and was associated with a statistically significant increase in multiple organ dysfunction scores (MD: 0.54 [0.02, 1.07]; P=0.044) (PMID: 32245569).
- A network meta-analysis of 28 RCTs in pediatric congenital heart disease surgery found that no intervention, including sildenafil, significantly affected the incidence of acute kidney injury (PMID: 40968005).

### Endothelial Dysfunction

#### Literature — Endothelial Dysfunction

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 4

The evidence is mixed: two randomized controlled crossover trials demonstrate that sildenafil protects against ischemia-reperfusion-induced endothelial dysfunction (PMID: 15699265) and abrogates acute smoking-induced endothelial dysfunction (PMID: 15533731), while a third randomized double-blind placebo-controlled crossover study found that sildenafil does not improve peripheral endothelium-dependent vasomotor or fibrinolytic function in patients with established coronary heart disease (PMID: 15863522). All three studies are of comparable methodological quality (randomized, placebo-controlled, crossover designs), pointing in opposite directions depending on the clinical context, precluding a clear net conclusion.

**Key findings:**
- In a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled crossover study of 16 patients with coronary heart disease, sildenafil increased endothelium-independent vasodilatation but did not improve acetylcholine-mediated forearm blood flow or fibrinolytic function, leading to the conclusion that PDE5 inhibition is unlikely to reverse generalized vascular dysfunction in coronary heart disease (PMID: 15863522).
- In a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled crossover study of 10 healthy male volunteers, oral sildenafil (50 mg) preserved flow-mediated dilatation of the radial artery after ischemia-reperfusion (FMD after IR: 6.2±1.1% with sildenafil vs. 1.2±0.7% with placebo, P<0.01), an effect mediated by K(ATP) channel opening (PMID: 15699265).
- In a randomized placebo-controlled crossover study of 14 male smokers, sildenafil (50 mg) abolished the acute smoking-induced decrease in brachial artery FMD (sildenafil/smoking: 3.83% to 4.33% vs. placebo/smoking: 4.56% to 2.80%, P<0.05), representing a 51% improvement (PMID: 15533731).
- The protective effect of sildenafil against ischemia-reperfusion-induced endothelial dysfunction was completely abolished by glibenclamide, a K(ATP) channel blocker, confirming a mechanistic role for K(ATP) channels (PMID: 15699265).
- Sildenafil caused a significant reduction in mean arterial blood pressure in both coronary heart disease patients and healthy controls but failed to alter endothelium-dependent responses to acetylcholine or substance P in either group (PMID: 15863522).

**Supporting PMIDs:** [15699265](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15699265/), [15533731](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15533731/), [40763482](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40763482/)
