## Findings by Disease

### Eisenmenger Syndrome

#### Literature — Eisenmenger Syndrome

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 7

Multiple clinical studies, including randomized controlled and prospective open-label trials, support the use of sildenafil for Eisenmenger syndrome. Sildenafil monotherapy significantly improved 6-minute walk distance, oxygen saturation, pulmonary vascular resistance, and functional class in Eisenmenger syndrome patients (PMID: 16569546, PMID: 20304507, PMID: 17174418, PMID: 21948962). Combination therapy with bosentan showed mixed results: adding sildenafil to bosentan improved resting oxygen saturation but did not significantly improve walk distance as a primary endpoint (PMID: 20202971), while add-on sildenafil after bosentan failure improved exercise tolerance, hemodynamics, and functional class (PMID: 21081251).

**Key findings:**
- In a randomized placebo-controlled crossover study of 20 patients (10 with Eisenmenger syndrome, 10 with idiopathic PAH), sildenafil significantly improved 6-minute walk distance from 262 ± 99 m to 358.9 ± 96.5 m (P < .0001), reduced pulmonary artery pressure from 98.8 ± 20.5 to 78.3 ± 15.3 mmHg (P < .0001), and improved NYHA class (PMID: 16569546).
- In a prospective study of 12 Eisenmenger syndrome patients in NYHA class III, 3 months of oral sildenafil improved 6MWT distance from 347.3 ± 80.7 m to 392.5 ± 82.0 m (p=0.002), all patients improved to NYHA II, and all components of the CAMPHOR quality-of-life score improved significantly (PMID: 20304507).
- In a prospective open-label study comparing Eisenmenger syndrome (n=7) vs idiopathic PAH (n=6), 6 months of sildenafil produced significant improvement in functional capacity, arterial saturation, and pulmonary hemodynamics including reduction in systolic and mean pulmonary artery pressures and pulmonary vascular resistance in the Eisenmenger group (PMID: 17174418).
- In a prospective open-label multicentre study of 84 Eisenmenger syndrome patients, 12 months of oral sildenafil 20 mg TID improved 6MWD by 56 m (95% CI 42–69, p<0.0001), increased resting SaO2 by 2.4% (p<0.0001), and reduced mean pulmonary arterial pressure by 4.7 mmHg and pulmonary vascular resistance index by 474 dyn×s×cm−5×m2 (p<0.0001) (PMID: 21948962).
- In a randomized double-blind crossover trial of 21 Eisenmenger syndrome patients on bosentan, adding sildenafil did not significantly improve 6-minute walk distance (primary endpoint, P=0.48) but did significantly increase resting oxygen saturation (2.9 vs -1.8%, P<0.01) compared to placebo add-on (PMID: 20202971).
- In a randomized trial of 22 Eisenmenger syndrome patients and 5 idiopathic PAH patients, sildenafil-ambrisentan combination therapy showed significant improvement in NYHA functional class and mean pulmonary artery pressure, with an insignificant improving trend in 6-minute walk distance and oxygen saturation compared to sildenafil monotherapy (PMID: 34627582).
- In 32 patients with CHD-related PAH (28 with Eisenmenger physiology) who had clinical worsening on bosentan, add-on sildenafil 20 mg TID for 6 months significantly improved WHO functional class (2.1 ± 0.4 vs 2.9 ± 0.3, P=0.042), 6-minute walk distance (360 ± 51 vs 293 ± 68 m, P=0.005), SpO2 at end of 6MWT (72 ± 10 vs 63 ± 15%, P=0.047), and pulmonary vascular resistance index (19 ± 9 vs 24 ± 16 WU/m2, P=0.003) (PMID: 21081251).

### Heart Failure

#### Literature — Heart Failure

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 12

Multiple randomized controlled trials have evaluated sildenafil for heart failure, yielding mixed results. Earlier single-center RCTs reported improvements in cardiac performance, exercise capacity, diastolic function, ventilatory efficiency, and pulmonary hemodynamics in HFrEF patients (PMIDs: 16275194, 18036451, 18381181, 22067410, 21036891), while the definitive multinational SilHF trial in HFrEF with pulmonary hypertension failed to meet its co-primary endpoints of patient global assessment and 6-minute walk test (PMID: 35596935). In HFpEF with pulmonary hypertension, sildenafil did not reduce pulmonary artery pressures, improve hemodynamics, cardiac structure/function, or quality of life, and showed opposing effects on ventricular versus vascular function (PMIDs: 26188003, 27873388, 25782985).

**Key findings:**
- Sildenafil 50 mg acutely increased cardiac index by 0.37 L/min/m² and reduced total systemic resistance by 479 dynes·s·cm⁻⁵ in HF patients with EF <35% (PMID: 16275194)
- Six months of sildenafil 50 mg twice daily in CHF reduced systolic pulmonary artery pressure (33.7 to 23.9 mmHg), improved peak VO₂ (14.8 to 18.7 mL/min/kg), and reduced VE/VCO₂ slope (35.5 to 29.8) compared to placebo (PMID: 18036451)
- Four weeks of sildenafil 50 mg three times daily improved maximal oxygen uptake, ventilatory efficiency, and reduced pulmonary artery systolic pressure in CHF outpatients compared to placebo (PMID: 18381181)
- One-year sildenafil treatment reversed exercise oscillatory breathing in 93% of HF patients, improved peak VO₂ (9.6 to 13.2 mL/min/kg), and reduced mean pulmonary artery pressure (34.8 to 24 mmHg) and pulmonary vascular resistance compared to placebo (PMID: 22067410)
- One-year sildenafil 50 mg three times daily improved LV ejection fraction, diastolic function (E/E' lateral decreased from 13.1 to 9.4), reversed LV remodeling (LV mass index 148 to 128 g/m²), and improved peak VO₂ and quality of life in systolic HF patients (PMID: 21036891)
- Intravenous sildenafil caused similar reductions in systemic and pulmonary vascular resistance as milrinone in end-stage CHF patients undergoing transplant assessment, though milrinone had greater effects on pulmonary artery wedge pressure (PMID: 19560695)
- The multinational SilHF RCT (n=69 HFrEF patients with PASP ≥40 mmHg) found sildenafil did not improve patient global assessment, 6-minute walk test, quality of life, or PASP compared to placebo, and those on sildenafil had numerically more serious adverse events (33% vs. 21%) (PMID: 35596935)
- In HFpEF patients with pulmonary hypertension (n=52), sildenafil 60 mg three times daily for 12 weeks did not reduce mean pulmonary artery pressure (−2.4 vs. −4.7 mmHg for placebo, P=0.14) or improve PAWP, cardiac output, or peak VO₂ (PMID: 26188003)
- Sildenafil for 12 weeks in HFpEF with pulmonary hypertension did not affect cardiac structure or function on echocardiography, integrative exercise responses, or quality of life compared to placebo (PMID: 27873388)
- In a 12-week RCT of systolic HF patients (n=106), sildenafil did not significantly reduce mean blood pressure (primary endpoint, placebo-corrected effect 1.16 mmHg, P>0.05) or improve 6-minute walk test (nonsignificant +14 m, P=0.67) (PMID: 23241003)
- In a RELAX trial subgroup (n=48 HFpEF), sildenafil reduced arterial elastance (−0.29 vs. +0.02 mmHg/mL, P=0.008) but also reduced LV contractility by 11–16% compared to placebo, with no effect on pulmonary artery systolic pressure (PMID: 25782985)
- The SilHF trial was designed as a 210-patient multinational RCT of sildenafil (40 mg three times daily) in HF with group 2 pulmonary hypertension, with co-primary endpoints of patient global assessment and 6-minute walk test, but was terminated early with only 69 patients enrolled (PMID: 23097067)

**Supporting PMIDs:** [16275194](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16275194/), [18036451](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18036451/), [19560695](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19560695/), [18381181](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18381181/), [25782985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25782985/), [22067410](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22067410/), [21036891](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21036891/)

#### Clinical Trials

**Development stage:** Phase 3 completed for this indication
Sildenafil has been evaluated across a substantial clinical programme in heart failure, spanning Phase 2 through Phase 4 trials. At the Phase 3 level, six completed studies are on record. NCT00763867 (RELAX Study, Phase 3, completed) evaluated sildenafil's effectiveness at improving health outcomes and exercise ability in diastolic heart failure. NCT01726049 (Phase 3, completed) examined sildenafil in heart failure with preserved ejection fraction and pulmonary hypertension. NCT00975494 (Phase 3, completed) assessed chronic sildenafil treatment in heart failure. NCT00309816 (Phase 3, completed) and NCT00309790 (Phase 3, completed) were parallel studies of sildenafil in heart failure and advanced heart failure, respectively. NCT00407446 (Phase 3, completed) investigated PDE5 inhibition with sildenafil in chronic heart failure. Two Phase 2/Phase 3 completed trials also contribute: NCT01156636 examined PDE5 inhibition and pulmonary hypertension in diastolic heart failure, and NCT01936350 assessed sildenafil's impact on ventricular function in heart failure patients. Two additional Phase 3 trials — NCT01616381 (Sildenafil Versus Placebo in Chronic Heart Failure) and NCT03460470 (SilHF-US) — have unknown current status. Supporting evidence comes from completed Phase 2 and Phase 4 studies, including NCT01185925 (Phase 2, completed) on exercise oscillatory breathing and sildenafil, NCT01046838 (SIDAMI, Phase 4, completed) on diastolic dysfunction after acute myocardial infarction, and NCT00781508 (Phase 4, completed) on cardiac function effects. One Phase 4 trial, NCT00793338, was withdrawn before initiation due to lack of funding.

_2 trial(s) excluded as a different indication: NCT00972569, NCT00818701._

_Why: The vast majority of trials directly study sildenafil as the experimental agent for heart failure (HFrEF, HFpEF, diastolic HF) — these are relevant regardless of co-listed secondary pulmonary hypertension, because the target disease is heart failure and the PH is comorbid/secondary, not primary PAH (an approved indication). Two trials (NCT00972569, NCT00818701) are contaminated because sildenafil is a background/adjunct co-intervention alongside the primary studied agent (BNP/nesiritide), making the studied drug not sildenafil._

### Raynaud Disease

#### Literature — Raynaud Disease

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 4

Multiple controlled clinical studies support the use of sildenafil for Raynaud disease. A double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT found that modified-release sildenafil significantly reduced Raynaud's phenomenon (RP) attack frequency by 44% versus 18.1% for placebo in patients with limited cutaneous systemic sclerosis (PMID: 21360507), and a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled study found sildenafil 100 mg/day significantly improved digital blood flow and RP symptom duration and severity scores in systemic sclerosis patients (PMID: 28281457). A series of n-of-1 randomized double-blind trials found a greater than 90% probability that on-demand sildenafil (40 mg or 80 mg) was superior to placebo for most RP outcomes, though aggregated effect sizes were not clinically relevant and substantial heterogeneity was observed among patients (PMID: 30383134). An open-label uncontrolled pilot study also reported significant improvement in RP severity in systemic sclerosis patients treated with sildenafil 25 mg three times daily for 3 months (PMID: 22833239).

**Key findings:**
- Modified-release sildenafil reduced mean RP attack frequency by 44.0% versus 18.1% for placebo (P=0.034) in patients with RP secondary to limited cutaneous systemic sclerosis (PMID: 21360507).
- Sildenafil 100 mg/day for 8 weeks significantly improved finger blood flow before and after cold stimulus (p=0.026 and p=0.028 respectively) and significantly improved duration of RP attacks and VAS score for RP severity compared to placebo in systemic sclerosis patients (PMID: 28281457).
- In a series of n-of-1 trials, the probability that sildenafil at 40 mg or 80 mg was more effective than placebo was greater than 90% for most RP outcomes, but aggregated effect sizes were not clinically relevant and substantial heterogeneity in efficacy was observed among patients (PMID: 30383134).
- In an open-label pilot study, sildenafil 25 mg three times daily for 3 months produced statistically significant improvement in severity of Raynaud's phenomenon in systemic sclerosis patients, with no new digital infarcts or ulcers and healing of existing ulcers (PMID: 22833239).

### Infertility

#### Literature — Infertility

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 6

Multiple randomized clinical trials and prospective studies examined sildenafil for infertility, primarily in women with unexplained infertility or thin endometrium. Sildenafil (oral or vaginal) combined with clomiphene citrate significantly improved endometrial thickness and pregnancy rates in some trials (PMID: 32286686, PMID: 24294742, PMID: 27783532), while one trial showed improved endometrial thickness but no significant increase in pregnancy rates (PMID: 37259740), and one study found neither vaginal sildenafil nor vaginal estradiol improved endometrial thickness in women with persistently thin endometria (PMID: 15266759). A single-dose study in infertile men found sildenafil acutely increased sperm progressive motility compared to baseline (PMID: 17544419), suggesting a potential benefit in male infertility as well.

**Key findings:**
- In a randomized clinical trial of 80 women with unexplained infertility, adding oral sildenafil citrate 25 mg twice daily (days 8–12) to clomiphene citrate significantly increased pregnancy rates (65% vs. 40%, P=0.043) and endometrial thickness (10.4 vs. 9.2 mm, P=0.007) compared to clomiphene alone (PMID: 32286686).
- In a prospective randomized study of 130 women with unexplained infertility, adding oral sildenafil citrate 20 mg twice daily to clomiphene citrate significantly improved median endometrial thickness (8 mm vs. 7 mm, p<0.01) but did not produce a statistically significant increase in pregnancy rates (PMID: 37259740).
- In a study of 42 women with anovulatory infertility, vaginal sildenafil citrate 25 mg combined with Serophene significantly increased mean endometrial thickness (11.8 vs. 10.2 mm), endometrial volume (5.2 vs. 3.6), and significantly decreased pulsatility index and resistance index to uterine artery compared to Serophene alone (PMID: 24294742).
- A clinical evaluation of a vaginal sildenafil citrate thermosensitive gel in women with clomiphene citrate failure due to thin endometrium showed that the in situ sildenafil vaginal gel significantly increased endometrial thickness and uterine blood flow with no reported side effects (PMID: 27783532).
- In a prospective randomized double-blind crossover study of 18 young infertile men, a single oral dose of sildenafil 50 mg significantly increased sperm progressive motility (median 37.0% vs. 28.5% at baseline), whereas tadalafil significantly decreased it (PMID: 17544419).
- In women with a history of thin endometria who failed to attain 8 mm thickness on oral estradiol, neither vaginal sildenafil (25 mg four times daily) nor vaginal estradiol significantly improved endometrial thickness or blood flow in a subsequent frozen embryo transfer cycle (PMID: 15266759).

**Supporting PMIDs:** [32286686](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32286686/), [37259740](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37259740/), [17544419](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17544419/), [24294742](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24294742/), [27783532](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27783532/)

### Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease

#### Literature — Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 4

The evidence for sildenafil in COPD is mixed across multiple randomized controlled trials. One RCT in severe COPD found significant improvements in six-minute walk distance and pulmonary artery pressure after 12 weeks of sildenafil (PMID: 21545068), but three other RCTs found no benefit on exercise tolerance, gas exchange, or dyspnea — and one noted worsened gas exchange and quality of life (PMID: 22360383, PMID: 23429918, PMID: 39389522). The overall body of evidence does not support routine use of sildenafil for COPD, with potential for harm in patients without pulmonary hypertension.

**Key findings:**
- In a 12-week RCT of 33 patients with severe COPD, sildenafil produced a median increase in 6-minute walk distance of 190 m versus 0 m for placebo (p<0.05), and significantly reduced pulmonary artery pressure (PMID: 21545068).
- In a randomized crossover trial of COPD and emphysema patients without pulmonary hypertension, sildenafil had no effect on 6-minute walk distance or peak oxygen consumption, significantly worsened the alveolar-arterial oxygen gradient (p=0.02), worsened symptoms (p=0.04), and decreased quality of life (p=0.03) (PMID: 22360383).
- In a double-blind RCT of 63 patients with severe COPD and moderately increased pulmonary arterial pressure undergoing pulmonary rehabilitation, sildenafil (20 mg three times daily) did not improve cycle endurance time, incremental exercise test performance, 6-minute walk distance, or quality of life compared to placebo (PMID: 23429918).
- In a randomized placebo-controlled crossover trial of 24 patients with mild-to-moderate COPD, acute oral sildenafil (50 mg or 100 mg) failed to improve ventilatory efficiency (VE/VCO2), dead space/tidal volume ratio, operating lung volumes, dyspnea, or exercise tolerance compared to placebo (PMID: 39389522).

### Covid-19

#### Literature — Covid-19

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 4

Two randomized controlled trials provide clinical evidence that sildenafil may benefit patients with COVID-19. In a placebo-controlled RCT of ICU patients with severe COVID-19 and pulmonary arterial hypertension, sildenafil significantly reduced mortality and intubation risks when adjusted for PAH (OR=0.21 and OR=0.26, respectively), and shortened ICU stay (15 vs. 19 days, p<0.001) (PMID: 37243243). A pilot RCT in COVID-19 inpatients with perfusion mismatch found no significant difference in oxygenation parameters but showed sildenafil was associated with zero patients requiring invasive mechanical ventilation versus 4 in the placebo group (p=0.04) and a shorter hospital stay (PMID: 34980198). A hypothesis paper (PMID: 32939238) and a broad review (PMID: 33226665) propose mechanistic rationale for sildenafil in COVID-19 via modulation of nitric oxide pathways and inflammatory cascades, though these do not provide clinical trial data. Overall, the evidence supports further investigation of sildenafil for COVID-19, particularly in patients with pulmonary complications.

**Key findings:**
- In a double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT of 150 ICU patients with severe COVID-19 and PAH, sildenafil (0.25 mg/kg t.i.d. for one week) significantly reduced mortality risk (OR=0.21, 95% CI: 0.05-0.89) and intubation risk (OR=0.26, 95% CI: 0.08-0.86) when adjusted for PAH, and shortened ICU stay (15 vs. 19 days, p<0.001) (PMID: 37243243)
- In a triple-blinded pilot RCT of 40 COVID-19 inpatients with perfusion mismatch on sCTA, no patients in the sildenafil arm required invasive mechanical ventilation versus 4 in the placebo arm (logrank p=0.04), and sildenafil patients had a significantly shorter median hospital stay (9 vs. 12 days, p=0.04) (PMID: 34980198)
- A hypothesis paper proposes that sildenafil could counter the inflammatory cascade and thromboembolic episodes in COVID-19 by modulating iNOS-driven nitric oxide overproduction (PMID: 32939238)
- A broad review of sildenafil's potential new indications notes promising results in various conditions and proposes mechanistic rationale for its use in inflammatory and vascular pathologies relevant to COVID-19 (PMID: 33226665)

### Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy

#### Literature — Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 6

Evidence for sildenafil in Duchenne muscular dystrophy is mixed. Preclinical studies in the mdx mouse model showed that sildenafil reversed cardiac dysfunction and reduced diaphragm muscle weakness and fibrosis (PMID: 20956307, PMID: 22653783), and a human study demonstrated that sildenafil increases skeletal muscle protein synthesis and reduces fatigue (PMID: 24330691). However, two randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trials in adults with Duchenne/Becker muscular dystrophy found no benefit of sildenafil on cardiac function or skeletal muscle performance, with one trial terminated early due to a higher proportion of subjects worsening on sildenafil (PMID: 25042693, PMID: 25042931). A cerebrovascular study in Becker muscular dystrophy patients showed sildenafil increased BOLD responses but had no effect on cerebral blood flow (PMID: 27485237).

**Key findings:**
- In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in adults with Duchenne/Becker muscular dystrophy and cardiomyopathy, 29% of subjects on sildenafil had ≥10% increase in left ventricular end-systolic volume versus 13% on placebo; the trial was terminated early by the data and safety monitoring board, and sildenafil was unlikely to improve cardiac function (PMID: 25042693).
- In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial in Becker muscular dystrophy patients, sildenafil had no effect on brachial artery blood flow, 6-minute walk test, maximal oxidative capacity, or cardiac function by MRI; PDE5 and nNOS were found to be deficient in muscle biopsies (PMID: 25042931).
- In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial in Becker muscular dystrophy patients, sildenafil increased event-related sensory and visual BOLD responses compared with placebo (p < 0.01), but did not alter cerebral blood flow or middle cerebral artery diameter (PMID: 27485237).
- Chronic sildenafil treatment in aged mdx mice reduced functional deficits in cardiac performance, and when started after cardiomyopathy had developed, established symptoms were rapidly reversed within a few days (PMID: 20956307).
- A 14-week treatment with sildenafil in mdx mice significantly reduced diaphragm muscle weakness, slowed fibrosis, reduced MMP-13 expression, normalized TNFα, and reduced Evans Blue dye accumulation indicative of improved muscle health (PMID: 22653783).
- Short-term daily administration of sildenafil in humans increased skeletal muscle protein synthesis, altered protein expression and nitrosylation, and reduced muscle fatigue (PMID: 24330691).

### Coronary Artery Disorder

#### Literature — Coronary Artery Disorder

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 9

Multiple controlled trials and mechanistic studies indicate that sildenafil has beneficial vascular effects in patients with coronary artery disorder, including dilation of epicardial coronary arteries, inhibition of platelet activation, improvement of endothelial dysfunction, reduction of aortic stiffness, and improvement of myocardial blood flow reserve (PMID: 12383570, 16205453, 15322323, 15125484). A pilot RCT in coronary slow flow phenomenon showed significant improvements in angina severity and functional capacity (PMID: 39214536), and combination with trimetazidine reduced ischemic burden during sexual activity more than nitrates alone (PMID: 15670539). However, two controlled studies found that sildenafil did not significantly improve exercise-induced ischemia or exercise capacity beyond beta-blocker therapy or placebo in stable CAD (PMID: 11851538, 11770869), and one hemodynamic study showed mixed results compared to isosorbide mononitrate (PMID: 16422873), making the overall evidence mixed.

**Key findings:**
- In a pilot RCT of primary coronary slow flow phenomenon, sildenafil 50 mg daily for 12 weeks significantly improved angina severity (all sildenafil recipients reached CCS I vs 40% placebo, p=0.011), functional capacity (METs increased from 9.9 to 13.1 vs no change with placebo, p=0.008), and Duke treadmill score (median increase 5.5 vs placebo, p=0.01) (PMID: 39214536)
- Sildenafil 100 mg dilated epicardial coronary arteries (+6.9±1.3%, p<0.0001), improved coronary endothelial function, and inhibited both resting and ADP-stimulated platelet IIb/IIIa receptor activation in CAD patients; its effect on myocardial ischemia during exercise was intermediate between placebo and isosorbide dinitrate (PMID: 12383570)
- Sildenafil improved myocardial blood flow dilator reserve in abnormal zones of ischemic heart disease patients: adenosine-stimulated myocardial blood flow reserve was 2.6±0.7 with sildenafil vs 2.0±1.3 with placebo (p<0.04) (PMID: 16205453)

### Endothelial Dysfunction

#### Literature — Endothelial Dysfunction

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 5

Multiple controlled trials provide mixed evidence for sildenafil in treating endothelial dysfunction. Three studies show sildenafil improves endothelial function: it improved coronary and peripheral endothelial function in CAD patients (PMID: 12383570), prevented ischemia-reperfusion-induced endothelial dysfunction in healthy volunteers via K-ATP channel opening (PMID: 15699265), abrogated acute smoking-induced endothelial dysfunction in smokers (PMID: 15533731), and acutely improved brachial artery flow-mediated dilation in chronic heart failure patients (PMID: 15574127). However, one randomized crossover study found sildenafil did not improve peripheral endothelium-dependent vasomotor or fibrinolytic function in patients with coronary heart disease (PMID: 15863522), indicating the evidence is mixed across populations and contexts.

**Key findings:**
- Sildenafil dilated epicardial coronary arteries (+6.9±1.3%), improved coronary epicardial and microvascular endothelial responses with acetylcholine and cold-pressor testing (with greater enhancement in CAD patients with endothelial dysfunction), and prolonged the duration of brachial artery hyperemia compared to placebo (PMID: 12383570)
- Oral sildenafil (50 mg) prevented ischemia-reperfusion-induced impairment of flow-mediated dilation in healthy volunteers (FMD after IR: 6.2±1.1% with sildenafil vs. 1.2±0.7% with placebo, P<0.01), an effect mediated by K-ATP channel opening and blocked by glibenclamide (PMID: 15699265)
- Sildenafil (50 mg) abolished the acute smoking-induced decrease in brachial artery FMD in male smokers (sildenafil/smoking: from 3.83% to 4.33% vs. placebo/smoking: from 4.56% to 2.80%, P<0.05) (PMID: 15533731)
- Sildenafil (50 mg) acutely increased brachial artery FMD at 1, 2, and 4 hours compared to placebo (+3.9±1.4%, +4.6±1.8%, and +3.7±1.3% respectively, all P<0.02) in chronic heart failure patients, with additive effects when combined with ramipril at 4 hours (PMID: 15574127)
- Intravenous sildenafil did not alter the forearm blood flow response to acetylcholine or verapamil, and did not affect acute tissue plasminogen activator release in response to substance P, in patients with coronary heart disease or healthy controls, indicating no improvement in endothelium-dependent vasomotor or fibrinolytic function (PMID: 15863522)

### Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia

#### Literature — Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 9

Multiple clinical trials and one ex vivo study provide evidence on sildenafil for benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH)/lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS). Several RCTs demonstrate that sildenafil significantly improves International Prostate Symptom Score compared to placebo (PMID: 17296414, PMID: 18597830, PMID: 28590828), and a single-dose study showed acute improvement in Qmax (PMID: 18649004), while ex vivo data support a prostatic smooth muscle relaxation mechanism (PMID: 19143907). However, two studies found no significant advantage of sildenafil combination therapy over comparators for BPH outcomes (PMID: 22211956, PMID: 25010303), and one study found sildenafil monotherapy did not significantly improve IPSS when compared against combination therapy (PMID: 21602833), yielding an overall mixed picture.

**Key findings:**
- In a 12-week RCT, sildenafil significantly improved International Prostate Symptom Score vs placebo (-6.32 vs -1.93, p<0.0001) and Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia Impact Index (-2.0 vs -0.9, p<0.0001), though urinary flow rates did not differ between groups (PMID: 17296414).
- Post-hoc analysis confirmed sildenafil improved LUTS scores independent of baseline LUTS severity or BMI, with greater improvement in severe LUTS (-8.6 vs -2.4, p<0.0001), though maximal urinary flow rate did not change significantly (PMID: 18597830).
- Sildenafil 25 mg qd and 25 mg bid significantly improved I-PSS vs placebo (p=0.020 and p=0.025, respectively), and sildenafil 50 mg qd specifically improved nocturia significantly vs placebo (p=0.027); Qmax and PVR did not change significantly in any group (PMID: 28590828).
- In a pilot RCT, sildenafil monotherapy improved IPSS by -11.8% (significant), and the combination of alfuzosin and sildenafil improved IPSS by -24.1%, superior to either monotherapy (p<0.03) (PMID: 17258855).
- A single dose of sildenafil 50 mg significantly increased Qmax (15.6 to 19.3 cc/s, p<0.0001) and average flow rate (7.3 to 9.1 cc/s, p<0.0001) in men with ED and BPH/LUTS (PMID: 18649004).
- Ex vivo experiments showed that sildenafil combined with doxazosin exerted greater relaxing effects on prostatic strips than either compound alone, and sildenafil significantly enhanced the relaxing effect of doxazosin on electrically-stimulated prostatic contractions (PMID: 19143907).
- In a randomized study of alfuzosin alone vs alfuzosin plus sildenafil for LUTS/BPH, both groups showed similar improvements in IPSS, Qmax, and QoL, with no significant advantage for the combination (PMID: 22211956).
- In a randomized placebo-controlled study, combination of tamsulosin plus sildenafil showed no significant advantage over tamsulosin alone for trial without catheter success rate (70% vs 62.7%, p=0.3) or repeated acute urinary retention at follow-up (PMID: 25010303).
- In an open comparative study, combined sildenafil and doxazosin GITS significantly improved both IIEF-5 and IPSS scores, whereas sildenafil monotherapy only significantly improved IIEF-5 scores but not IPSS compared with pre-treatment (PMID: 21602833).

### Stroke

#### Literature — Stroke

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 2

The OxHARP randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial (PMID: 38832504) enrolled 65–69 patients after nonembolic cerebrovascular events with white matter hyperintensities and found that sildenafil 50 mg three times daily improved cerebrovascular reactivity (CVR-TCD, CVR-WMH, CVR-normal-appearing white matter), cerebral perfusion, and cerebrovascular conductance compared to placebo, though it did not reduce the primary endpoint of cerebral pulsatility. A secondary analysis of the same OxHARP trial (PMID: 40641043) confirmed that cerebrovascular conductance index and gray matter perfusion on MRI were the most sensitive markers of drug response, with all markers associated with white matter hyperintensity volume and improved by sildenafil. Together, the evidence supports further investigation of sildenafil for cerebrovascular dysfunction relevant to stroke prevention in small vessel disease, though no direct stroke outcome data are available from these abstracts.

**Key findings:**
- Sildenafil 50 mg three times daily improved cerebrovascular reactivity on transcranial ultrasound (CVR-TCD: +0.83 cm/s per mm Hg, p=0.007) and on MRI within white matter hyperintensities (CVR-WMH: +0.07, p=0.043) and normal-appearing white matter (CVR-NAWM: +0.06, p=0.048) versus placebo in patients after nonembolic cerebrovascular events (PMID: 38832504).
- Sildenafil increased cerebral perfusion in white matter hyperintensities (+1.82 mL/100g/min, p=0.008) and normal-appearing white matter (+2.12 mL/100g/min, p=0.006) versus placebo (PMID: 38832504).
- Sildenafil reduced cerebrovascular resistance (sildenafil vs placebo: 0.08, p=4.9×10⁻⁸) in patients with cerebral small vessel disease after cerebrovascular events (PMID: 38832504).
- Sildenafil did not reduce the primary endpoint of middle cerebral artery pulsatility versus placebo (0.02, -0.01 to 0.05; p=0.18) in the OxHARP trial (PMID: 38832504).
- In the OxHARP trial analysis, cerebrovascular conductance index (CVCi) was the most sensitive marker of sildenafil response, requiring only 20 patients for a crossover trial at 80% power, and all markers including CVCi, gray matter perfusion, and CVR indices were associated with white matter hyperintensity volume and improved by sildenafil (PMID: 40641043).
- Repeatability was greatest for mean flow velocity, pulsatility, CVCi, and CVR-WMH (ICC >0.8), and CVR-TCD was associated with CVR on MRI (CVR-WMH: r²=0.12, p=0.02; CVR-GM: r²=0.22, p=0.001) in the OxHARP population treated with sildenafil (PMID: 40641043).

### Ischemic Stroke

#### Literature — Ischemic Stroke

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 8

The evidence supports repurposing sildenafil for ischemic stroke, though the clinical data remain limited. Preclinical studies consistently show that sildenafil promotes neurogenesis, angiogenesis, and functional recovery in rat and gerbil models of cerebral ischemia (PMID: 12411660, PMID: 39335590), and a systematic review of 32 preclinical publications confirmed neuroprotective and neurorestorative effects of PDE5 inhibitors including sildenafil (PMID: 28648945). In humans, a small uncontrolled safety study found sildenafil 25 mg daily for 2 weeks appeared safe in subacute ischemic stroke patients (PMID: 19717023), an observational study found sildenafil associated with reduced ischemic stroke in LVAD patients with hemolysis (PMID: 29092891), and a meta-analysis in cerebral small vessel disease showed improved cerebral blood flow in white matter hyperintensity regions but no significant cognitive benefit and increased adverse events (PMID: 42051769).

**Key findings:**
- Sildenafil (2 or 5 mg/kg/day orally for 7 days starting 2 or 24 hours post-stroke) significantly enhanced neurological recovery and increased neurogenesis in the subventricular zone and dentate gyrus in a rat embolic stroke model, without reducing infarct volume (PMID: 12411660).
- A systematic review of 32 preclinical publications (3646 animals) found PDE5 inhibitors including sildenafil reduced neuronal apoptosis, oxidative stress, and neuroinflammation, increased angiogenesis, elevated regional cerebral blood flow in the ischemic penumbra, and improved functional recovery in animal models of ischemic stroke (PMID: 28648945).
- Sildenafil treatment in a gerbil model of global cerebral ischemia reduced astrocyte activation markers (GFAP and aquaporin-4 co-expression) and improved cognitive function across multiple behavioral tests (PMID: 39335590).
- A small uncontrolled safety study of sildenafil 25 mg daily for 2 weeks in 12 patients with subacute ischemic stroke found the drug appeared safe; among 10 survivors at 90 days, median NIHSS score was 2, median Barthel index was 95, and median modified Rankin score was 1.5 (PMID: 19717023).
- In an observational single-center study of HeartMate II patients with low-level hemolysis, sildenafil was associated with reduced device thrombosis and ischemic stroke (adjusted hazard ratio 1.7 vs 15 for LLH not on sildenafil), with reduced mean platelet volume increase (PMID: 29092891).
- A meta-analysis of 4 RCTs (236 patients) of PDE5 inhibitors including sildenafil in cerebral small vessel disease showed significantly increased cerebral blood flow in white matter hyperintensity regions (MD = 1.31 mL/100 g/min, p=0.002) but no significant cognitive improvements and an increased overall risk of adverse events (RR=2.36, p=0.0001) (PMID: 42051769).
- Combined atorvastatin and sildenafil (0.3 mg/kg each) in a mouse transient MCA occlusion model produced significantly better performance in the adhesive removal test compared to control and atorvastatin alone, but sildenafil alone did not reach statistical significance versus control in that test, and no group differences were found in infarct volume (PMID: 22854903).
- A narrative review concluded that PDE5 inhibitors including sildenafil may have potential roles in acute ischemic stroke management through modulation of cerebral blood flow, cAMP/cGMP/NO pathway, and neuroinflammation, and recommended further preclinical and clinical studies (PMID: 37395897).

### Acute Kidney Injury

#### Literature — Acute Kidney Injury

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 4

The evidence for sildenafil in acute kidney injury (AKI) is mixed. Two clinical RCTs found no renoprotective benefit of sildenafil — one in robotic partial nephrectomy with hilar clamping (PMID: 27613357) and one in cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass (PMID: 32245569), with the latter also showing a statistically significant increase in multiple organ dysfunction scores in the sildenafil group. In contrast, a rat model of ischemia-reperfusion-induced AKI demonstrated significant renoprotection via PPAR-γ agonism (PMID: 26850186), while a case report documented reversible AKI from acute tubular necrosis following sildenafil overdose (PMID: 30473955), suggesting potential harm at supratherapeutic doses.

**Key findings:**
- In a double-blind RCT of sildenafil 100 mg orally prior to robotic partial nephrectomy, there was no significant difference in eGFR decline between sildenafil and placebo at 1 day, 2 days, 1 month, or intermediate follow-up, indicating no renoprotective effect (PMID: 27613357).
- In a double-blind RCT of IV sildenafil citrate 12.5 mg/kg in cardiac surgery patients at risk for AKI, there was no significant difference in serum creatinine between groups, and sildenafil was associated with a statistically significant increase in multiple organ dysfunction scores (MD: 0.54 [0.02, 1.07]; P=0.044) (PMID: 32245569).
- In a rat model, sildenafil (0.5 and 1.0 mg/kg IP) administered 1 hour before bilateral renal ischemia-reperfusion significantly protected against AKI as measured by creatinine clearance, blood urea nitrogen, oxidative stress markers, and histopathology; this protection was abolished by a PPAR-γ antagonist (PMID: 26850186).
- A case report described a 67-year-old man who developed reversible AKI with acute tubular necrosis (peak serum creatinine 5.07 mg/dL) following a sildenafil overdose of 400 mg, with recovery on supportive care (PMID: 30473955).

### Astrocytoma (excluding Glioblastoma)

#### Literature — Astrocytoma (excluding Glioblastoma)

**Evidence strength:** weak, supports
**Relevant studies:** 4

Preclinical and review evidence indicates that sildenafil, a PDE5 inhibitor, selectively increases blood-brain tumor barrier permeability in rat brain tumor models and enhances chemotherapy delivery (PMID: 18674521). In vitro and mechanistic studies show sildenafil kills stem-like glioblastoma cells in combination with OSU-03012 and disrupts chaperone networks relevant to brain cancer (PMID: 25736380, PMID: 25546329). A focused review on PDE5 inhibitors in glioblastoma multiforme (GBM) discusses antitumor mechanisms including apoptosis induction, growth suppression, and reversal of tumor microenvironment immunosuppression, while also noting contradictory pro-oncogenic cGMP signals and limited clinical trial data (PMID: 35050491). No human clinical evidence specifically for astrocytoma excluding glioblastoma was identified; all relevant studies involve glioblastoma or gliosarcoma models.

**Key findings:**
- Oral administration of sildenafil selectively increased tumor capillary permeability in 9L gliosarcoma-bearing rats with no significant increase in normal brain capillaries, mediated by increased tumor cGMP levels and vesicular transport (PMID: 18674521).
- Sildenafil interacted in a greater-than-additive fashion with OSU-03012 to kill stem-like GBM cells in vitro, and the combination was more efficacious than celecoxib/sildenafil at killing tumor cells in vivo without damaging normal tissues (PMID: 25736380).
- OSU-03012/sildenafil treatment caused rapid degradation of GRP78 and other HSP70 and HSP90 family chaperone proteins and abolished expression of multiple oncogenic growth factor receptors and plasma membrane drug efflux pumps in brain cancer stem cells (PMID: 25736380).
- PDE5 inhibitors including sildenafil have been reported to trigger apoptosis, suppress tumor cell growth and invasion, and reverse tumor microenvironment immunosuppression in GBM, though contradictory pro-oncogenic roles for neuronal cGMP have also been noted and only a few clinical trials are evaluating PDE5 inhibitors in GBM (PMID: 35050491).
- OSU-03012/sildenafil killed brain cancer stem cells and decreased expression of viral entry receptors, and PDE5 inhibitors enhanced OSU-03012 killing in bacterial models, illustrating broad mechanistic activity of the combination (PMID: 25546329).
