## Findings by Disease

### Attention Deficit-hyperactivity Disorder

#### Literature — Attention Deficit-hyperactivity Disorder

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 11

The preponderance of controlled trial evidence supports bupropion as an effective treatment for ADHD across age groups and formulations. Multiple randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in both adults and children demonstrated statistically significant reductions in ADHD symptoms with bupropion versus placebo (PMIDs: 11156812, 8885585, 15820237, 25325205), and one active-comparator crossover trial found bupropion equivalent to methylphenidate (PMID: 7775360). The two contradicting studies (PMIDs: 11791949, 16102908) were underpowered or conducted in highly specialized comorbid populations (methadone-maintained patients) with high placebo response rates, and do not offset the consistent positive signal from the broader controlled evidence base.

**Key findings:**
- In a 6-week double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT in adults, bupropion SR produced a 42% reduction in ADHD symptoms vs. 24% for placebo, with 76% of bupropion-treated patients responding vs. 37% on placebo (PMID: 11156812).
- A multisite, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in children aged 6–12 found significant treatment effects for bupropion on both teacher and parent Conners ratings as well as cognitive performance measures, concluding bupropion may be a useful addition to available ADHD treatments (PMID: 8885585).
- A multisite, 8-week placebo-controlled RCT of bupropion XL in 162 adults with ADHD showed 53% responders vs. 31% on placebo (p=.004), with an effect size of 0.6 on the ADHD Rating Scale (PMID: 15820237).
- A double-blind crossover trial in 15 youths aged 7–17 found bupropion and methylphenidate produced equivalent and statistically significant improvements on teacher/parent ratings, CGI, and cognitive measures (PMID: 7775360).
- A 6-week double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT in 42 adults found a significant difference in CAARS scores favoring bupropion 150 mg/day over placebo at week 6 (PMID: 25325205).
- A randomized, double-blind, parallel-design study in 30 adults found response rates of 64% for bupropion SR and 27% for placebo, but the difference did not reach statistical significance (p=0.14), likely due to small sample size (PMID: 11791949).
- A 12-week double-blind, three-arm RCT in 98 methadone-maintained patients with ADHD found no significant differences between bupropion SR, methylphenidate SR, and placebo, with a high placebo response rate of 46% in this specialized comorbid population (PMID: 16102908).
- An open trial in 19 adults with ADHD found 14 of 19 patients experienced moderate to marked benefit from bupropion, with 10 choosing to continue it over their prior medication (PMID: 2115746).
- A 12-week single-blind trial in 11 adults with comorbid cocaine dependence and ADHD found significant reductions in attention difficulties, hyperactivity, and impulsivity with bupropion, with improvements similar to those previously observed with methylphenidate (PMID: 11916368).
- A 5-week open-label trial in 13 adolescent boys with ADHD, conduct disorder, and substance use disorders found significant declines in Conners Hyperactivity Index scores and CGI-S ratings with bupropion (PMID: 9847499).

### Bipolar Disorder

#### Literature — Bipolar Disorder

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 11

The preponderance of evidence from controlled trials, observational studies, and uncontrolled reports supports bupropion as an efficacious treatment for bipolar depression with a relatively favorable safety profile. Multiple randomized trials demonstrate significant antidepressant response rates for bupropion in bipolar depression (PMID: 7929019, PMID: 16880481, PMID: 12180276, PMID: 10701483, PMID: 37122643), and a large retrospective study found bupropion monotherapy was associated with significantly lower risk of psychiatric hospitalization than lithium (PMID: 29920885). While two uncontrolled case series raised concerns about mood switching (PMID: 1487473, PMID: 2856918), the controlled evidence consistently shows bupropion carries lower switch risk than comparators such as venlafaxine and desipramine (PMID: 7929019, PMID: 16880481, PMID: 16449476), and the mixed-verdict papers do not offset this pattern.

**Key findings:**
- In a double-blind trial, bupropion and desipramine showed similar antidepressant efficacy in bipolar depression, but mania/hypomania occurred in only 1/9 bupropion-treated patients versus 5/10 desipramine-treated patients (PMID: 7929019).
- In a 10-week randomized trial of 174 bipolar depressed patients, bupropion, sertraline, and venlafaxine showed similar acute response rates (49–53%) and remission rates (34–41%), with venlafaxine carrying significantly higher switch risk than bupropion (PMID: 16880481).
- In a single-blind randomized study, bupropion SR produced a 59% response rate (≥50% HDRS reduction) in bipolar I/II depression with no cases of affective switch (PMID: 12180276).
- In a double-blind comparison, both idazoxan and bupropion produced approximately 50% reductions in Hamilton Depression scores in bipolar I depressed patients over 6 weeks (PMID: 10701483).
- In a large retrospective study of 190,894 individuals with bipolar or schizoaffective disorder, bupropion monotherapy was associated with a significantly lower risk of psychiatric hospitalization than lithium (RR = 0.80, P = 2.80 × 10⁻⁴) (PMID: 29920885).
- In a randomized triple-blind trial of bipolar II depressed patients, both bupropion and venlafaxine significantly reduced HDRS scores after 4 weeks with no significant difference between groups, supporting bupropion as an effective short-term antidepressant monotherapy (PMID: 37122643).
- In a continuation trial of adjunctive antidepressants in bipolar depression, venlafaxine had the highest ratio of threshold switches to subthreshold hypomanias, while bupropion had the lowest switch ratio among the three agents studied (PMID: 16449476).
- An open trial of 11 bipolar/schizoaffective patients maintained on bupropion for 1 year or more showed little or no relapse and few side effects, suggesting mood-stabilizing properties (PMID: 2856918).
- An uncontrolled case series of 11 bipolar patients found that 6 of 11 experienced manic or hypomanic episodes requiring bupropion discontinuation, raising caution about switch risk, though this was not a controlled study (PMID: 1487473).

### Cocaine Dependence

#### Literature — Cocaine Dependence

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 7

The evidence for bupropion in cocaine dependence is mixed, with the highest-quality controlled trials generally failing to show a significant overall benefit. The largest RCTs found no statistically significant difference between bupropion and placebo on primary cocaine use outcomes (PMID: 18551884; PMID: 36920397), while one controlled trial suggested benefit only in a specific subgroup (abstinence initiation) and in combination with contingency management (PMID: 16461866; PMID: 36920397). Positive signals come from a small uncontrolled case series in ADHD-comorbid patients (PMID: 11916368) and preclinical rat data (PMID: 39881654), which are insufficient to offset the null findings from controlled trials.

**Key findings:**
- A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (N=70) found no statistically significant differences between bupropion and placebo in urine drug screen results, treatment retention, cocaine craving, or depressive symptoms when combined with cognitive behavioral therapy (PMID: 18551884).
- A 25-week placebo-controlled RCT in methadone-maintained patients found that combining contingency management with bupropion significantly reduced cocaine-positive samples during weeks 3–13, but bupropion alone (without CM) showed no significant improvement in cocaine use (PMID: 16461866).
- A 26-week double-blind RCT (N=80) found no significant overall difference in cocaine abstinence between bupropion SR and placebo; however, in the abstinence initiation subgroup, bupropion SR produced more cocaine-negative samples (30.3 vs. 17.1) and higher end-of-study abstinence rates (66.7% vs. 30.0%, P=.04) (PMID: 36920397).
- A small (N=8) within-subject placebo-controlled laboratory study found that acute bupropion pre-treatment reduced choice of high-dose cocaine versus money but also enhanced positive subjective effects of cocaine, yielding contradictory signals (PMID: 22168398).
- An uncontrolled 12-week single-blind trial (N=11) in cocaine-dependent adults with comorbid ADHD reported significant reductions in self-reported cocaine use, craving, and cocaine-positive toxicologies with bupropion, but lacked a placebo control (PMID: 11916368).
- In male and female Wistar rats, systemic bupropion at 60 mg/kg attenuated cocaine self-administration during extended-access sessions without altering locomotor activity or food intake, with greater efficacy in females during the first hour (PMID: 39881654).

### Alcohol Dependence

#### Literature — Alcohol Dependence

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 4

Evidence on bupropion monotherapy for alcohol use disorder is mixed. The only randomized controlled trial found that bupropion alone (Placebo + Bupropion arm) did not significantly reduce either primary outcome (B-PEth or %HDD) compared to placebo, as the statistical hierarchy was precluded after the bupropion-alone arm was not formally tested (PMID: 40487775). The sole preclinical study found that bupropion alone at two doses also failed to reduce voluntary alcohol intake in alcohol-preferring rats, though combination with naltrexone was effective (PMID: 29222923).

**Key findings:**
- In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n=384), the Placebo + Bupropion arm did not demonstrate statistically significant reductions in B-PEth or %HDD versus placebo, as the sequential hierarchical analysis was stopped before bupropion-alone comparisons could be made (PMID: 40487775).
- The combination of Varenicline + Bupropion reduced both B-PEth (Cohen's d=0.39, p=0.004) and %HDD (d=0.31, p=0.008) versus placebo, but the effect was not significantly different from Varenicline alone, making bupropion's independent contribution unclear (PMID: 40487775).
- Bupropion alone at doses of 10.0 mg/kg and 20.0 mg/kg failed to reduce voluntary alcohol intake in alcohol-preferring (P) rats, while the combination of low-dose naltrexone + bupropion significantly reduced alcohol consumption (PMID: 29222923).
- Bupropion reduced varenicline-induced nausea incidence (36/100 vs 49/96, p=0.048) and duration in the RCT, suggesting a tolerability benefit in combination but not a standalone efficacy signal for AUD (PMID: 40487775).

### Neuropathic Pain

#### Literature — Neuropathic Pain

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 6

The totality of evidence supports repurposing bupropion for neuropathic pain. Two randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trials both demonstrated statistically significant pain reduction versus placebo (PMID: 11706096; PMID: 42956041), with the larger trial noting a modest but real effect and positioning bupropion as an alternative for patients intolerant of first-line agents (PMID: 42956041). Preclinical and mechanistic studies consistently corroborate these findings by showing antihyperalgesic effects mediated through spinal noradrenaline and dopamine pathways (PMID: 25427287) and reversal of pain-depressed behavior in rodent models (PMID: 26588213; PMID: 38958173).

**Key findings:**
- In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial of 41 nondepressed patients, bupropion SR reduced mean average pain scores by 1.7 points versus no change with placebo (p < 0.001), with 73% of patients reporting improved or much improved pain relief (PMID: 11706096).
- In a larger randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial of 67 patients with peripheral neuropathic pain, bupropion produced a statistically significant reduction in daily average pain of -0.5 NRS points versus placebo (95% CI -0.8 to -0.1, p = 0.005), though NNT for 50% pain reduction was 15, higher than first-line treatments (PMID: 42956041).
- Intrathecal bupropion produced dose-dependent antihyperalgesic effects in a rat spinal nerve ligation model, mediated through spinal α2-adrenoceptor and dopamine D2 receptor pathways, with microdialysis confirming increased noradrenaline and dopamine in the dorsal horn (PMID: 25427287).
- Bupropion reversed formalin-induced depression of intracranial self-stimulation and mechanical allodynia in rats, with effects sustained during repeated treatment, consistent with its clinical efficacy in neuropathic pain (PMID: 26588213).
- Co-administration of bupropion and citicoline produced additive anti-nociceptive effects in sciatic nerve-ligated mice, as measured by tail-flick and hot-plate latencies (PMID: 38958173).

**Supporting PMIDs:** [11706096](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11706096/), [42956041](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42956041/), [25427287](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25427287/), [38958173](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38958173/), [26588213](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26588213/)

### Narcolepsy

#### Literature — Narcolepsy

**Evidence strength:** weak, supports
**Relevant studies:** 2

Two uncontrolled case reports describe bupropion improving narcolepsy-related symptoms including excessive daytime sleepiness, cataplexy, depression, and REM-sleep propensity in individual patients (PMID: 24930928; PMID: 9614600). In one case, objective sleep latency normalized and REM-sleep propensity reversed with bupropion, reverting upon discontinuation (PMID: 9614600). The evidence supports repurposing bupropion for narcolepsy, though it is limited to case-level observations.

**Key findings:**
- A 58-year-old woman with narcolepsy-cataplexy syndrome unresponsive to imipramine and modafinil became asymptomatic after 3 months on bupropion 300 mg/day, with Epworth Sleepiness Scale score improving from 14 at baseline (PMID: 24930928).
- A 46-year-old narcoleptic woman treated with bupropion 100 mg t.i.d. showed normalization of mood (BDI from 24 to 6), objective sleepiness (mean sleep latency from 1.8 to 9.1 minutes), and REM-sleep propensity, with all parameters reverting to pretreatment levels upon discontinuation (PMID: 9614600).
- The authors of PMID 9614600 proposed that bupropion's reversal of sleepiness and REM-sleep propensity in narcolepsy may be due to blockade of dopamine or norepinephrine reuptake, and called for further investigation in controlled trials.

**Evidence strength:** weak, supports
**Relevant studies:** 1

### Binge Eating Disorder

#### Literature — Binge Eating Disorder

**Evidence strength:** moderate, contradicts
**Relevant studies:** 3

The evidence for bupropion in binge eating disorder is mixed. A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (PMID: 23656848) found that bupropion did not improve binge eating frequency, food craving, or eating disorder features relative to placebo, failing its primary endpoint for BED, though it produced modest weight loss. A retrospective cohort study (PMID: 21927936) reported that bupropion reduced binge frequency alongside depressive symptoms in patients with comorbid BED and depression, and an animal model study (PMID: 41679557) found that bupropion alone and in combination suppressed binge-like intake of chocolate in rats.

**Key findings:**
- In a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial of 61 overweight/obese women with BED, bupropion 300 mg/day for 8 weeks did not significantly differ from placebo on binge-eating frequency, food craving, or eating disorder psychopathology, failing its primary BED endpoint (PMID: 23656848).
- Bupropion produced significantly greater weight loss than placebo (1.8% vs 0.6% BMI loss; p=0.002) in women with BED, though the effect was described as modest (PMID: 23656848).
- In a retrospective cohort study of depressed patients with BED, bupropion 150 mg/day reduced binge frequency and anxious-depressive symptoms over 24 weeks, with greater weight loss than sertraline (PMID: 21927936).
- In a rat model of binge-like eating, bupropion alone and in combination with naltrexone suppressed binge-like intake of chocolate, suggesting a pharmacological effect on binge eating behavior (PMID: 41679557).

### Post-traumatic Stress Disorder

#### Literature — Post-traumatic Stress Disorder

**Evidence strength:** moderate, contradicts
**Relevant studies:** 5

The preponderance of evidence contradicts the repurposing of bupropion for PTSD. A placebo-controlled trial found no significant effect of bupropion SR on PTSD symptoms (PMID: 17414245), a systematic review and meta-analysis found bupropion failed to differentiate from placebo (PMID: 27126398), and a network meta-analysis found bupropion similar to placebo for sleep quality disturbances in PTSD (PMID: 35395322). The only supporting evidence comes from a small case series of four patients with comorbid PTSD and methamphetamine use disorder (PMID: 39175424), which is insufficient to offset the consistent negative findings from higher-quality controlled studies.

**Key findings:**
- A placebo-controlled 8-week trial of bupropion SR (n=30) found no significant between-group differences in PTSD symptom reduction compared to placebo (PMID: 17414245).
- A systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized clinical trials found that bupropion failed to differentiate from placebo in the treatment of PTSD (PMID: 27126398).
- A systematic review and network meta-analysis of RCTs found bupropion similar to placebo for improving sleep quality in PTSD patients (MD -2.28, 95% CrI -4.75 to 0.21, very low certainty of evidence) (PMID: 35395322).
- A small case series of four patients with comorbid PTSD and methamphetamine use disorder reported greater reductions in PTSD checklist scores and lower relapse rates with bupropion-containing regimens compared to serotonergic agents alone, but the very small sample and uncontrolled design severely limit the generalizability of this finding (PMID: 39175424).

### Schizophrenia

#### Literature — Schizophrenia

**Evidence strength:** moderate
**Relevant studies:** 1

A systematic review (PMID: 24201231) covering 13 RCTs and additional observational data found that bupropion added to stable antipsychotic regimens in schizophrenia patients produced marked clinical improvement across depression, negative symptoms, cognition, and smoking cessation without inducing psychosis in 229 patients, while also identifying 30 cases of bupropion-induced psychosis, predominantly in patients without concomitant antipsychotic coverage. The evidence thus points in both directions: benefit when used adjunctively with antipsychotics, but risk of psychosis when used without them (PMID: 24201231). Overall the evidence supports cautious repurposing as adjunctive therapy but is mixed given the safety signal in unprotected patients.

**Key findings:**
- 229 schizophrenic patients on stable antipsychotic regimens were successfully treated with bupropion and experienced marked clinical improvement without developing psychosis (PMID: 24201231).
- 30 cases of bupropion-induced psychosis were identified; 57% were associated with the immediate-release formulation and 93% occurred without concomitant antipsychotic medication (PMID: 24201231).
- The systematic review incorporated 13 randomized controlled trials (28 publications), 3 open prospective evaluations, 5 multiple case reports, and 22 single case reports evaluating bupropion in schizophrenia (PMID: 24201231).
- Pharmacokinetic interactions with antipsychotics were rare, but electroencephalographic abnormalities occurred frequently with bupropion use in schizophrenia patients (PMID: 24201231).
- The authors concluded that the efficacy of combined dopamine and norepinephrine agonism in schizophrenia is biologically plausible and called for further trials focusing on negative symptoms and cognitive deficits (PMID: 24201231).
