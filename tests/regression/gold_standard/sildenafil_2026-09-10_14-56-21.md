## Findings by Disease

### Raynaud Disease

#### Literature — Raynaud Disease

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 4

The preponderance of evidence supports sildenafil as an efficacious treatment for Raynaud's phenomenon. Two randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials demonstrated significant improvements in attack frequency and microvascular blood flow with sildenafil versus placebo (PMID: 21360507; PMID: 28281457), and an uncontrolled pilot study reported significant improvement in RP severity (PMID: 22833239). The one mixed-verdict study found a greater-than-90% probability that sildenafil outperformed placebo on most outcomes but noted that the aggregated effect size was not clinically relevant and that patient response was highly heterogeneous (PMID: 30383134), which tempers but does not override the consistent positive signal from the controlled trials.

**Key findings:**
- In a double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT of 57 patients with RP secondary to limited cutaneous systemic sclerosis, modified-release sildenafil produced a mean 44.0% reduction in attacks per week versus 18.1% for placebo (P=0.034) (PMID: 21360507).
- In a double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT of 41 SSc patients, sildenafil 100 mg/day for 8 weeks significantly improved finger blood flow before and after cold stimulus (p=0.026 and p=0.028, respectively) and reduced RP duration and VAS severity scores compared to placebo (PMID: 28281457).
- An open-label, uncontrolled pilot study of 16 SSc patients treated with sildenafil 25 mg three times daily for 3 months showed statistically significant improvement in severity of Raynaud's phenomenon, with no new digital infarcts or ulcers and healing of existing ulcers (PMID: 22833239).
- A series of n-of-1 randomized, double-blind trials in 38 patients found a greater-than-90% probability that sildenafil at 40 mg or 80 mg was superior to placebo for most outcomes, but the aggregated effect size was not clinically relevant and substantial inter-patient heterogeneity was observed (PMID: 30383134).

**Supporting PMIDs:** [30383134](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30383134/), [21360507](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21360507/), [28281457](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28281457/), [22833239](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22833239/)

**Contradicting PMIDs:** [30383134](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30383134/)

### Heart Failure

#### Literature — Heart Failure

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 8

The evidence on sildenafil for heart failure is mixed, with earlier smaller randomized controlled trials in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) showing improvements in cardiac output, exercise capacity, diastolic function, and cardiac remodeling (PMIDs 16275194, 18036451, 18381181, 21036891), while larger or more recent controlled trials—particularly in HFrEF with pulmonary hypertension and in heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF)—failed to demonstrate meaningful clinical benefit on symptoms, exercise capacity, or quality of life (PMIDs 35596935, 27873388, 23241003). A mechanistic ancillary study further revealed opposing ventricular and vascular effects of sildenafil in HFpEF, with reduced left ventricular contractility potentially offsetting vascular benefits (PMID 25782985), underscoring the complexity and context-dependence of the drug's effects.

**Key findings:**
- In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover study of 20 HFrEF patients, sildenafil 50 mg significantly increased cardiac index by 0.37 L/min/m² and reduced total systemic resistance, aortic stiffness, and wave reflection (PMID 16275194).
- A 6-month randomized placebo-controlled trial in 46 CHF patients found sildenafil 50 mg twice daily significantly improved peak VO2 (from 14.8 to 18.7 mL/min/kg), reduced VE/VCO2 slope, and improved endothelial function compared to placebo (PMID 18036451).
- A 4-week randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial in 19 HFrEF patients showed sildenafil improved maximal oxygen uptake, ventilatory efficiency, and reduced pulmonary artery systolic pressure compared to placebo (PMID 18381181).
- A 1-year randomized placebo-controlled study in 45 systolic HF patients demonstrated sildenafil 50 mg three times daily improved LV ejection fraction, diastolic function (E/E' ratio from 13.1 to 9.4), reversed cardiac remodeling, and improved peak VO2 and quality of life (PMID 21036891).
- The multinational SilHF randomized controlled trial in 69 HFrEF patients with pulmonary hypertension found sildenafil did not improve symptoms, quality of life, pulmonary artery systolic pressure, or 6-minute walk test distance compared to placebo, and was associated with numerically more serious adverse events (33% vs. 21%) (PMID 35596935).
- A 12-week randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial in 52 HFpEF patients with pulmonary hypertension found sildenafil 60 mg three times daily did not improve cardiac structure, function, integrative exercise responses, or quality of life compared to placebo (PMID 27873388).
- A 12-week randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial in 106 systolic HF patients found sildenafil did not significantly reduce mean blood pressure (placebo-corrected effect 1.16 mmHg, P>0.05) or improve 6-minute walk test distance (nonsignificant +14 m, P=0.67) (PMID 23241003).
- An ancillary study of 48 RELAX trial participants with HFpEF found sildenafil reduced arterial elastance (beneficial vascular effect) but also reduced left ventricular contractility by 11–16% compared to placebo, with no effect on pulmonary artery systolic pressure, suggesting opposing ventricular and vascular effects (PMID 25782985).

**Supporting PMIDs:** [16275194](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16275194/), [18036451](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18036451/), [18381181](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18381181/), [25782985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25782985/), [21036891](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21036891/)

**Contradicting PMIDs:** [27873388](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27873388/), [35596935](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35596935/), [23241003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23241003/), [25782985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25782985/)

### Eisenmenger Syndrome

#### Literature — Eisenmenger Syndrome

**Evidence strength:** strong, supports
**Relevant studies:** 4

Multiple clinical studies support the use of sildenafil for Eisenmenger syndrome. A randomized, placebo-controlled crossover trial demonstrated significant improvements in 6-minute walk distance, pulmonary artery pressure, and NYHA class with sildenafil monotherapy in Eisenmenger syndrome patients (PMID: 16569546). Prospective studies further confirmed improvements in exercise capacity, oxygen saturation, hemodynamic parameters, and quality of life with oral sildenafil treatment over 3–12 months (PMID: 20304507, PMID: 17174418, PMID: 21948962). The evidence supports repurposing sildenafil for Eisenmenger syndrome.

**Key findings:**
- In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover study of 10 Eisenmenger syndrome patients, sildenafil improved 6-minute walk distance from 262±99 to 358.9±96.5 m (P<0.0001) and reduced pulmonary artery pressure from 98.8±20.5 to 78.3±15.3 mmHg (P<0.0001) (PMID: 16569546)
- NYHA class improved from 2.65±0.59 to 1.55±0.51 (P<0.0001) and exercise duration improved from 6.4±3.1 to 10.2±2.05 minutes (P<0.0001) with sildenafil in Eisenmenger syndrome patients (PMID: 16569546)
- In a prospective open-label study of 12 Eisenmenger syndrome patients, 3 months of oral sildenafil improved 6MWT distance from 347.3±80.7 to 392.5±82.0 m (p=0.002) and significantly improved all components of the CAMPHOR quality-of-life score (total score 27.6±10.5 to 15.8±10.4, p=0.002) (PMID: 20304507)
- All 12 Eisenmenger syndrome patients improved from NYHA class III to NYHA class II after 3 months of sildenafil therapy with no major adverse events (PMID: 20304507)
- In a prospective open-label study comparing Eisenmenger syndrome (n=7) vs idiopathic PAH (n=6), 6 months of sildenafil produced significant reductions in systolic and mean pulmonary artery pressures and pulmonary vascular resistance, and increased pulmonary blood flow and arterial saturation in the Eisenmenger syndrome group (PMID: 17174418)
- In a prospective multicentre study of 84 Eisenmenger syndrome patients, 12 months of oral sildenafil 20 mg three times daily increased 6MWD by 56 m (95% CI 42–69, p<0.0001) and resting SaO2 by 2.4% (p<0.0001), with significant reductions in mean pulmonary arterial pressure and pulmonary vascular resistance index (PMID: 21948962)

### Covid-19

#### Literature — Covid-19

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 1

The single available controlled trial (PMID: 34980198) produced mixed results: sildenafil showed no significant improvement in the primary oxygenation outcomes (PaO2/FiO2 ratio and A-a gradient) but was associated with significant reductions in invasive mechanical ventilation requirement and hospital length of stay. Because comparably credible evidence points in both directions within the same trial, the overall direction is mixed.

**Key findings:**
- No statistically significant differences were found in mean PaO2/FiO2 ratios or A-a gradients between sildenafil and placebo groups (repeated-measures ANOVA p=0.67 and p=0.69, respectively) (PMID: 34980198).
- Invasive mechanical ventilation was required in 4 placebo patients and 0 sildenafil patients (log-rank p=0.04), suggesting a potential clinical benefit for this hard outcome (PMID: 34980198).
- Sildenafil-treated patients had a significantly shorter median hospital stay (9 days IQR 7–12) compared to placebo (12 days IQR 9–21, p=0.04), though the trial was a small pilot (n=40) and authors called for larger confirmatory studies (PMID: 34980198).

#### Literature — Infertility

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 1

The single available randomized placebo-controlled trial (PMID: 31710189) yields a mixed result: vaginal sildenafil was associated with an approximately two-fold increase in chemical pregnancy rates compared to placebo, but this difference did not reach statistical significance, likely due to the small sample size (n=66). The paper-level verdict is therefore mixed, reflecting a clinically suggestive but statistically unconfirmed benefit of sildenafil for repeated implantation failure patients undergoing IVF/ICSI.

**Key findings:**
- In a randomized placebo-controlled trial of 66 infertile women with repeated IVF/ICSI failures, vaginal sildenafil (100 mg/daily) was associated with a two-fold increase in chemical pregnancy rates compared to placebo, but this increase was statistically non-significant (PMID: 31710189).
- No significant difference in endometrial thickness on the hCG injection day was observed between the sildenafil and placebo groups (PMID: 31710189).
- Implantation rates appeared higher in the placebo/sildenafil combination group compared to other groups, though abortion rates did not differ significantly among groups (PMID: 31710189).
- The authors concluded that vaginal sildenafil may conceivably improve chemical pregnancy rates in repeated IVF failure patients, but called for larger randomized clinical trials to confirm the finding (PMID: 31710189).

### Coronary Artery Disorder

#### Literature — Coronary Artery Disorder

**Evidence strength:** moderate, supports
**Relevant studies:** 1

A pilot randomised controlled trial found that 50 mg daily oral sildenafil for 12 weeks significantly improved functional capacity, Duke treadmill score, and angina severity compared to placebo in patients with primary coronary slow flow phenomenon, a coronary artery disorder (PMID: 39214536). The evidence supports repurposing sildenafil for this coronary artery condition, though the trial was small (n=20) and conducted at a single centre (PMID: 39214536).

**Key findings:**
- All patients in the sildenafil group achieved CCS Class I (asymptomatic during regular physical activity) compared to only 40% in the placebo group (p=0.011) (PMID: 39214536)

### Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia

#### Literature — Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 2

The available controlled trial evidence presents a mixed picture for sildenafil monotherapy in BPH/LUTS. Sildenafil 25 mg qd and 25 mg bid significantly improved IPSS versus placebo, and 50 mg qd specifically improved nocturia, but no regimen improved objective urodynamic measures (Qmax, PVR) (PMID: 28590828). In the combination study, sildenafil monotherapy produced the smallest IPSS improvement (-11.8%) among the three arms and failed to improve frequency, nocturia, PVR, or Qmax as monotherapy, with those benefits confined to the alfuzosin-containing arms (PMID: 17258855).

**Key findings:**
- Sildenafil 25 mg qd and 25 mg bid significantly improved IPSS versus placebo (p=0.020 and p=0.025, respectively), with voiding domain more affected than storage domain (PMID: 28590828).
- Only sildenafil 50 mg qd improved nocturia significantly versus placebo (p=0.027), but no sildenafil regimen significantly changed Qmax or PVR (PMID: 28590828).
- In a three-arm pilot RCT, sildenafil 25 mg OD monotherapy produced the smallest IPSS improvement (-11.8%) compared to alfuzosin (-15.6%) and combination therapy (-24.1%), and did not significantly improve frequency, nocturia, Qmax, or PVR as monotherapy (PMID: 17258855).
- Combination of alfuzosin and sildenafil was superior to sildenafil monotherapy for both LUTS and erectile function outcomes (PMID: 17258855).

### Ischemic Stroke

#### Literature — Ischemic Stroke

**Evidence strength:** weak, supports
**Relevant studies:** 3

The evidence supports repurposing sildenafil for ischemic stroke, with preclinical and early clinical data pointing in a favorable direction. In rat and gerbil models, sildenafil promoted neurogenesis, reduced neurological deficits, mitigated astrocyte activation, and improved cognitive function after cerebral ischemia (PMID: 12411660; PMID: 39335590). A small human safety study (n=12) found sildenafil 25 mg daily for 2 weeks appeared safe in subacute ischemic stroke patients, with favorable functional outcomes at 90 days among survivors, though one sudden death occurred during treatment (PMID: 19717023).

**Key findings:**
- In rats with embolic middle cerebral artery occlusion, oral sildenafil (2 or 5 mg/kg/day for 7 days starting 2 or 24 hours post-stroke) significantly enhanced neurological recovery on foot-fault and adhesive removal tests and significantly increased neurogenesis in the subventricular zone and dentate gyrus without reducing infarct volume (PMID: 12411660).
- Sildenafil increased cortical cGMP levels and PDE5 mRNA was detected in both ischemic and non-ischemic brain tissue in rats, suggesting a mechanistic basis for its neuroprotective effects (PMID: 12411660).
- In a gerbil model of transient global cerebral ischemia, sildenafil-treated groups showed significantly reduced co-expression of GFAP and aquaporin-4 (markers of astrocyte activation and water homeostasis) compared to the ischemia-only group, and behavioral tests indicated improved cognitive function (PMID: 39335590).
- In a human safety study of 12 subacute ischemic stroke patients (NIHSS 2–21, days 2–9 post-onset) treated with sildenafil 25 mg daily for 2 weeks, the primary safety outcome (stroke worsening, new stroke, MI, vision/hearing loss, or death) occurred in one patient (sudden death); among 10 survivors at 90 days, median NIHSS was 2, median Barthel index was 95, and median modified Rankin score was 1.5 (PMID: 19717023).

### Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease

#### Literature — Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease

**Evidence strength:** strong, contradicts
**Relevant studies:** 4

The preponderance of controlled trial evidence does not support sildenafil as an effective treatment for COPD. Three randomized controlled trials found no improvement in exercise capacity, gas exchange, or dyspnea with sildenafil, with one trial additionally reporting worsened gas exchange and quality of life (PMIDs: 39389522, 22360383, 18342559). The single supporting trial (PMID: 21545068) showed benefit in severe COPD with pulmonary hypertension, but its findings are outweighed by the consistent negative results across the other controlled studies.

**Key findings:**
- Acute oral sildenafil (50 mg or 100 mg) failed to improve gas exchange efficiency, excess exercise ventilation, dyspnea, or exercise tolerance in patients with mild-to-moderate COPD in a randomized, placebo-controlled crossover trial (PMID: 39389522).
- In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial of patients with COPD and emphysema without pulmonary hypertension, sildenafil had no effect on 6-minute walk distance or peak oxygen consumption, and significantly worsened gas exchange (increased alveolar-arterial oxygen gradient), symptoms, and quality of life (PMID: 22360383).
- Sildenafil attenuated exercise-induced increases in mean pulmonary artery pressure in COPD patients, but this hemodynamic effect was not accompanied by improved stroke volume, cardiac output, or maximal exercise capacity in a study using right heart catheterization and cardiopulmonary exercise testing (PMID: 18342559).
- In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of 33 patients with severe COPD, sildenafil significantly improved 6-minute walk distance (median change +190 m vs. 0 m for placebo) and reduced pulmonary artery pressure after 12 weeks (PMID: 21545068).

**Supporting PMIDs:** [21545068](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21545068/)

**Contradicting PMIDs:** [39389522](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39389522/), [22360383](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22360383/), [18342559](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18342559/)

(PMIDs: [22360383](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22360383/), [20073379](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20073379/)).

### Acute Kidney Injury

#### Literature — Acute Kidney Injury

**Evidence strength:** moderate, contradicts
**Relevant studies:** 3

The controlled clinical evidence consistently shows that sildenafil does not provide renoprotection in acute kidney injury settings. Two randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials found no significant benefit on renal functional outcomes following hilar clamping during partial nephrectomy (PMID: 27613357) or cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass (PMID: 32245569), with the latter also noting a statistically significant increase in multiple organ dysfunction scores in the sildenafil group. The single supporting study is a preclinical rat model (PMID: 26850186), which does not offset the consistent negative findings from controlled human trials.

**Key findings:**
- A randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial of 30 patients found no significant difference in eGFR decline between sildenafil and placebo at 1 day, 2 days, 1 month, or intermediate follow-up after robotic partial nephrectomy with hilar clamping (PMID: 27613357).
- A double-blind RCT of 129 patients undergoing cardiac surgery found no statistically significant difference in serum creatinine between sildenafil and placebo groups (MD: 0.88 μmol/L [-5.82, 7.59]), and sildenafil was associated with a statistically significant increase in multiple organ dysfunction scores (MD: 0.54 [0.02, 1.07]; P=0.044) (PMID: 32245569).
- In a rat ischemia-reperfusion model, sildenafil (0.5 and 1.0 mg/kg i.p.) significantly protected against AKI as measured by creatinine clearance, blood urea nitrogen, oxidative stress markers, and histopathology, with the effect abolished by a PPAR-γ antagonist (PMID: 26850186).

### Endothelial Dysfunction

#### Literature — Endothelial Dysfunction

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 3

The evidence is mixed: two randomized controlled crossover trials demonstrate that sildenafil protects against ischemia-reperfusion-induced endothelial dysfunction (PMID: 15699265) and abrogates acute smoking-induced endothelial dysfunction (PMID: 15533731), while a third randomized double-blind placebo-controlled crossover study found that sildenafil does not improve peripheral endothelium-dependent vasomotor or fibrinolytic function in patients with established coronary heart disease (PMID: 15863522). All three studies are of comparable methodological quality (randomized, placebo-controlled, crossover designs), pointing in opposite directions depending on the clinical context, precluding a single unified conclusion.

**Key findings:**
- In a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled crossover study of 16 patients with coronary heart disease, sildenafil increased endothelium-independent vasodilatation but did not improve acetylcholine-mediated forearm blood flow or substance P-stimulated tissue plasminogen activator release, indicating no reversal of established endothelial dysfunction (PMID: 15863522).
- In a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled crossover study of 10 healthy volunteers, oral sildenafil 50 mg preserved flow-mediated dilatation after ischemia-reperfusion (FMD: 7.0% before vs. 6.2% after IR, P=NS), whereas placebo resulted in significant blunting of FMD (7.9% before vs. 1.2% after IR, P<0.01), demonstrating protection against IR-induced endothelial dysfunction via K(ATP) channel opening (PMID: 15699265).
- In a randomized placebo-controlled crossover study of 14 male smokers, sildenafil 50 mg abolished the acute smoking-induced decrease in brachial artery flow-mediated dilatation (FMD improved by 51% compared to placebo session, P<0.05), suggesting benefit in an acute oxidative stress model of endothelial dysfunction (PMID: 15533731).
- The protective effect of sildenafil against ischemia-reperfusion-induced endothelial dysfunction was completely abolished by glibenclamide, a K(ATP) channel blocker, confirming a mechanistic role for K(ATP) channels in sildenafil's endothelial protective effect (PMID: 15699265).
