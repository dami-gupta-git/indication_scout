## Drug Safety

_Drug-wide safety signal — applies to the drug generally, not to any single candidate indication below._

**Regulatory label:** FDA label boxed-warning text (7 product labels, LLM-summarized — see appendix for verbatim text):
All seven labels agree that the sole boxed-warning hazard is metformin-associated lactic acidosis, which has resulted in death, hypothermia, hypotension, and resistant bradyarrhythmias in postmarketing cases; onset is often subtle, with nonspecific symptoms (malaise, myalgias, respiratory distress, somnolence, abdominal pain) and laboratory findings of elevated blood lactate (>5 mmol/L), anion gap acidosis without ketonuria or ketonemia, increased lactate/pyruvate ratio, and metformin plasma levels generally >5 mcg/mL. Risk factors cited across all or most labels include renal impairment, concomitant use of certain drugs (e.g., carbonic anhydrase inhibitors such as topiramate), age ≥65 years, radiological studies with contrast, surgery and other procedures, hypoxic states (e.g., acute congestive heart failure), excessive alcohol intake, and hepatic impairment; if lactic acidosis is suspected, the product should be immediately discontinued and prompt hemodialysis initiated in a hospital setting. Two labels differ from the others in a clinically substantive way regarding the risk-factor list: ZITUVIMET and Glumetza explicitly add mitochondrial diseases as an additional named risk factor, whereas the remaining five labels (Metformin Hydrochloride generics and Riomet) do not include mitochondrial diseases in their boxed warnings. Additionally, the abbreviated highlight sections of the Metformin Hydrochloride generic labels (two separate products) express the age cutoff as ">65 years old" rather than "≥65 years old" or "65 years old or greater" used elsewhere, which could be read as excluding 65-year-olds themselves, though the full-text sections of those same labels use the inclusive "65 years old or greater" phrasing.

Open Targets warning metadata: warning type: Black Box Warning; toxicity categories: cardiotoxicity, metabolic toxicity, respiratory toxicity, vascular toxicity.

**Pharmacovigilance:** Open Targets/FAERS pharmacovigilance signals: lactic acidosis (logLR 27686.6; 7697 reports), acute kidney injury (logLR 10158.7; 5387 reports), metabolic acidosis (logLR 6146.0; 2428 reports), hypoglycaemia (logLR 5460.3; 2567 reports), hyperkalaemia (logLR 2224.8; 1327 reports). ## Findings by Disease

### Preeclampsia

#### Literature — Preeclampsia

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 8

Multiple RCTs and meta-analyses have examined metformin for preeclampsia with mixed results. One RCT found that extended-release metformin prolonged gestation in preterm pre-eclampsia by a median of 7.6 days (borderline significance in the intention-to-treat analysis, significant in per-protocol analyses) (PMID: 34551918), and a meta-analysis of RCTs comparing metformin to insulin found a reduced risk of pre-eclampsia with metformin (RR 0.68, 95% CI 0.48–0.95) (PMID: 29044702). However, a large RCT in women with early gestational or type 2 diabetes found no significant reduction in preterm preeclampsia with metformin added to insulin (aOR 1.04, 95% CI 0.70–1.56) (PMID: 39236318), and an RCT in PCOS pregnancies found no reduction in preeclampsia prevalence (7.4% vs 3.7%, P=0.18) (PMID: 20926533). A placental ageing study found no evidence that metformin decelerates placental ageing pathways associated with pre-eclampsia (PMID: 40448705), and a systematic review/meta-analysis concluded metformin showed greater preventive effects for pregnancy-induced hypertension than for preeclampsia specifically (PMID: 30399639).

**Key findings:**
- In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of 180 women with preterm pre-eclampsia, extended-release metformin prolonged gestation by a median of 7.6 days (geometric mean ratio 1.39, 95% CI 0.99–1.95; P=0.057) in the intention-to-treat analysis, and by 9.6 days among those who took any dose (GMR 1.67, 95% CI 1.16–2.42) (PMID: 34551918).
- No serious adverse events related to metformin were observed in the preterm pre-eclampsia trial, though diarrhoea was more common in the metformin arm (PMID: 34551918).
- A meta-analysis of 8 RCTs comparing metformin (n=838) with insulin (n=836) showed a reduced risk of pre-eclampsia with metformin (RR 0.68, 95% CI 0.48–0.95; P=0.02; I²=0%) (PMID: 29044702).
- A meta-analysis of 5 RCTs comparing metformin with placebo/control found no significant difference in pre-eclampsia risk (RR 0.86, 95% CI 0.33–2.26; P=0.76; I²=66%) (PMID: 29044702).
- Mean gestational weight gain was lower in the metformin group compared with both placebo (P=0.05) and insulin (P=0.004) groups across meta-analyzed studies (PMID: 29044702).
- In a randomized controlled trial of 831 women with early gestational or type 2 diabetes, metformin added to insulin was not associated with a significant reduction in preterm preeclampsia before 37 weeks (aOR 1.04, 95% CI 0.70–1.56) or before 34 weeks (aOR 1.43, 95% CI 0.73–2.81) (PMID: 39236318).
- Metformin was not associated with differences in serum biomarkers (soluble endoglin, sFlt-1, PlGF, VCAM-1, adiponectin, hsCRP, ApoB, VEGF) related to cardiovascular/preeclampsia risk (PMID: 39236318).
- In a randomized, placebo-controlled multicenter trial of 274 PCOS pregnancies, preeclampsia prevalence was 7.4% in the metformin group vs 3.7% in the placebo group (difference 3.7%; 95% CI -1.7 to 9.2; P=0.18), showing no significant reduction (PMID: 20926533).
- In a small randomized trial of 40 PCOS pregnancies, pregnancy complications including severe pre-eclampsia occurred only in the placebo group (7 of 22 vs 0 of 18; P=0.01), and metformin reduced uterine artery impedance between 12 and 19 weeks (PMID: 17330831).
- A systematic review and meta-analysis concluded that metformin showed greater preventive effects for pregnancy-induced hypertension than for preeclampsia specifically, and suggested metformin may gain a place in preventive treatments for PE once dosages, gestational age, and treatment time are optimized (PMID: 30399639).
- In a randomized trial of women with GDM, preeclampsia was significantly less common in metformin-treated groups compared with insulin alone, and mean birth weight was significantly lower in metformin groups (PMID: 25467617).
- A study of placental ageing using human RCT samples, isolated trophoblasts, and a mouse model found no evidence that metformin decelerates or accelerates placental ageing pathways associated with adverse pregnancy outcomes such as pre-eclampsia (PMID: 40448705).
- The placental ageing study found no differences between metformin-exposed and control placentas in telomere length, fibrosis, calcification, gene expression, or methylation patterns, and concluded metformin cannot be recommended to prevent adverse pregnancy outcomes on this basis (PMID: 40448705).

### Atherosclerosis

#### Literature — Atherosclerosis

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 6

Multiple clinical studies indicate that metformin exerts beneficial effects relevant to atherosclerosis across several patient populations. Metformin significantly reduced circulating MDA-LDL (an oxidized LDL and atherosclerosis risk factor) in type 2 diabetes patients (PMID: 25090264), improved peripheral arterial blood flow and raised HDL cholesterol in patients with peripheral atherosclerosis (PMID: 6209500), reduced major cardiovascular events compared to glipizide in type 2 diabetic patients with coronary artery disease (PMID: 23230096), improved arterial stiffness markers (PWV and AI) in NAFLD patients (PMID: 21411114), improved carotid artery diameter and blood flow in type 2 diabetes (PMID: 22892913), and reduced maximal carotid intima-media thickness progression in type 1 diabetes patients at cardiovascular risk, though the primary mean cIMT endpoint was not significantly reduced (PMID: 28615149). Taken together, the evidence supports a potential role for metformin in reducing atherosclerotic burden and cardiovascular risk beyond its approved indication.

**Key findings:**
- Increasing the dose of metformin significantly decreased MDA-LDL levels (from 94.40 to 77.83 U/L, P<0.005) in type 2 diabetes patients, and metformin treatment was the only independent factor associated with decreased MDA-LDL (PMID: 25090264).
- Metformin (850 mg tid) significantly increased peripheral arterial blood flow by +17.3% after 3 months and +40.0% after 6 months in patients with peripheral atherosclerosis, and raised HDL cholesterol by +8.3% during treatment (PMID: 6209500).
- In a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of type 2 diabetic patients with coronary artery disease, metformin treatment for 3 years reduced major cardiovascular events with an adjusted hazard ratio of 0.54 (95% CI 0.30–0.90; P=0.026) compared to glipizide over a median 5-year follow-up (PMID: 23230096).
- In a randomized, placebo-controlled trial of NAFLD patients, metformin significantly decreased pulse wave velocity and augmentation index (markers of arterial stiffness), alongside improvements in fasting glucose, triglycerides, and HDL cholesterol (PMID: 21411114).
- Metformin increased systolic carotid artery diameter and total and systolic blood flow in type 2 diabetes patients in a randomized crossover study, providing more protection against macrovascular complications compared to glimepiride (PMID: 22892913).
- In the REMOVAL double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial in type 1 diabetes patients at cardiovascular risk, metformin did not significantly reduce mean cIMT progression (primary endpoint; p=0.1664), but significantly reduced maximal cIMT progression (−0.013 mm/year; p=0.0093), LDL cholesterol (−0.13 mmol/L; p=0.0117), and bodyweight (−1.17 kg; p<0.0001) (PMID: 28615149).

### Polycystic Ovary Syndrome

#### Literature — Polycystic Ovary Syndrome

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 15

The body of evidence from multiple RCTs and controlled trials is mixed regarding metformin for polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). Several studies demonstrate benefits including improved menstrual regularity, reduced hyperandrogenism, improved ovulation rates when combined with clomiphene, higher clinical pregnancy rates in IVF, and reduced ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (PMID: 10206447, PMID: 10634377, PMID: 16501038, PMID: 20517830, PMID: 19757275, PMID: 23904883, PMID: 19552097, PMID: 19522426). However, other RCTs found no significant benefit on ovulation or pregnancy rates, and one large RCT showed clomiphene alone was superior to metformin alone for live birth, while metformin worsened ovarian response in PCOS patients with reduced ovarian reserve (PMID: 17287476, PMID: 16169430, PMID: 11994052, PMID: 19692630, PMID: 21917254). The evidence supports a role for metformin in PCOS, particularly in combination with clomiphene and for reducing OHSS risk, but its efficacy varies by patient phenotype, obesity status, and clinical context.

**Key findings:**
- Metformin induced resumption of normal menses in 39 of 43 (91%) previously amenorrheic women with PCOS, with reductions in testosterone and increases in estradiol independent of weight loss (PMID: 10206447)
- In a 6-month RCT, metformin improved menstrual frequency, reduced plasma insulin and free testosterone, and increased insulin sensitivity in PCOS women compared to placebo (PMID: 10634377)
- In a randomized placebo-controlled IVF trial, metformin significantly improved clinical pregnancy rates per cycle (38.5% vs 16.3%, p=0.023) and significantly reduced severe OHSS incidence (3.8% vs 20.4%, p=0.023) compared to placebo (PMID: 16501038)
- In a prospective comparative study, metformin combined with clomiphene achieved a 34% full-term pregnancy rate vs 12.9% with clomiphene alone, with a significant difference in ongoing pregnancies (p=0.04) (PMID: 20517830)
- Metformin augmented clomiphene-induced ovulation in non-obese PCOS women, with ovulation occurring in 71.11% of cycles in the metformin group vs 11.11% in the placebo group (p<0.001) (PMID: 19552097)
- In CC-resistant PCOS women undergoing IVF, metformin significantly improved embryo quality, implantation rate, and reduced abortion rate and OHSS rate compared to placebo, though pregnancy rate increase was not significant (PMID: 19757275)
- Metformin combined with CC achieved significantly higher rates of regular cycles (71.4% vs 38.1%), ovulation (76.2% vs 38.1%), and conception (66.6% vs 28.6%) compared to CC alone in PCOS patients (PMID: 23904883)
- Ovulation rate with metformin plus clomifene was 62.5% vs 37.5% with placebo plus clomifene in PCOS women, with higher mature follicle number and estradiol concentration in the metformin group (PMID: 19522426)
- In a large RCT of 626 infertile PCOS women, the live-birth rate was significantly lower with metformin alone (7.2%) than with clomiphene alone (22.5%) or combination therapy (26.8%), and the conception rate among ovulators was significantly lower in the metformin group (PMID: 17287476)
- Metformin did not improve IVF/ICSI outcomes (fertilization rate, oocyte number) in PCOS patients in a prospective randomized trial (PMID: 16169430)
- In a randomized double-blind crossover study of clomiphene-resistant PCOS women, metformin did not significantly improve spontaneous menstruation, ovulation induction, or pregnancy rates compared to placebo (PMID: 11994052)
- In a small RCT of obese PCOS women, high-dose metformin did not significantly improve urinary FSH, LH, pregnanediol glucuronide, glucose disposal, ovulation rates, or serum sex steroids compared to placebo (PMID: 19692630)
- In PCOS patients with reduced ovarian reserve undergoing IVF, metformin worsened ovarian response to gonadotropins, with significant differences in peak estradiol, dominant follicles, retrieved oocytes, and metaphase II oocytes compared to placebo; enrollment was stopped early due to unacceptable risk (PMID: 21917254)
- A secondary analysis of a crossover RCT found no significant overall decrease in AMH levels after 6 months of metformin in hyperandrogenemic PCOS women, though a small trend toward decrease was observed in obese women (PMID: 25736975)
- In a prospective study of 359 PCOS patients, metformin did not produce statistically significant differences in ovulation or pregnancy rates across any of the four PCOS phenotypes compared to non-metformin controls, though numerical improvements were seen in phenotypes B and C (PMID: 23605170)

### Metabolic Dysfunction-associated Steatotic Liver Disease

#### Literature — Metabolic Dysfunction-associated Steatotic Liver Disease

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 9

Multiple randomized controlled trials have evaluated metformin for treating NAFLD/MASLD, yielding mixed results. Some trials found no significant improvement in liver histology with metformin versus placebo (PMID: 19811343, PMID: 41828627), and rosiglitazone but not metformin reduced liver fat content in a head-to-head comparison (PMID: 15277403), while other trials reported improvements in liver steatosis by ultrasound, liver enzymes, and metabolic parameters with metformin (PMID: 20179669, PMID: 21411114, PMID: 18721166, PMID: 31460584). The overall evidence is mixed, with histology-based endpoints generally failing to show benefit while surrogate metabolic and imaging markers sometimes improve.

**Key findings:**
- In a 6-month randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of 48 patients with biopsy-proven NAFLD, metformin showed no significant difference versus placebo in liver steatosis assessed histologically or by CT, NAS score, or liver transaminases, though it improved body weight, cholesterol, LDL, glucose, and HbA1c (PMID: 19811343).
- In a 4-arm randomized placebo-controlled trial of 64 MASLD patients, metformin alone did not produce significant improvements in liver steatosis (CAP or kPa scores) compared to placebo at 6 months, while Ganwei alone did (PMID: 41828627).
- In a 6-month randomized study of 50 overweight/obese nondiabetic NAFLD patients, both metformin plus diet and diet alone reduced echographic fatty liver, but metformin was more effective in normalizing HOMA-IR, impaired fasting glucose, and metabolic syndrome criteria (PMID: 20179669).
- In a randomized placebo-controlled trial of 63 NAFLD patients, metformin significantly decreased pulse wave velocity, augmentation index, fasting glucose, triglycerides, and alkaline phosphatase, and marginally decreased ALT and increased adiponectin over 4 months (PMID: 21411114).
- In a 12-week randomized study of 16 overweight/obese women with NAFLD, metformin (1 g/day) modified liver fat content by 10.37% (CI95% -1.74, 22.48), though this did not reach statistical significance, while aerobic exercise achieved a significant 14.6% reduction (PMID: 24304731).
- In a randomized trial of 50 obese insulin-resistant adolescents with NAFLD, metformin plus lifestyle intervention significantly improved fatty liver prevalence (p<0.04), severity (p<0.04), and fasting insulin (p<0.025) compared to placebo plus lifestyle (PMID: 18721166).
- In a 48-week open-label multicenter randomized trial of biopsy-proven NASH patients, the NAC + metformin group showed significant intragroup improvements in steatosis degree (P=0.014), ballooning (P=0.027), NAS score (P=0.005), and ALT, though no significant differences were found between the three treatment groups (PMID: 31460584).
- In a 48-week randomized trial of 137 biopsy-proven NASH patients, combination of rosiglitazone plus metformin showed no significant difference versus rosiglitazone alone in histologic parameters (steatosis P=0.137, inflammation P=0.320, fibrosis P=0.229), and metformin did not significantly mitigate rosiglitazone-associated weight gain (PMID: 21748770).
- In a 16-week double-blind randomized trial of 20 drug-naive type 2 diabetes patients, rosiglitazone decreased liver fat by 51% (P=0.003) while metformin did not significantly reduce liver fat (13±3% to 14±3%, NS), and metformin did not increase serum adiponectin or peripheral glucose uptake (PMID: 15277403).

### Metabolic Syndrome

#### Literature — Metabolic Syndrome

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 10

Multiple randomized controlled trials demonstrate that metformin improves key components of metabolic syndrome in non-diabetic patients, including endothelial function, insulin resistance, diastolic function, and biochemical normalization of metabolic parameters (PMIDs: 16115299, 33830437, 17924869, 15838067). One trial in obese European adolescents found that metformin did not significantly outperform placebo for metabolic syndrome resolution, and one trial in Thai menopausal women found no statistically significant superiority over placebo for cardiovascular risk factors (PMIDs: 20639355, 32342619). Overall, the evidence supports the use of metformin for treating metabolic syndrome, though some individual trials show mixed or null results.

**Key findings:**
- Metformin (500 mg twice daily for 3 months) significantly improved endothelial function (flow-mediated dilatation from 7.4% to 12.4%) and reduced HOMA-IR by 26% compared to placebo in patients with metabolic syndrome (PMID: 16115299).
- In the MET-DIME randomized trial, metformin treatment of non-diabetic metabolic syndrome patients with diastolic dysfunction was associated with a significant improvement in mean e' velocity at 24 months (+0.67 cm/s in metformin arm vs. -0.33 cm/s in control arm) and a significant decrease in HOMA-IR (PMID: 33830437).
- In the Diabetes Prevention Program, metformin therapy reduced the incidence of metabolic syndrome by 17% (P=0.03) compared to placebo over a mean follow-up of 3.2 years in participants with impaired glucose tolerance (PMID: 15838067).
- High-dose combination of fenofibrate 80 mg + metformin 850 mg twice daily led to normalization of biochemical abnormalities in 17.4% of metabolic syndrome patients vs. 5.0% with metformin monotherapy and 5.8% with fenofibrate monotherapy; metformin monotherapy normalized fasting glucose in a subset of patients (PMID: 17924869).
- Metformin 2000 mg daily for 3 months in patients with abdominal obesity reduced body weight by 4.2 kg and waist circumference by 4.2 cm, improved lipid profile, and decreased HOMA index; effects were more pronounced in patients with metabolic syndrome (PMID: 25306749).
- In adult subjects with metabolic syndrome and T2DM, metformin (1500–2000 mg/day) improved glycemic control, HOMA-IR, and lipid profile comparably to Policaptil Gel Retard over 6 months (PMID: 33906073).
- In obese European adolescents, HOMA-IR and fasting insulin improved similarly in both metformin and placebo groups during the medication period, and metabolic syndrome remained unchanged in both groups, suggesting no significant advantage of metformin over placebo in this population (PMID: 20639355).
- In middle-aged Thai women with metabolic syndrome, metformin (1700 mg/day for 6 months) improved BMI, fasting blood glucose, hsCRP, and 10-year coronary heart disease risk within the metformin group, but there was no statistically significant difference in improvement between metformin and placebo groups for cardiovascular risk factors (PMID: 32342619).
- In a pilot RCT of the Mediterranean diet and metformin in women with components of metabolic syndrome, long-term metformin use was associated with a significant reduction in serum total B12 and holo-TC-II levels, with 26 of 81 metformin-treated subjects falling below the normal B12 threshold vs. 10 of 84 placebo-treated subjects (PMID: 31067706).

### Obesity

#### Literature — Obesity

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 11

Multiple randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials demonstrate that metformin reduces body weight, BMI, body fat, and waist circumference in obese non-diabetic children, adolescents, and adults compared to placebo (PMIDs: 11735093, 16595599, 21228310, 28759403, 30070925, 31821361). Mechanistic studies suggest metformin reduces food intake, increases satiety, and raises GLP-1 levels in obese non-diabetic subjects (PMIDs: 9693934, 11289473, 25483291). One study found that both metformin and placebo improved insulin sensitivity when added to lifestyle intervention, with most between-group differences not reaching statistical significance, representing a mixed result (PMID: 20639355).

**Key findings:**
- In a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial of 24 hyperinsulinemic nondiabetic obese adolescents, metformin (850 mg twice daily) produced greater weight loss (6.5% vs 3.8%, P<0.01), greater decrease in body fat (P<0.001), and greater reductions in plasma leptin, cholesterol, triglycerides, and free fatty acids compared to placebo (PMID: 11735093)
- In a randomized double-blind crossover trial of 28 obese pediatric patients aged 9-18 years, metformin (1 g twice daily) had a greater treatment effect over placebo for weight (-4.35 kg, P=0.02), BMI (-1.26 kg/m², P=0.002), waist circumference (-2.8 cm, P=0.003), subcutaneous abdominal adipose tissue (-52.5 cm², P=0.002), and fasting insulin (-2.2 mU/liter, P=0.011) (PMID: 16595599)
- In a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial of 100 severely obese insulin-resistant children aged 6-12 years, metformin produced significantly greater decreases in BMI (difference -1.09 kg/m², P=0.006), body weight (difference -3.38 kg, P<0.001), BMI Z score (difference -0.07, P=0.02), and fat mass (difference -1.40 kg, P=0.04) compared to placebo (PMID: 21228310)
- A randomized double-blind placebo-controlled multicenter trial of 160 nondiabetic obese children aged 7-14 years found metformin (1 g/day) decreased BMI z score versus placebo in the prepubertal group (-0.8 vs -0.6, difference 0.2, P=0.04) and improved insulin sensitivity and adiponectin-leptin ratio in prepubertal children (PMID: 28759403)
- A randomized controlled trial of 150 obese school children found metformin produced statistically significant reductions in weight, BMI/Age-SDS, fat mass, systolic blood pressure, total cholesterol, LDL, and hsCRP at 6 months compared to placebo (PMID: 30070925)
- A pilot double-blind placebo-controlled trial of 18 pre-pubertal and early pubertal children with obesity over 24 months found metformin (850 mg/day) tended to reduce BMI-SDS, leptin, leptin-to-HMW adiponectin ratio, hsCRP, carotid intima-media thickness, fat mass, and liver fat compared to placebo (PMID: 31821361)
- In a double-blind randomized study of 30 obese non-diabetic patients, metformin administration for 15 days produced significantly greater reductions in body weight (-2.8 vs -0.3 kg, P<0.01), body fat (-1.4 vs -0.3 kg, P<0.01), plasma leptin, and food intake (-642 vs -70 kJ/24h, P<0.01) compared to placebo (PMID: 9693934)
- In a 6-month randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial of 100 obese hyperinsulinemic children aged 6-12 years, metformin (1000 mg twice daily) significantly reduced energy intake after a pre-meal load (metformin: -104.7 kcal vs placebo: +144.2 kcal, P=0.034), decreased hunger ratings (P=0.013), and increased fullness ratings (P=0.01) compared to placebo (PMID: 25483291)
- In 10 obese nondiabetic male patients, 14-day metformin treatment (2550 mg/day) significantly increased GLP-1(7-36)amide/(7-37) levels at 30 and 60 minutes after oral glucose load compared to untreated controls (P<0.05), with in vitro evidence that metformin inhibits GLP-1 degradation via DPP-IV inhibition (PMID: 11289473)
- In 46 patients with abdominal obesity, metformin 2000 mg daily for 3 months reduced body weight by 4.2 kg and waist circumference by 4.2 cm compared to 1.7 kg and 1.1 cm in the comparison group, with more significant lipid profile improvement and a decrease in HOMA index only in the metformin group (PMID: 25306749)
- In a randomized study of 70 obese European adolescents who had failed lifestyle intervention, both metformin (2×500 mg/day) and placebo groups showed similar improvements in HOMA-IR and fasting insulin during the 6-month medication period, with BMI remaining unchanged in both groups; the insulin sensitivity index improved only in the metformin group but most between-group differences did not reach statistical significance (PMID: 20639355)

### Insulin Resistance

#### Literature — Insulin Resistance

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 9

Multiple randomized controlled trials demonstrate that metformin improves insulin sensitivity and reduces insulin resistance across diverse populations including prediabetic adults, obese adolescents, and HIV-infected patients with lipodystrophy. Metformin stabilized or improved insulin sensitivity in prediabetic persons (PMID: 32601731), significantly reduced HOMA-IR in obese hyperinsulinemic adolescents (PMID: 18556965), improved fasting insulin and body composition in obese insulin-resistant pediatric patients (PMID: 16595599), and reduced insulin AUC and insulin resistance in HIV-infected patients with lipodystrophy (PMID: 10904511). Some trials showed mixed results, with improvements in fasting insulin but less consistent effects on formal insulin sensitivity indices (PMID: 20639355, PMID: 31821361), and one crossover study found improved basal glucose disposal and HOMA-based insulin resistance but no change in insulin-stimulated glucose disposal under hyperinsulinemic conditions (PMID: 10477209), indicating the evidence overall supports but is not uniformly positive.

**Key findings:**
- Metformin stabilized insulin sensitivity in prediabetic sub-cohorts and was more effective in persons with more pronounced impaired fasting glucose, reducing diabetes incidence by 43% in that subgroup (PMID: 32601731)
- In obese hyperinsulinemic adolescents, metformin significantly reduced HOMA-IR from 4.95 to 2.6 (p<0.001) and fasting insulin from 19.2 to 11.1 µU/ml (p<0.001) compared to placebo (PMID: 18556965)
- Metformin had a greater treatment effect over placebo for fasting insulin (-2.2 mU/liter, P=0.011) and improved insulin sensitivity index (Si) in 45% of subjects vs 27% on placebo in obese insulin-resistant pediatric patients (PMID: 16595599)
- In obese patients with impaired glucose tolerance, metformin improved insulin resistance as quantified by HOMA by 1.1 (95% CI 0.2-2.0, P=0.004) without change in insulin secretion (PMID: 10477209)
- HIV-infected patients with lipodystrophy treated with metformin demonstrated significant reductions in insulin AUC 120 minutes after OGTT (-2930 vs -414 µIU/mL, P=0.01) compared to placebo (PMID: 10904511)
- In normoglycemic morbidly obese adolescents, metformin was associated with enhanced insulin sensitivity as determined by fasting plasma glucose:insulin, 2-hour glucose:insulin, and AUC glucose:AUC insulin ratios compared to controls (P<0.01) (PMID: 11735093)
- In patients with abdominal obesity, a decrease in the HOMA index was observed only in the metformin group, with effects more pronounced in those with metabolic syndrome and insulin resistance (PMID: 25306749)
- In obese European adolescents, HOMA-IR and fasting insulin improved in both metformin and placebo groups during the medication period, but the insulin sensitivity index improved only in the metformin group; most differences did not reach statistical significance (PMID: 20639355)
- In pre-puberal and early pubertal children with obesity, 24-month metformin treatment showed no significant changes in insulin sensitivity (HOMA-IR) compared to placebo, though improvements in body composition and inflammation markers were observed (PMID: 31821361)

### Heart Failure

#### Literature — Heart Failure

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 13

Multiple randomized controlled trials and systematic reviews indicate that metformin has beneficial effects in heart failure patients, including improved myocardial efficiency, reduced oxidative stress, improved insulin resistance, and reduced mortality compared to controls (PMIDs: 31863557, 37816240, 22740509, 23508758, 31362743). A systematic review of observational studies found metformin associated with reduced mortality and hospitalizations in diabetic patients with heart failure, and no increased lactic acidosis risk (PMID: 23508758). Some trials showed mixed results, with primary endpoints not always met (e.g., peak VO2 unchanged) but secondary endpoints improved (PMID: 22740509, 32770520), and one trial in T2DM hypertensive patients without overt HF found no significant change in LV mass or diastolic function (PMID: 31721026), reflecting heterogeneity across populations and endpoints.

**Key findings:**
- In a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial of 36 non-diabetic HFrEF patients, metformin increased myocardial efficiency (work metabolic index) by ~20% relative to placebo and reduced myocardial oxygen consumption (PMID: 31863557).
- A randomized controlled trial of 70 nondiabetic HFrEF patients found metformin increased total antioxidant capacity and preserved left ventricular mass index compared to standard of care over 6 months (PMID: 37816240).
- In a double-blind placebo-controlled trial of 62 non-diabetic insulin-resistant CHF patients, metformin significantly reduced insulin resistance (FIRI) and improved the VE/VCO2 slope, though peak VO2 (primary endpoint) did not differ between groups (PMID: 22740509).
- A systematic review of 9 cohort studies (~34,000 patients) found metformin associated with reduced all-cause mortality (23% vs 37%; pooled adjusted risk estimate 0.80) and reduced hospitalizations in diabetic patients with heart failure, with no increased lactic acidosis risk (PMID: 23508758).
- A meta-analysis of 40 studies (1,066,408 patients) found metformin reduced all-cause mortality in heart failure patients (aHR=0.84) and reduced CV events in HF patients (aHR=0.83) (PMID: 31362743).
- A systematic review of 9 RCTs (2,486 patients) found metformin improved myocardial oxygen consumption and reduced NT-proBNP and LDL levels, though it did not consistently affect left ventricular function (PMID: 32157481).
- In an 81-patient randomized study of CHF patients with type 2 diabetes, metformin use was associated with improved NYHA functional class, central hemodynamics, quality of life, and reduced CHF decompensations, with no cases of lactic acidosis over 12 months (PMID: 18429758).
- A pilot RCT found that despite a formal contraindication, metformin was the most commonly prescribed oral antihyperglycemic agent in screened HF patients (51%), suggesting widespread real-world use and clinical acceptance (PMID: 19203392).
- The MET-DIME trial was designed to evaluate whether metformin improves diastolic dysfunction in non-diabetic metabolic syndrome patients with left ventricular diastolic dysfunction, with primary endpoint of early diastolic mitral annular velocity change (PMID: 24515256).
- The GIPS-III trial was designed to evaluate metformin's efficacy in preserving left ventricular ejection fraction in non-diabetic STEMI patients, with animal data suggesting metformin reduces myocardial injury and preserves cardiac function (PMID: 22968678).
- In a post hoc analysis of SAVOR-TIMI 53 (12,156 patients), metformin use was associated with lower all-cause mortality (HR 0.75) but no significant reduction in the composite cardiovascular endpoint; the association was less apparent in patients with prior heart failure (PMID: 31362530).
- In 18 non-diabetic HFrEF patients randomized to metformin or placebo, metformin reduced body weight and HbA1c but did not improve whole-body insulin sensitivity, endogenous glucose production, or skeletal muscle mitochondrial respiratory capacity (PMID: 32770520).
- In a randomized controlled trial of 164 T2DM hypertensive patients without overt HF, 1 year of metformin did not significantly change LV mass index, BNP, or E/e' compared to controls, though it reduced BMI, LDL-C, and preserved renal function (PMID: 31721026).

### Breast Cancer

#### Literature — Breast Cancer

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 12

Multiple randomized controlled trials have evaluated metformin for treating breast cancer in non-diabetic patients, yielding predominantly negative results for clinical endpoints alongside some positive biological and biomarker signals. The large phase III MA.32 trial found that adjuvant metformin did not improve invasive disease-free survival or overall survival in patients with hormone receptor-positive or hormone receptor-negative breast cancer (PMID: 35608580), and phase II trials in metastatic breast cancer similarly showed no significant improvement in progression-free or overall survival (PMID: 31472446, PMID: 30536182). However, preoperative window-of-opportunity and biomarker studies demonstrated biological activity including upregulation of pAMPK, downregulation of pAkt, suppression of Ki67 in HER2-positive DCIS, and reductions in circulating sex hormones (PMID: 25682077, PMID: 26276754, PMID: 24267731), and some trials showed non-significant trends toward improved tumor response or IGF-1 reduction (PMID: 35538143, PMID: 40579605, PMID: 40374694).

**Key findings:**
- In the phase III MA.32 trial (n=3649), adjuvant metformin vs placebo did not significantly improve invasive disease-free survival (HR 1.01; 95% CI 0.84-1.21; P=0.93) or overall survival (HR 1.10; 95% CI 0.86-1.41; P=0.47) in ER/PgR-positive non-diabetic breast cancer patients (PMID: 35608580).
- In the MA.32 secondary analysis, metformin did not reduce the risk of new primary invasive cancers compared to placebo (HR 1.25; 95% CI 0.94-1.68; P=0.13) in non-diabetic breast cancer patients (PMID: 37695982).
- A phase II RCT of metformin plus chemotherapy vs placebo plus chemotherapy in metastatic breast cancer showed no significant difference in progression-free survival (mean PFS 5.4 vs 6.3 months, HR 1.2; 95% CI 0.63-2.31) or overall survival (PMID: 31472446).
- The MYME phase II trial in HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer found no significant difference in median PFS between metformin plus chemotherapy (9.4 months) vs chemotherapy alone (9.9 months; P=0.651) (PMID: 30536182).
- In a preoperative window-of-opportunity RCT, metformin significantly upregulated pAMPK (p=0.04) and downregulated pAkt (p=0.043) in primary breast cancer tissue, and suppressed insulin receptor and serum insulin levels compared to controls (PMID: 25682077).
- In a presurgical RCT, metformin significantly reduced Ki67 labeling index in HER2-positive/ER-positive DCIS lesions adjacent to invasive breast cancer (12% vs 32% in placebo; P=0.004) (PMID: 26276754).
- In a randomized study of non-diabetic breast cancer patients in natural menopause, metformin 1500 mg/day produced a significant reduction in free testosterone (-29%) and estradiol (-38%) compared to 1000 mg/day (PMID: 24267731).
- In a randomized neoadjuvant chemotherapy trial (n=80), adding metformin to anthracycline/taxane-based NACT increased overall response rate (80.6% vs 68.4%), clinical complete response (27.8% vs 10.5%), and pathological complete response (22.2% vs 10.5%) compared to NACT alone, though differences were non-significant (PMID: 35538143).
- In a randomized trial of 50 metastatic breast cancer patients, metformin plus chemotherapy significantly reduced IGF-1 levels compared to chemotherapy alone (p=0.011), and the percentage post-treatment drop in IGF-1 differed significantly between groups (p=0.001) (PMID: 40579605).
- In the MetBreCS phase II RCT, metformin treatment in postmenopausal breast cancer survivors with BMI>25 was associated with differential expression of genes including MS4A1, HBA2, MT-RNR1, MT-RNR2, EGFL6, and FDCSP in breast tissue, with downregulation of immune response genes and enrichment of steroid hormone biosynthesis pathways (PMID: 40374694).
- In a presurgical RCT of 100 non-diabetic breast cancer patients, metformin did not significantly modulate apoptosis (TUNEL) overall vs placebo, though a trend toward differential effect by insulin resistance status was observed (PMID: 24157825).
- In a phase II RCT of non-diabetic breast cancer patients with obesity and/or pre-diabetes, metformin 1000 mg significantly reduced glucose and HbA1c levels compared to placebo (p=0.008 and 0.009, respectively), with effects increasing with higher BMI (PMID: 26293146).

### Coronary Artery Disorder

#### Literature — Coronary Artery Disorder

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 9

Multiple clinical studies, including several RCTs, have examined metformin for coronary artery disease (CAD) across various endpoints. Several trials support benefit: metformin reduced major cardiovascular events compared to glipizide in type 2 diabetic CAD patients (PMID: 23230096), regressed left ventricular hypertrophy in non-diabetic CAD patients with insulin resistance (PMID: 30993313), improved coronary endothelial function and reduced MACE in prediabetic patients with stable angina and nonobstructive coronary stenosis (PMID: 30796109), reduced LDL-C and PCSK9 levels in CAD patients without diabetes (PMID: 38922587), and improved lipid profiles in non-diabetic CAD patients (PMID: 8772621, 9597374). However, one RCT found that short-term metformin pretreatment did not reduce periprocedural myocardial injury (troponin levels) in non-diabetic patients undergoing coronary artery bypass graft surgery (PMID: 26179504), indicating the evidence is mixed depending on the specific endpoint and clinical context.

**Key findings:**
- In a multicenter RCT of 304 type 2 diabetic patients with CAD, metformin reduced the composite of major cardiovascular events (HR 0.54, 95% CI 0.30–0.90, P=0.026) compared to glipizide over a median 5-year follow-up (PMID: 23230096).
- In the MET-REMODEL RCT (n=68 non-diabetic CAD patients with insulin resistance/pre-diabetes), metformin XL 2000 mg/day for 12 months significantly reduced left ventricular mass index (mean difference -1.37, P=0.033), LVM, office systolic BP, body weight, subcutaneous adipose tissue, and oxidative stress compared to placebo (PMID: 30993313).
- In the CODYCE multicenter prospective study, metformin therapy in prediabetic patients with stable angina and nonobstructive coronary stenosis significantly reduced coronary endothelial dysfunction and lowered MACE at 24 months compared to untreated prediabetic patients (PMID: 30796109).
- In a randomized open-label trial of CAD patients without diabetes, one month of metformin combined with cholesterol-lowering agents significantly reduced LDL-C by 20.81% (P<0.001) and PCSK9 levels by 15.03% (P<0.001), and reduced small-dense LDL particle count more than cholesterol-lowering agents alone (PMID: 38922587).
- In a 12-week randomized study of 60 non-diabetic male CAD patients, metformin added to lovastatin lowered the LDL/HDL-cholesterol ratio by 12% at week 4 and reduced body weight by 1.8 kg at week 12; in normal-weight patients, metformin reduced total cholesterol by 9% and LDL-cholesterol by 12% (PMID: 8772621).
- In a 12-week prospective study of 60 non-diabetic male CAD patients, metformin reduced insulin resistance (insulin area/glucose area ratio) by 24% overall and 30% in obese subjects (P=0.049), with weight reduction not correlating with insulin resistance improvement (PMID: 9597374).
- In a clinical trial of non-diabetic patients undergoing coronary artery bypass surgery, metformin 500 mg twice daily was similarly effective to insulin infusion in controlling postoperative hyperglycemia, with no significant difference in blood glucose levels between groups (PMID: 28994678).
- In the MetCAB double-blind RCT (n=100 non-diabetic patients undergoing elective on-pump CABG), short-term metformin pretreatment (500 mg three times daily for 3 days) did not significantly reduce high-sensitivity troponin I concentrations at 6, 12, or 24 hours post-reperfusion compared to placebo (difference 12.3%, 95% CI -12.4 to 44.1, P=0.35), and gastrointestinal discomfort was significantly higher with metformin (PMID: 26179504).
- The MET-REMODEL study design (rationale paper) described a planned RCT to investigate metformin's ability to regress LVH in 64 CAD patients with insulin resistance, with primary endpoint of left ventricular mass index change (PMID: 25545400).

### Hyperandrogenism

#### Literature — Hyperandrogenism

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 7

Multiple randomized controlled trials demonstrate that metformin reduces androgen levels in women and adolescents with hyperandrogenism. Metformin significantly lowered serum testosterone and related androgen markers in adolescents with PCOS (PMID: 16520442), in adolescents with type 1 diabetes and hyperandrogenism (PMID: 24280743), and in women with PCOS through a direct effect on LH-stimulated testosterone secretion independent of insulin sensitization (PMID: 25304843). One study found no additional benefit of metformin over diet alone in obese hirsute women (PMID: 7608255), and a comparison with rimonabant showed metformin did not significantly reduce testosterone or insulin resistance over 12 weeks in obese PCOS patients (PMID: 18410553), introducing some mixed findings. Overall, the evidence supports the use of metformin for reducing biochemical hyperandrogenism, particularly in PCOS and related conditions.

**Key findings:**
- Metformin (500 mg tid for 6 months) significantly reduced serum free testosterone (P<0.05) and attenuated ovarian hyperandrogenism in women with PCOS, independent of changes in body weight (PMID: 10634377).
- In a 12-week randomized placebo-controlled trial in adolescents with PCOS, metformin produced a significant decline in mean serum testosterone (-38.3 ng/dL vs -0.86 ng/dL with placebo) (PMID: 16520442).
- In a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial of adolescents with type 1 diabetes and hyperandrogenism, metformin (850 mg bid for 9 months) significantly decreased testosterone, free androgen index, androstenedione, and 17-OH progesterone compared to placebo, though clinical parameters such as hirsutism and ovulation were not significantly affected (PMID: 24280743).
- Metformin induced a prompt decrease in LH-stimulated testosterone secretion after only 2 days of use in women with PCOS, independent of changes in weight, metabolic parameters, or insulin sensitivity (PMID: 25304843).
- In obese PCOS women on a hypocaloric diet, metformin (850 mg twice daily for 6 months) combined with flutamide showed additive effects in reducing testosterone concentrations and synergistic effects in increasing HDL cholesterol and SHBG, and metformin alone had significant benefits on menstrual status (PMID: 14725687).
- In a randomized study of obese hirsute women on a low-calorie diet, metformin administration had no additional benefit over diet alone on androgen levels or insulin, suggesting diet-induced weight loss was the primary driver of improvement in that population (PMID: 7608255).
- In a randomized open-label comparison in obese PCOS patients, metformin (1.5 g/day for 12 weeks) did not significantly reduce free androgen index, testosterone, or insulin resistance, whereas rimonabant did (PMID: 18410553).

### Miscarriage

#### Literature — Miscarriage

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 7

Multiple clinical studies, including randomized controlled trials and prospective cohort studies, indicate that metformin reduces miscarriage rates in women with polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). A pooled individual participant data analysis from three RCTs found that metformin significantly reduced late miscarriage and preterm birth compared to placebo in PCOS pregnancies (PMID: 30792154), and prospective cohort and pilot studies in PCOS patients showed markedly lower early pregnancy loss rates with metformin continuation through the first trimester (PMID: 17162710, PMID: 11163815, PMID: 20441061). One RCT in obese non-PCOS pregnant women without diabetes found no significant difference in the combined adverse outcome including miscarriage between metformin and placebo groups, introducing some uncertainty about generalizability beyond PCOS (PMID: 26165398).

**Key findings:**
- In a pooled individual participant data analysis of three RCTs, late miscarriage or preterm delivery occurred in 5% of women in the metformin group vs. 10% in the placebo group (OR 0.43, 95% CI 0.23–0.79; p=0.004) in pregnant women with PCOS (PMID: 30792154).
- In the primary RCT (PregMet2), the composite outcome of late miscarriage and preterm birth occurred in 5% of metformin-treated vs. 10% of placebo-treated PCOS women (OR 0.50, 95% CI 0.22–1.08; p=0.08), a non-significant trend (PMID: 30792154).
- In a prospective cohort of 200 non-diabetic PCOS patients undergoing assisted reproduction, early pregnancy loss was 11.6% in the metformin group vs. 36.3% in the control group (p<0.0001; OR=0.23, 95% CI 0.11–0.42) (PMID: 17162710).
- In a pilot study of 19 PCOS women receiving metformin throughout pregnancy, first-trimester spontaneous abortion occurred in 10% vs. 73% in 22 previous pregnancies without metformin (P<0.002) (PMID: 11163815).
- In a study of 75 pregnant PCOS women, continuing metformin until 8 weeks reduced miscarriage from 40% to 8%, and until 12 weeks reduced it from 32% to 4%, with statistically significant differences in groups B and C (PMID: 20441061).
- Metformin increased luteal phase serum glycodelin (3-fold) and IGFBP-1 concentrations and enhanced uterine vascularity and blood flow in PCOS women, suggesting an improved endometrial environment for pregnancy maintenance (PMID: 11238496).
- In a multicenter RCT of PCOS women with anovulatory infertility, miscarriage rates were low and similar between metformin (15.2%) and placebo (17.9%) groups (P=0.8), though metformin significantly improved live-birth rates overall (PMID: 22419702).
- In a RCT of obese pregnant women without diabetes or PCOS, the combined adverse outcome of miscarriage, termination, stillbirth, or neonatal death was numerically higher in the metformin group (n=7) vs. placebo (n=2), though not statistically significant (OR 3.60, 95% CI 0.74–17.50; p=0.11) (PMID: 26165398).

### Sepsis

#### Literature — Sepsis

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 10

Multiple systematic reviews and meta-analyses of observational cohort studies consistently show that preadmission or in-hospital metformin use is associated with significantly reduced mortality in patients with sepsis and diabetes mellitus (PMID: 42087873, 35002982, 30777119, 32166255, 35120041, 40394230). A narrative review highlights metformin's anti-oxidant and anti-inflammatory mechanisms via AMPK activation as potentially beneficial in sepsis-induced organ failure, with no evidence of lactic acidosis at therapeutic doses (PMID: 31376317). One large nationwide cohort study found no significant association between prior metformin therapy and 30-day mortality or risk of developing sepsis (PMID: 32146543), and a Phase II RCT in burn patients (where sepsis was a secondary concern) showed mixed results with reduced inflammation but was not designed to assess sepsis mortality as a primary endpoint (PMID: 27355267), introducing some uncertainty into the overall picture.

**Key findings:**
- Meta-analysis of 14 studies (12,687 patients) found preadmission metformin use in sepsis-diabetes patients was associated with reduced overall mortality (OR 0.58, 95% CI 0.44-0.75, P < 0.00001), with significant reductions in 28-day, 90-day, 365-day, and in-hospital mortality (PMID: 42087873)
- Meta-analysis of 11 studies (8,195 patients) found preadmission metformin use was associated with lower mortality rate (OR 0.74, 95% CI 0.62-0.88, P < 0.01) in patients with sepsis and diabetes, with no significant increase in serum creatinine or lactic acid (PMID: 35002982)
- Meta-analysis of 5 observational cohort studies (1,282 patients) found metformin use was associated with significantly lower mortality in septic adult patients with diabetes mellitus (OR 0.59, 95% CI 0.43-0.79, P = 0.001) (PMID: 30777119)
- Meta-analysis of 8 observational studies (4,144 patients) found premorbid metformin exposure was associated with reduced mortality in sepsis (OR 0.57, 95% CI 0.40-0.80), and was not significantly associated with initial lactate levels (PMID: 32166255)
- Retrospective cohort study of 14,847 ICU patients with type 2 diabetes and sepsis found metformin exposure during hospitalization was associated with decreased 90-day mortality (11.9% vs 22.7%; OR 0.46, 95% CI 0.35-0.60), reduced severe acute kidney injury, and increased renal recovery (PMID: 35120041)
- Retrospective observational study of 320 septic shock patients with type 2 diabetes found metformin administration within 48 h was independently associated with decreased 90-day mortality (aHR 0.371, 95% CI 0.153-0.900, P = 0.028) and 365-day mortality (aHR 0.453, 95% CI 0.219-0.937, P = 0.033) (PMID: 40394230)
- A review concluded that metformin improves sepsis-induced organ failure by inhibiting reactive oxygen species and pro-inflammatory cytokines, preventing activation of inflammation-related transcription factors, decreasing neutrophil accumulation, and maintaining mitochondrial membrane potential, with no evidence of lactic acidosis at therapeutic doses (PMID: 31376317)
- A review of metformin's antimicrobial properties noted that in mice with models of endotoxemia, metformin diminished levels of proinflammatory cytokines and improved survival, and highlighted emerging literature on its role in sepsis (PMID: 29271563)
- A nationwide cohort study of 77,337 diabetic patients in South Korea found that prior metformin therapy was not significantly associated with the risk of sepsis (OR 0.92, 95% CI 0.82-1.03, P = 0.143) or 30-day mortality after sepsis diagnosis (OR 0.94, 95% CI 0.75-1.17, P = 0.571) after propensity score adjustment (PMID: 32146543)
- A Phase II RCT in severely burned patients found metformin controlled blood glucose as effectively as insulin, was associated with significantly reduced inflammation (P < 0.05), and had only 1 mild hypoglycemic episode (6%) versus 15% in the insulin group, though sepsis was a secondary concern rather than the primary endpoint (PMID: 27355267)

### Cardiovascular Disorder

#### Literature — Cardiovascular Disorder

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 6

Multiple clinical studies, including RCTs and meta-analyses, indicate that metformin supports treatment or prevention of cardiovascular outcomes in non-diabetic or pre-existing CVD populations. A meta-analysis of 35 RCTs found a significant benefit of metformin versus placebo/no therapy on cardiovascular events (PMID: 21205121), and a pooling synthesis found metformin associated with reduced all-cause mortality, cardiovascular mortality, coronary revascularisation, and heart failure in patients with pre-existing CVD (PMID: 35032305). However, one RCT in menopausal women with metabolic syndrome found metformin not superior to placebo for cardiovascular risk factor improvement (PMID: 32342619), and a small RCT in nondiabetic heart failure patients showed metformin improved antioxidant capacity and preserved left ventricular mass index but did not significantly change ejection fraction or blood pressure (PMID: 37816240).

**Key findings:**
- Meta-analysis of 35 RCTs found metformin significantly reduced cardiovascular events versus placebo/no therapy (MH-OR 0.79 [0.64–0.98], p=0.031), though not versus active comparators; combination with sulphonylureas was associated with reduced survival (PMID: 21205121)
- Pooling synthesis of 33 studies (61,704 patients) found metformin associated with reduced all-cause mortality (HR 0.90), cardiovascular mortality (HR 0.89), coronary revascularisation (HR 0.79), and heart failure (HR 0.90) in patients with pre-existing CVD (PMID: 35032305)
- RCT in 70 nondiabetic HFrEF patients found metformin increased total antioxidant capacity (Δ=0.12 mmol/L, p=0.007) and preserved left ventricular mass index (Δ=−23 g/m², p=0.014) compared to standard of care (PMID: 37816240)
- In IGT adults, metformin alone lowered systolic and diastolic blood pressure by 6% and reduced hs-CRP (trend p=0.06) compared to placebo, and reduced body weight and metabolic syndrome Z-score; however, combining metformin with exercise attenuated some CVD risk factor benefits seen with exercise alone (PMID: 23505172)
- A double-blind RCT in menopausal women with metabolic syndrome found metformin 1700 mg/day improved BMI, fasting blood glucose, hs-CRP, and 10-year coronary heart disease risk within the metformin group, but was not statistically superior to placebo for cardiovascular risk factor improvement (PMID: 32342619)
- A protocol for a 12-month double-blind RCT in children with type 1 diabetes was designed to assess metformin's effect on vascular health outcomes including flow-mediated dilatation and carotid intima media thickness, based on the premise that metformin improves vascular health in insulin-resistant populations (PMID: 23865839)

### Endothelial Dysfunction

#### Literature — Endothelial Dysfunction

**Evidence strength:** strong, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 13

Multiple randomized controlled trials and controlled studies demonstrate that metformin improves endothelial function across diverse patient populations including those with metabolic syndrome, type 2 diabetes, polycystic ovary syndrome, hypertension, and first-degree relatives of diabetic patients (PMIDs: 16115299, 23981104, 11300445, 23140261, 16644641, 31806099, 18426821, 17263761, 30509271). The evidence supports repurposing metformin for endothelial dysfunction, with improvements observed in flow-mediated dilation, endothelium-dependent vasodilation, and biomarkers such as vWF, sVCAM-1, PAI-1, t-PA, ICAM-1, and inflammatory cytokines. Some studies report mixed or limited findings on specific endpoints such as carotid IMT progression or certain adhesion molecules, and one study found no significant overall change in endothelial function in PCOS patients unless stratified by baseline dysfunction (PMIDs: 24622715, 22892913, 24710641, 31806099).

**Key findings:**
- Metformin 500 mg twice daily for 3 months significantly improved flow-mediated dilation of the brachial artery in patients with metabolic syndrome (from 7.4% to 12.4% vs. 7.3% to 6.9% in placebo), with improvement correlated with reduced insulin resistance (PMID: 16115299).
- In a 4.3-year RCT of type 2 diabetes patients on insulin, metformin significantly reduced vWF, sVCAM-1, t-PA, PAI-1, CRP, and sICAM-1, with improvements in vWF and sVCAM-1 explaining approximately 34% of the reduction in CV morbidity and mortality risk (PMID: 23981104).
- Metformin 500 mg twice daily for 12 weeks significantly improved acetylcholine-stimulated forearm blood flow (endothelium-dependent) in type 2 diabetes patients compared to placebo, with insulin resistance as the sole predictor of endothelium-dependent blood flow after treatment (PMID: 11300445).
- A single dose of metformin 500 mg prevented the decline in brachial artery flow-mediated vasodilatation caused by acute glucose load in patients with primary hypertension, and inhibited glucose load-induced declines in SOD and T-AOC antioxidant levels (PMID: 23140261).

### Disorder Of Lipid Metabolism

#### Literature — Disorder Of Lipid Metabolism

**Evidence strength:** moderate, mixed
**Relevant studies:** 3

Three relevant studies examined metformin's effects on lipid metabolism disorders, specifically intrahepatic lipid content and NAFLD-related parameters. Metformin significantly reduced intrahepatic lipid content in a 24-week comparative study (PMID: 36072931) and significantly decreased fetuin A levels (a marker correlated with hepatic lipid and glucose metabolism enzymes) in a randomized controlled trial of NAFLD patients (PMID: 22170794). However, a metabolic investigation using stable-isotope tracers found that 12 weeks of metformin did not change intrahepatic triglyceride content despite weight loss and improved insulin sensitivity, with increased hepatic de novo lipogenesis offsetting potential benefits (PMID: 35038311), yielding a mixed overall picture.

**Key findings:**
- In a 24-week open-label comparative study of T2DM patients with NAFLD, the metformin group showed a statistically significant reduction in intrahepatic lipid content before and after treatment (P<0.05), while the control group did not (PMID: 36072931).
- In a randomized controlled trial of NAFLD patients, metformin treatment significantly decreased plasma fetuin A levels compared to placebo (-40 ± 47 vs 15 ± 82 mg/L, P=0.008), and hepatic fetuin A mRNA correlated with key enzymes in lipid metabolism including sterol regulatory element-binding protein 1c and carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1 (PMID: 22170794).
- Metformin also induced a dose-dependent decrease in fetuin A secretion in vitro in HepG2 cells (PMID: 22170794).
- In a metabolic investigation of insulin-resistant, overweight/obese NAFLD patients, 12 weeks of metformin did not change intrahepatic triglyceride content or fatty acid oxidation, despite significant reductions in body weight and improved insulin sensitivity (PMID: 35038311).
- Metformin treatment was associated with a significant decrease in VLDL-triglyceride concentrations but a significant increase in the relative contribution of de novo lipogenesis-derived fatty acids to VLDL-TG, suggesting compensatory hepatic lipogenesis (PMID: 35038311).

Glumetza (effective 20260902):
WARNING: LACTIC ACIDOSIS Postmarketing cases of metformin-associated lactic acidosis have resulted in death, hypothermia, hypotension, and resistant bradyarrhythmias. The onset of metformin-associated lactic acidosis is often subtle, accompanied only by nonspecific symptoms such as malaise, myalgias, respiratory distress, somnolence, and abdominal pain. Metformin-associated lactic acidosis was characterized by elevated blood lactate levels (>5 mmol/Liter), anion gap acidosis (without evidence of ketonuria or ketonemia), an increased lactate/pyruvate ratio, and metformin plasma levels generally >5 mcg/mL [see Warnings and Precautions ( 5.1 )] . Risk factors for metformin-associated lactic acidosis include renal impairment, concomitant use of certain drugs (e.g., carbonic anhydrase inhibitors such as topiramate), age 65 years old or greater, having a radiological study with contrast, surgery and other procedures, hypoxic states (e.g., acute congestive heart failure), excessive alcohol intake, hepatic impairment, and mitochondrial diseases [see Warnings and Precautions ( 5.1 )] . If metformin-associated lactic acidosis is suspected, immediately discontinue GLUMETZA and institute general supportive measures in a hospital setting. Prompt hemodialysis is recommended [see Warnings and Precautions ( 5.1 )].
